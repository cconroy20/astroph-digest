Constraints on the r-process Delay Time from Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies

Lauren E. Henderson, Evan N. Kirby, Mithi A. C. de los Reyes, Rebecca Surman

accepted by The Astrophysical Journal on 10/10/26

PDFWed 14 Oct
The ancient stellar populations and simple structure of the local dwarf spheroidal galaxies (dSphs) make them excellent laboratories for studying the chemical evolution of the early universe. In this paper, we fit one-zone galactic chemical evolution models to C, Mg, Si, Ca, Fe, Sr, Y, Ba, and Eu abundances in the dSphs Sculptor and Draco. By including the s- and r-processes in our model, we are able to place constraints on the timescale of r-process enrichment in these dSphs. We find that neutron star mergers cannot be the only source of the r-process in Sculptor and Draco, and that a prompt r-process source is required to explain the observed stellar abundances. When neutron star mergers are the only source of the r-process in our models, their minimum delay time must be just 14-16 Myr. The neutron-capture abundances in Sculptor and Draco can also be fit by models with a longer neutron star merger minimum delay time combined with a prompt r-process source that operates on the same timescale as massive star evolution, such as magneto-rotational supernovae. Additionally, the Sr and Y abundances in the dSphs set limits on the Sr and Y yields per r-process event. We find that theoretical r-process yields overestimate Sr and Y production by a factor of up to 100.

Cluster Ages to Reconstruct the Milky Way Assembly (CARMA). VI. The age-metallicity relation of in-situ clusters and the age of the Universe

Chiara Zerbinati, Davide Massari, Fernando Aguado-Agelet, Cristiano Fanelli, Massimiliano Matteuzzi +9

15 pages, 12 figures, accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFWed 14 Oct
Within the homogeneous framework for the derivation of GC ages defined by the CARMA collaboration, we focus on the clusters born in-situ in the MW. As they are the oldest among the old GCs, their absolute age provides constraints about the age of the Universe. Moreover, the shape of their AMR is expected to show the imprints of past merger events, in case these were able to trigger GC formation in the Galaxy. We derive new homogeneous ages for 18 GCs dynamically and/or chemically classified as in-situ, and build the MW AMR from a total sample of 34 GCs. We find that all the selected GCs follow a well defined AMR, with the exception of NGC6642 and Pal1. While the former is likely a member of the LKH merger event, thus putting chronological evidence for the existence of this progenitor on even more robust ground, for the latter we propose an alternative scenario according to which it formed in-situ, but in a metal-depleted region of the Galaxy, possibly due to the interaction with an unspecified merger event. To find evidence of merger-driven GC formation, we use a GMM for the observed age distribution. This is statistically best-fit by 4 components, which we associate with significant events of the MW merger history. Cluster ages were finally calibrated to a more accurate absolute age scale based on asteroseismic measurements of the mass of red giant stars in NGC6791, and the resulting AMR was fit to compute the look-back time for the beginning of cluster formation in the MW. The fit yields t_GC,i=13.59+0.33-0.32 Gyr for initial cluster formation, and tU=13.79+/-0.34 Gyr for the age of the Universe. This value translates to H0=68.72+3.95-3.49(stat.)+2.65-2.32(sys.) km s-1 Mpc-1, where the systematic error comes from a realistic estimate of the zero point uncertainty on the GC absolute ages derived from stellar models.

The $R$-Process Alliance: On J0010$-$1735, an Accreted, Chemically Unusual, Metal-poor Star with $R$-process Enhancement

Zoe Hackshaw, Rana Ezzeddine, Shivani P. Shah, Terese T. Hansen, Ruijie Shi +9

PDFWed 14 Oct
We present a detailed chemo-dynamical analysis of the chemically peculiar very metal-poor ([Fe/H]$=-2.22\pm 0.11$) Milky Way (MW) halo star, 2MASS J00101758$-$1735387 (hereafter J0010-1735), discovered by the $R$-Process Alliance. We derive abundances for 45 elements, including 28 neutron-capture elements, and confirm that \thestar is highly $r$-process-enhanced, with [Eu/Fe]=$+1.30$. The light, $\alpha$, and Fe-peak element abundances ratios relative to Fe are all sub-solar, aside from C, Si, and most extremely, Ca. We investigate three possible formation scenarios based on the star's chemical abundances and orbital kinematics: whether it formed in the in-situ MW halo, in a MW dwarf galaxy satellite, or in a satellite of the Magellanic Cloud system. Using the position of \thestar in the [Mg/Mn]–[Al/Fe] plane, the abundance pattern of both the light and heavy elements, and the kinematics, we conclude that J0010-1735 most plausibly originated in a now-disrupted ultra-faint dwarf galaxy that was accreted into the MW halo. We examine potential chemical enrichment scenarios of J0010-1735, including one similar to the $r$-process-enhanced UFD Reticulum II, or enrichment via a sub-Chandrasekhar Type Ia SNe, potentially supplemented by a Ca-rich gap transient and by a separate neutron-rich site that facilitated $r$-process enrichment. A nuclear cosmochronometric age of $12.5\pm4.2$ Gyr is derived from the [Th/Eu] ratio, consistent with an ancient origin in one of the earliest star-forming systems in the universe.

The DAWN JWST Archive: A spectroscopically-informed population study of the physical properties of galaxies at z>2

Rashmi Gottumukkala, Gabriel Brammer, Sune Toft, Mengyuan Xiao, Anna de Graaff +28

23 pages, 15 figures, submitted to Astronomy and Astrophysics (A&A)

PDFWed 14 Oct
Charting the evolution of star-formation, stellar mass and dust build-up over cosmic time relies on the accurate characterisation of the stellar population properties of high redshift galaxies. We present a catalogue of spectroscopically derived physical properties for ~12,000 galaxies at z>2, with archival JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy and JWST/NIRCam imaging from the DAWN JWST Archive. We present a custom version of the SED fitting code BAGPIPES, used to perform joint spectrophotometric modelling of PRISM spectra and broadband NIRCam photometry. In doing so, we publicly release a science-ready catalogue of the physical properties of galaxies beyond cosmic noon. We report key physical properties, such as stellar masses, star-formation rates (SFR) and timescales, and V-band dust attenuation (Av). We further probe the recent star-formation histories (SFH) of galaxies via their SFRs on 3 Myr, 10 Myr, and 100 Myr timescales, capturing both rising and falling recent SFHs. Through dust-corrected ugi colour-colour diagrams of massive galaxies, we show how quiescent and dusty galaxies populate this colour space at z>2, with galaxies experiencing a recent downturn in star-formation occupying distinct parameter space. We also study the dustiest galaxies in our sample, showing that they are preferentially more massive and strongly star-forming relative to the parent sample, residing on the high-mass end of the star-forming main-sequence. The slope of the attenuation curve has a strong dependence on Av, varying from steep to shallow as the dust attenuation increases and the specific star-formation rate decreases. Our findings show that the unparalleled number statistics of this catalogue of spectroscopically derived stellar population properties uniquely enables the study star-formation, quenching and dust properties of galaxies from cosmic noon to cosmic dawn.

The Galactic disk structure revealed by Gaia XP spectra

Zhenkang Tang, Jianhui Lian, Jialong Zhou, Jiadong Li

Accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics. 24 pages, 11 figures, including appendices

PDFWed 14 Oct
The spatial structures of chemical populations constrain Galactic disk evolution and its underlying physical processes. Gaia XP spectra provide one of the largest spectroscopic samples available. We use Gaia XP spectra to study Galactic disk mono-abundance populations (MAPs), measuring their scale heights as a function of radius and radial surface-mass-density profiles. We construct a red giant branch (RGB) sample and divide it into MAPs according to $[\alpha/{\rm M}]$ and $[{\rm M/H}]$. The sample covers a $6\times6$ kpc region centered on the Sun in the Galactic plane and extends to $|Z|<3.7$ kpc. We estimate XP completeness using a Gaia photometric parent sample and combine PARSEC isochrones with a Kroupa initial mass function (IMF) to calculate the selection function and convert observed stellar counts into intrinsic stellar mass densities. We fit vertical density profiles with an exponential model to derive scale heights and integrate the fitted profiles to obtain surface mass densities. The resulting profiles are generally consistent with those from APOGEE. All MAPs show flaring, with the high-$\alpha$ population exhibiting the strongest flaring and the metal-poor and sub-solar low-$\alpha$ populations showing the weakest. Surface-mass-density profiles differ significantly among MAPs: the high-$\alpha$ and metal-rich low-$\alpha$ populations show approximately exponential declines, whereas the other low-$\alpha$ populations exhibit plateau-like features, indicating that the Galactic disk cannot be described by a simple single-exponential profile. These differential structures suggest a complex Galactic disk assembly history likely regulated by birth conditions, secular dynamical heating, radial migration, and external perturbations.

Modelling the broken age-[Fe/H] relation and metallicity distribution function of mono-age populations

Qinhao Shao, Jianhui Lian

PDFWed 14 Oct
This study investigates the chemical evolution history and radial migration in the Milky Way utilizing high-precision age measurements based on large-scale stellar spectroscopic surveys. Recent observational data have revealed a distinct break in the age-metallicity relation of stars in the solar neighborhood, suggesting that stellar populations of the Milky Way can be divided into two evolutionary sequences. Based on galactic chemical evolution models, we confirm that this break in the age-metallicity relation can be explained by a recent major gas accretion event, which diluted the metallicity in the disk and triggered a starburst, leading to the formation of an independent age-metallicity sequence. To assess the impact of stellar radial migration, we incorporate a simplified treatment of this process into our multi-zone chemical evolution model. Radial migration is modeled as a probabilistic diffusion process, characterized by a Gaussian distribution. By fitting the model-predicted metallicity distribution functions (MDFs) of mono-age stellar populations to observations, we infer a migration strength of $\sigma = 2.15^{+0.100}_{-0.103}\,{\rm kpc}$ for stars with ages between 2 and 6 Gyr located at 9.5-11.5 kpc, corresponding to an average radial displacement of 1.92 kpc over 10 Gyr. This result is lower than previous estimates of stellar radial migration, suggesting that the role of radial migration in shaping the chemical structure of the Milky Way is less important than previously estimated.

Untangling Similarities and Differences in Star Formation Histories Across Cosmological Simulations

Alicia M. Savelli, Matthew A. Kustec, Joshua S. Speagle, Kartheik G. Iyer, Eshal Ahmed +3

36 pages, 14 figures

PDFWed 14 Oct
How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. We identify sSFH shapes that many simulations share ("common"), well described to first order by dark matter halo assembly, and a few tied to individual simulations ("unique"), which likely reflect subgrid and parametrization choices such as merger-triggered burst prescriptions. A notable exception is a common population of rapidly quenched galaxies found across most simulations, likely driven by e.g., active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, that halo assembly alone cannot reproduce. The origin of the most distinctive unique shape, an Illustris-dominated history with an early peak and slow decline, remains unclear, but we test the effects of short-term variability. With a multi-objective auto-encoder neural network, we recover the simulation that a given sSFH is drawn from with $\sim$95% out-of-sample accuracy; ablation tests indicate this information is embedded in long-timescale sSFH morphology rather than short-term variability. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.

Magnetic field evolution in feedback-dominated galaxies

Yue Samuel Lu, Dušan Kereš, Philip F. Hopkins, Sam B. Ponnada

17 pages, of which 12 are the main text, with 8 figures and 1 table. Submitted to MNRAS. Comments welcome!

PDFWed 14 Oct
Over the last decade, magnetic fields (B-fields) have become a common ingredient in galaxy formation simulations. Although seemingly unimportant in matching observational constraints such as the $M_*-M_{\rm halo}$ relations, the B-field influences the circumgalactic medium (CGM) gas and determines cosmic ray (CR) propagation, making it an indispensable non-thermal component. Nevertheless, the intricate physics of B-fields, particularly how they evolve to the observed $\mu$G-level field, remains largely elusive. In this work, we systematically address the evolution of B-fields over cosmic time using the FIRE-2 (Feedback In Realistic Environments) magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) simulations. We present results for both Milky Way (MW)-mass and dwarf galaxies, with and without CRs. We find that CRs have little impact on B-field properties, while the halo mass controls the saturated B-field strength at $z\sim 0$. However, all runs reach a halo-independent magnetic-to-turbulent ratio of $f_{\rm B}\sim 1-10\%$, though the methods used to measure turbulence could affect this value. We identify a three-stage evolutionary course for the B-field in tandem with galaxy formation: i. expansion, ii. amplification, and iii. saturation. The feature is universal, though the timings depend on the initial location and star formation history of the gas. Flux-freezing is the predominant driver for the first and last stages, exhibiting power relations $B\sim \rho^{2/3}$ and $\rho^{1/2}$, although the latter could also be explained by dynamical and energetic equilibria. The intermediate amplification stage, potentially driven by “dynamos”, is fuelled by structure formation, feedback, and gas turbulence. We conjecture a numerically triggered, turbulence-driven amplification process to explain this phenomenon.

R(V) parameter of the dust extinction curve: mapping based on hot stars and connections to diffuse interstellar bands

R. Lallement, J.L. Vergely, N.L.J. Cox

14 pages, 16 figures, accepted for publication in Astron. Astrophys

PDFWed 14 Oct
Favored carriers of the Diffuse Interstellar Bands (DIBs) are carbonaceous molecules whose constituents have been presumably expelled by dying stars along with those of dust grains. The parallel evolution of DIB carriers and grains and their potential interplay are unknown. We assembled published R(V) measurements of 505 hot stars. We inverted a catalog of extinction data based on Gaia XP spectra and NIR photometry, and used the resulting new 3D extinction map to locate the opaque areas responsible for R(V) for each star. Using archived spectra, we measured their 5780, 5797 and 8620 A DIBs. In parallel, we compared local and line-of-sight (LOS) R(V)s from recent 3D maps with local and LOS Gaia DIB strengths from our recent map. Results: R(V)s from hot stars reveal variations on large spatial scales, confirming with independent data the published 3D pattern. Gaia DIB strength maps show large spatial scale variations similar to those of R(V) maps, with higher abundance of DIB carriers corresponding to higher R(V). LOS Gaia DIB strengths and R(V)s from 3D maps also correlate positively in the R(V) range 2.3-3.4, but there is no connection for higher R(V), likely due to the disappearance of DIB carriers in dense clouds. Despite averaging effects along the LOS to hot stars, we find differences in R(V)-DIB relationships among the three DIBs, with an evolution from negative to positive correlation from the zeta- to the sigma-type DIBs. This can be understood in the frame of the bimodal evolution of R(V) with the extinction, with the zeta (resp. sigma) DIB carrier disappearance in the decreasing (resp. increasing) phase of R(V). Various combinations of DIB strengths and DIB ratios may allow to define the range of R(V), but the relationship between DIBs and R(V) also appears to depend on the wavelength range used to determine R(V).

B. I. Ciocan, N. F. Bouché, J. Fensch, D. Krajnović, J. Freundlich +3

Submitted to A&A, comments are welcome

PDFWed 14 Oct
(Abridged) Baryonic feedback is expected to transform cuspy dark-matter (DM) haloes into cores, but direct observational links between inner DM slopes and galaxy star-formation histories (SFHs) remain limited. We investigate and quantify the connection between halo inner slopes and baryonic assembly histories in a large sample of intermediate-z galaxies. We analyse 375 galaxies at 0.1<z<1.43 spanning 7<log(Mstar/Msun)<11.2 using 3D forward modelling of MUSE kinematics and disk-halo decompositions with mass-dependent (DC14-based) and generalised Navarro-Frenk-White (gNFW) halo profiles. Stellar population parameters are inferred from multi-band Spectral Energy Distribution fitting of UV-to-MIR photometry using a non-parametric SFH model. Bayesian model comparison shows that the DC14-based halo density profiles are statistically preferred over gNFW for a substantial fraction of the sample. The inferred inner slopes from gNFW fits nevertheless follow the DC14 expectation and reproduce the expected dependence on the stellar-to-halo mass ratio, despite this not being explicitly imposed. Partial correlation coefficient and random forest regression analyses consistently identify stellar mass (or, equivalently, the stellar-to-halo mass ratio) as the primary driver of the inner density slope, a result that holds robustly even when the analysis is restricted to the gNFW fits. In contrast, SFH-related quantities (e.g. formation time and 'burstiness' indicators) exhibit no independent predictive power on the DM halo inner slope once the dependence on stellar mass is accounted for. Cored systems preferentially occupy lower-mass, more bursty bins with later assembly times, whereas cuspy systems are associated with higher stellar masses, lower star formation activity, and earlier assembly epochs, in agreement with theoretical expectations of stellar-feedback-induced cores.

Peng Wang, Yu Luo, Jiang Chang, Xi Kang, Shi Shao

We examine whether the unusual spatial flattening and orbital-plane coherence of the Milky Way's classical satellites are connected to the Galaxy's location in the cosmic web. We compare the 11 classical satellites with the 11 most massive satellites of 11,922 Milky Way-mass hosts in the TNG300-1 hydrodynamical simulation. We measure flattening with both the simple inertia tensor (SIT), which retains radial information, and the reduced inertia tensor (RIT), which isolates angular anisotropy. Orbital-plane coherence is characterized by the minimum opening angle containing the axes of the eight most coplanar satellite orbits. For the Milky Way, we find $(c/a,b/a)=(0.183,0.558)$ for SIT and $(0.364,0.736)$ for RIT, together with an eight-orbit opening angle of $21.9^\circ$. This result does not rule out an origin in anisotropic accretion, since neither the present-day web class nor its shear eigenvalues fully encodes the assembly history of an individual satellite system.

Nearly pristine gas in a galaxy 1 billion years after the Big Bang

Sarah E. I. Bosman, Frederick B. Davies, Louise Welsh, Emanuele P. Farina, Prakash Gaikwad +20

Accepted in Nature

PDFWed 14 Oct
Excitation of carbon and oxygen ions is the primary mechanism by which collapsing gas clouds dissipate heat to form new stars. At early cosmic times, in galaxies with chemically pristine gas, many stars are expected to have masses far larger than the mass of our sun due to the deficit in these elements. We searched for ultra-metal-poor galaxies using their spectroscopic absorption signatures in front of bright background quasars, employing the new GHOST high-resolution spectrograph. Here we report the discovery of the first galaxy containing a reservoir of neutral hydrogen gas with metallicity below the ultra-metal-poor threshold: with a measured carbon-to-hydrogen ratio 11,000 times lower than the Sun, it is the most pristine ever found by a factor of four. UMP-DLA-1 is the only known galaxy with measured carbon and oxygen abundances too low to enable efficient formation of stars with solar mass or below. The abundance pattern of UMP-DLA-1 is likely a close representation of the atomic yields of first- or early second-generation stars.

EDGE-INFERNO: The chemical signatures of assembly history in reionization fossils

Eric P. Andersson, Martin P. Rey, Alexander P. Ji, Justin I. Read, Oscar Agertz +7

Accepted for publication in A&A. Abstract abridged for arXiv

PDFWed 14 Oct
In galaxies, stellar populations represent a superposition of both in situ and accreted components, tracing internal chemical evolution as well as the histories of merged satellites. We investigate how assembly history shapes the chemical properties of present-day stellar populations in low-mass dwarf galaxies. We analyze a suite of EDGE-INFERNO star-by-star cosmological zoom-in simulations of galaxies with present-day halo mass $\sim1.4\times10^9\,{\rm M}_{\odot}$. We systematically vary galaxy growth while preserving the present-day halo mass, engineering our simulations to span a continuum of assembly histories. Early-forming systems form most of their stars in a single progenitor, with centrally concentrated star formation and a continuous enrichment history. In contrast, our late-forming systems assemble by accretion of multiple progenitors with their own stellar populations, resulting in fragmented star formation across several halos with independent chemical evolution tracks. Fully separating in situ and accreted populations likely requires kinematic information. Accreted stars can contribute substantially even at high metallicities in multi-progenitor systems, while external enrichment can cause accreted material to follow similar chemical trends as in situ stars, particularly in the single-progenitor regime.

Globular cluster Observations for Luminosity-dispersion Distances (GOLD): Project description and velocity catalogue

Galactic Outflows and Star-Formation Quenching: A JWST Perspective

Rebecca L. Davies, Sirio Belli

To be published as an invited review in the Astrophysics & Space Science 2025 Astronomy Prize Awardees Collection

PDFThu 15 Oct
Outflows driven by active galactic nuclei (AGN) are widely considered a key mechanism for regulating and quenching star formation in massive galaxies. However, direct observational evidence linking outflows to quenching has remained elusive for decades. This article reviews recent findings for massive galaxies at cosmic noon ($z\sim 2$), based on sensitive James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations tracing both star formation histories (through Balmer absorption lines) and neutral outflows (through blueshifted NaID absorption). Neutral outflows are found in $\sim$ 25 - 50% of massive ($\log(M_*/M_\odot)>$ 10) galaxies, independent of star formation rate. Outflows from star-forming galaxies are consistent with being powered by star-formation and/or AGN activity, whereas outflows from quiescent systems are generally attributed to current or previous AGN activity. There is growing evidence that recent rapid quenching of star-formation is associated with powerful AGN-driven outflows with mass loading factors $\eta \gtrsim$ 1 - 10. The neutral gas dominates the outflow mass budget but in most cases its velocity is below the halo escape velocity, indicating that this gas may form part of a galactic fountain. Detections of neutral gas inflows and residual cold gas reservoirs in quiescent galaxies suggest that other feedback mechanisms such as AGN heating are required to keep galaxies quenched over long timescales. We highlight the potential of multiphase outflow measurements to constrain feedback models, describe the main systematic uncertainties in mass outflow rate measurements, and discuss how the $z\sim 2$ results inform our understanding of the formation of the earliest massive quiescent galaxies.

Cosmology with strong gravitational lenses

Tommaso Treu

Reviews of Modern Physics, invited article; 59 pages, 32 figures

PDFThu 15 Oct
Three of the most pressing open questions in cosmology – the nature of dark energy and dark matter, and the growing tension in the value of the Hubble constant H$_0$ – call for cosmological probes that are independent of, and complementary to, traditional methods. Strong gravitational lensing offers exactly that: a purely gravitational probe of the universe's expansion history and matter distribution, sensitive to dark matter's presence regardless of whether it interacts with light. Time-delay lenses and multiplane lenses can measure H$_0$ and the dark energy equation of state directly, while the distortion of lensed images can reveal the abundance and internal structure of dark matter subhalos. After a brief theoretical and historical introduction, I review recent breakthroughs enabled by JWST and by advances in lens modeling and stellar dynamics. Examples include the recent measurement of H$_0$ with 3% precision in flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter cosmology, and a tentative detection of dark matter self-interaction. Strong lensing is entering a pivotal era: wide-field surveys with Euclid, Rubin, and Roman, and radio surveys with the Square Kilometer Array, are poised to increase the number of known strong lenses by orders of magnitude, positioning strong lensing to play a central role in answering these open questions in the decade ahead.

The Role of Environment in Shaping the Assembly Timelines of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at Cosmic Noon

Tianmu Gao, J. Trevor Mendel, Letizia Bugiani, Minjung Park, Sirio Belli +8

8 pages, 3 figures. Resubmitted to MNRAS after moderate revision

PDFThu 15 Oct
JWST observations of massive quiescent galaxies (MQGs) at $z\sim2$ have revealed diverse star formation histories (SFHs). In this letter, we investigate whether these variations are linked to the environment in which MQGs reside, as quantified by the host dark matter halo mass. We identify 12 MQGs from a representative sample of galaxies at $1.6<z<3.5$ obtained by the Blue Jay survey, a JWST Cycle 1 program, and infer their SFHs via modelling of the deep NIRSpec medium-resolution spectra along with multi-band photometry. By correlating the formation and quenching epochs of individual MQGs with their respective host halo masses from the 3D-HST survey, we find that after accounting for the redshift range of the sample ($1.8<z<2.5$), MQGs in more massive haloes formed at significantly earlier cosmic epochs. Meanwhile, although these galaxies also quenched earlier overall, the scatter in quenching epochs across similar halo masses indicates that the quenching process likely involves other contributing factors and is not solely determined by halo mass.

Simulations of Dust Content and Attenuation in $z=5-15$ Galaxies

Dhruv T. Zimmerman, Jed McKinney, Desika Narayanan, Caitlin M. Casey, John Chisholm +8

20 pages, 11 figures. Comments welcome! To be submitted

PDFThu 15 Oct
Correctly modeling the dust attenuation law in galaxies is essential to properly inferring their growth and physical properties. With the rapid discovery of $z>6$ galaxies by JWST and lack of constraints on the properties of dust at these redshifts, we must turn to theoretical models to predict the properties of dust at these redshifts. In this paper, we develop a theoretical foundation for the nature of high-redshift attenuation laws. We analyze $107$ cosmological zoom-in simulations of galaxies run with the SMUGGLE galaxy formation model. SMUGGLE includes an explicitly modeled multiphase interstellar medium and a state-of-the-art dust model with a self-consistently evolving dust grain size distribution. We forward model these simulations with the radiative transfer package POWDERDAY to produce synthetic spectra and attenuation curves for a set of $z=5-15$, $M_*\sim10^{6.5}-10^{10}\; \mathrm{M}_{\odot}$ galaxies. We find evidence for flatter attenuation curves than the commonly used locally-calibrated literature attenuation laws assumed when modeling high-redshift galaxies. The vast majority of our attenuation curves also lack the 2175 Angstrom absorption feature. Our attenuation laws do not evolve strongly over $z=15-5$, which we attribute to relatively constant fractions of small grains up to $z=4$ in our simulations. We present a set of simulation-informed priors on dust attenuation law parameterization that could be implemented in SED fitting codes. There is an anti-correlation between galaxy mass and attenuation curve slope and positive correlation between galaxy mass and $A_V$, which we attribute to dust growth becoming efficient in our massive galaxies with $\log{M_*/\mathrm{M}_{\odot}}\gtrsim9$ and with ISM conditions above a critical metallicity threshold.

Sleeping or Dead? Probing the Quenching Mechanism of JWST Dormant Galaxies at z > 3 with ALMA

Mengyuan Xiao, Songnan Qi, Pascal A. Oesch, Longji Bing, Miroslava Dessauges-Zavadsky +14

19 pages, 12 figures, 3 tables (including Appendix), submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFThu 15 Oct
JWST has revealed a population of "dormant” galaxies at z>3 with strong Balmer breaks and weak emission lines, whose star formation appears to have declined only recently. Whether these galaxies are undergoing a lasting transition toward quiescence or merely experiencing a temporary suppression of star formation remains unclear. We investigate their molecular gas content using archival ALMA observations of [CII]158um and CO rotational transitions. Starting from all publicly available JWST/NIRSpec prism observations and using joint spectrophotometric fitting, we construct a parent sample at z>3 and log(Mstar/Msun)>9.5 in the ALMA-accessible COSMOS, UDS, and GOODS-South fields. Our final sample comprises 7 dormant galaxies and 15 quiescent galaxies with ALMA constraints on their molecular gas content (mostly 3 sigma upper limits). We find that dormant galaxies exhibit systematically lower star formation rates averaged over the past 10Myr (SFR10Myr) than over the past 100Myr (SFR100Myr), supporting a recent rapid decline in their star formation. For the ~70% of the sample with sufficiently constraining ALMA observations, their molecular gas fractions lie at least ~0.3-2dex below those expected for main-sequence galaxies. Moreover, the molecular gas fractions of dormant galaxies show no systematic difference from those of quiescent galaxies in our sample. This indicates that dormant galaxies have already reached a gas-poor state shortly after quenching and favors a scenario in which the rapid shutdown of star formation is associated with depletion or removal of the molecular gas reservoir, rather than primarily with suppressed star formation efficiency while retaining a substantial molecular gas reservoir. Our results suggest that, if no substantial gas replenishment occurs in the future, dormant galaxies are more consistent with becoming "dead” than with being temporarily "sleeping”.

Galactic Morpholography: Linking present-day galaxy compactness to assembly history and relic formation in the COLIBRE simulations

Maximilian Zemsch, Claudia D.P. Lagos, Chiara Spiniello, Michele Cappellari, Ángel Chandro-Gómez +9

21 pages, 15 figures

PDFThu 15 Oct
Compactness is widely used to identify candidate relic galaxies, the largely untouched survivors of the first compact massive galaxies. Yet relicness is an evolutionary rather than structural property. Using the COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, we test whether present-day structure traces galaxy formation and assembly histories. Six complementary tracers (stellar age, metallicity, [Mg/Fe], sSFR, velocity dispersion and ex-situ mass fraction) vary coherently across the stellar mass-size plane and independently identify a characteristic compactness scale of $\log_{10}(\Sigma_{1.5,\mathrm{crit}}/(\mathrm{M}_\odot\mathrm{kpc}^{-1.5}))=9.75\pm0.10$. We term this mapping between present-day structure and assembly history morpholography. Combining this threshold with the Degree of Relicness ($\mathrm{DoR}>0.6$), which quantifies early and rapid star formation, we distinguish compact from ancient galaxies and identify 267 relic candidates at their intersection. Ancient galaxies span nearly the full ex-situ-fraction range, from predominantly in-situ formation to substantial merger-driven growth. Relic candidates are concentrated at stellar masses of $\approx 10^{10}$-$10^{11}$ solar masses, host black holes that lie $\approx 0.25$-$2.5$ dex above the median black-hole-stellar-mass relation, and are predominantly satellites (76%), preferentially in cluster environments. Only $\approx 3$% show evidence of strong tidal stripping, suggesting that environment primarily preserves rather than creates relic-like systems. Approximately one third (87/267) are direct descendants of the $z=2$ massive quiescent galaxies identified in COLIBRE. Present-day structure thus retains a statistical fossil record of assembly history, supporting morpholography as a link between nearby galaxies and the early Universe.

SQuIGGLE: Radio AGN Found Predominantly in Young, Gas-rich Post-starburst Galaxies at z ~ 0.7

Vincenzo R. D'Onofrio, Justin S. Spilker, Krista L. Smith, Rachel Bezanson, Robert Feldmann +11

21 pages, 11 figures, submitted to ApJ. Comments welcome!

PDFThu 15 Oct
The processes that rapidly quench massive galaxies and maintain their quiescence remain poorly understood. Substantial molecular gas reservoirs can survive this shutdown in post-starburst galaxies, raising the question of what prevents the remaining gas from efficiently forming stars. We present deep, subarcsecond VLA 6 GHz observations of 52 massive SQuIGGLE post-starburst galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ to test the prevalence and mechanical impact of radio activity during the gas-rich post-burst phase. We detect 6 GHz continuum emission in 18/52 galaxies ($\approx$35%), spanning $\log(L_{1.4\,{\rm GHz}}/{\rm W\,Hz^{-1}})\simeq23.0$–$25.0$ and generally exceeding the contributions expected from ongoing star formation and the fading starburst, favoring an AGN origin. Ancillary data at other wavelengths provide little evidence for a population of luminous, radiatively efficient AGN, suggesting that these sources are instead dominated by comparatively weak, mechanically powered radio AGN. Radio activity is strongly concentrated within the first $\sim$200 Myr post-burst and is predominantly associated with galaxies retaining substantial gas reservoirs. Above a common radio luminosity threshold, the radio detection fraction is enhanced by a factor of $\approx$2.5 relative to the radio AGN candidate fraction in mass- and redshift-matched quiescent LEGA-C galaxies, primarily due to lower power sources. For efficient jet–ISM coupling, the inferred mechanical power could supply the approximate energy required to unbind the molecular gas on timescales comparable to its observed decline. Together, these results identify a population of radiatively inefficient AGN hosted predominantly by young, gas-rich post-starburst galaxies at $z\sim0.7$ and suggest that they may have a role in maintaining inefficient star formation.

LiDO: The Large inclination Distant Objects Survey

Mike Alexandersen, Rosemary E. Pike, Ying-Tung Chen, Samantha M. Lawler, Mark Comte +18

40 pages, 17 figured, 5 tables,

PDFThu 15 Oct
Previous trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs) discovery surveys targeted the ecliptic plane, where the overall highest density is seen. High-inclination objects spend little time near the plane, and are thus under-represented in previous TNO surveys. The Large inclination Distant Objects (LiDO) survey targets these high-inclination populations in 34 this http URL., located in two blocks 14-21 degrees north and south of the ecliptic plane, finding 148 objects, all with i>14 degrees due to survey design. A careful characterization of detection efficiency and other survey parameters results in a "characterized sample" of 125 objects detected within the survey's magnitude and rate limits, thus suitable for model comparison via survey simulations. LiDO detected 62 TNOs classified as resonant, including 21 Plutinos, of which 12 are securely Kozai resonators (always coming to pericenter off-ecliptic), as well as 9 objects in the 5:2 resonance, and several mixed mode inclination-eccentricity resonators. No objects beyond 63 au were detected, despite sensitivity to objects as far as 150 au. We found that the i>14 degree orbital distribution of the previous model from OSSOS was rejectable with the inclusion of the 38 characterized hot classical TNOs from LiDO. We constructed a proof-of-concept orbital distribution model of hot classical TNOs based on long-term stability sculpting, which is statistically acceptable for the LiDO and OSSOS samples, while producing population estimates (21,000$^{+3000}_{-3000}$ hot classicals with H_r<8.3) consistent with OSSOS estimates. The discovery of 125 characterized mid-to-high inclination objects in a variety of orbital classifications provides useful constraints for population modeling and the migration history of the Solar System.

Strong gravitational lensing with Euclid

Alessandro Sonnenfeld

Contribution to the Focus Point ''Surprises from major new telescopes''

PDFThu 15 Oct
Euclid data is going to lead to the discovery of approximately 10^5 new strong gravitational lenses, thereby increasing the number of known lenses by a factor of 30. Such an impressive jump has the potential to revolutionise the field of strong lensing, by covering an ever-increasing range in the parameter space occupied by the lenses and lensed sources, and by uncovering rare and precious strong lens systems. The first quick data release of Euclid has already revealed hundreds of lenses, giving us a taste of what the final Euclid strong lens sample might look like. These lenses include a variety of objects acting as deflectors, from isolated galaxies to clusters, from early-type galaxies to edge-on disks. As many as four double-source plane lenses, in which one foreground object lenses two galaxies at two different redshifts, have already been discovered. As more data are acquired, it might become possible to detect the strong lensing signature of dwarf galaxy satellites, and to find lenses with an unusually large number of images. In this paper, we try to anticipate the kind of discoveries that these data will bring along.

As the next generation of space telescopes prepares for operations, including the China Space Station Telescope (CSST), accurate PSF modeling has become increasingly important. However, accurately modeling the PSF remains challenging, particularly for poorly sampled images. The ringing Large Magellanic Cloud: evidence for a recent dwarf-dwarf collision

Slater J. Oden, Bethany Garver, Joshua Povick, David L. Nidever, Victor P. Debattista

PDFThu 15 Oct
Galaxy interactions are ubiquitous in the Universe and represent a primary pathway for galaxy growth. Close passages of dwarf galaxies can induce large-scale vertical oscillations in stellar disks, a phenomenon first identified in the Milky Way within the past decade. Here, we use recent red clump distance maps of the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) to show that its stellar periphery is vertically offset from the central disk plane by $\sim$1.2 kpc at radii of $\sim$8.5 kpc and exhibits an S-shaped warp reaching $\sim$3.6 kpc at $\sim$11.5 kpc. We compare these observations to an $N$-body simulation of the LMC-Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) system, in which a direct collision between the Clouds excites vertical oscillations in the outer LMC disk with amplitudes of $\sim$1~kpc and a period of $\sim$600 Myr. The simulated disk best matches the observed vertical structure $\sim$260 Myr after the collision, although this timing depends on the adopted LMC model. At that time, the simulation reproduces the sign and radial onset of the observed offset and the orientation of the warp, but underpredicts the amplitude of the warp by a factor of $\sim$4-5. These results support a collision-driven origin for the large-scale vertical structure of the LMC disk and demonstrate that dwarf–dwarf interactions can reshape stellar disks on galactic scales.

The mass functions of multiple stellar populations in 47 Tucanae

M. Scalco, J. Bruce, E. Vesperini, L. R. Bedin

6 pages, 6 figures (3 in Appendices A-B), 1 table in Appendix A. Published in A&A Letters (this https URL). Accepted on 8 July 2026

PDFThu 15 Oct
We used a combination of James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations to investigate the mass functions (MFs) of the different stellar populations in the globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae (NGC 104) over a wide stellar-mass interval. We identified the multiple populations along the lower main sequence using only JWST photometry and, in particular, colour combinations sensitive to variations in oxygen abundance. We derived the MFs of the first- and second-population (1P and 2P) stars separately and found that in the low-mass regime, the present-day MF of the 1P population is significantly steeper than that of the 2P population, which appears nearly flat. We also present the results of a dynamical model showing that this behaviour requires a different initial mass function (IMF) for the 2P and the 1P populations in the 0.1-0.4 solar masses mass range, thus providing a new fundamental constraint for the study of the star formation of multiple populations in GCs.

The convective-transition gap in 47 Tucanae with JWST

M. Scalco, L. R. Bedin, M. Libralato, M. Griggio, E. Vesperini +1

We report the detection of the convective-transition gap along the lower main sequence (MS) of the Galactic globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae using deep James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations. We analyse the stellar luminosity function (LF) in the vicinity of the gap location using two independent colour-magnitude diagrams (CMDs). In both cases, the LF is characterised by a narrow gap followed by a discontinuous increase in the stellar number counts, and presents an overall morphology qualitatively consistent with that reported in previous studies. By modelling the observed LFs with an empirical likelihood model, we estimate the statistical significance of the observed feature. Additional JWST passbands exhibit similar signatures, providing further evidence for the presence of a discontinuity.

Uncovering the origin of complex velocity distributions in edge-on disc galaxies

Sophie Townson, Marie Martig, Robert J. J. Grand, Prashin Jethwa, Jesús Falcón-Barroso +1

Accepted for publication by MNRAS

PDFThu 15 Oct
Stellar line-of-sight velocity distributions are a powerful tool for studying galaxy kinematics and formation history, with multiple-peaked LOSVDs often indicating the presence of complex kinematic components. We extract stellar LOSVDs across the disc of a simulated spiral galaxy viewed edge-on, and study how often and why we see multiple-peaked LOSVDs. We find that the multiple-peaked LOSVDs are mainly found close to the midplane because they result from the presence of spiral arms. This is not because the spiral arms have significantly different kinematics to the disc, but because they correspond to over-densities. Variations in the number of arms present along a line-of-sight create variations in the number of peaks in LOSVDs. This can be used to quantify the strength of the spiral arms above the midplane, and how the thickness of spiral arms vary across the disc. We find that the commonly used fourth order Gauss-Hermite moment, $h_4$, is negative in the regions where there are multiple-peaked LOSVDs, and hence traces spiral arms. Finally, we discuss the observability of the multiple-peaked regions and their associated $h_4$ measurements with current integral field spectrographs. We find that the spectral resolution of MUSE might not be high enough to uncover multiple peaks in LOSVDs when they result from spiral arms, but should allow for identification of the regions of negative $h_4$. On the other hand, higher resolution spectrographs like KOALA and WEAVE may be able to recover multiple peaks in LOSVDs, representing a promising avenue for characterising spiral arms in edge-on disc galaxies.

How JWST is Revolutionizing Our View of Early Galaxy Formation

Rychard Bouwens

41 pages, 25 figures, 1 table, invited review, accepted for publication in Contemporary Physics

PDFThu 15 Oct
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has been revolutionizing our understanding of early galaxy formation over its first four years of science operations. With un- precedented sensitivity, spatial resolution, and wavelength coverage from the near- to mid-infrared, JWST has enabled astronomers to identify hundreds of star-forming galaxies at redshifts z>~8 (within ~650 million years of the Big Bang) through strong spectral breaks and characterize these galaxies through sensitive spectroscopy. These capabilities have provided new insights into the stellar populations, neutral gas, nebular continuum emission, chemical enrichment, dust content, and ionization conditions of galaxies during cosmic dawn, revealing more numerous and brighter systems than anticipated from pre-JWST models. We discuss the implications of these discoveries for reionization, the buildup of stellar mass and metals, and the evolution of galaxy populations and luminosity functions. JWST is also revealing a new physical picture of early galaxies, characterized by compact, clumpy star formation, high gas fractions, and intense radiation fields. Despite much progress, many open questions remain, including the efficiency, intermittency, and stellar initial mass function (IMF) of early star formation, conditions in the interstellar medium (ISM), masses, and structure of the earliest galaxies, and the topology of reionization. Resolving these open questions will require lensing magnification, sensitive spectroscopy to > 5 micron, including with the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA), exploration to higher redshifts, and larger, more complete samples, helping to clarify how the first galaxies formed and evolved.

Hot Molecular Hydrogen in Galaxies at Redshift ~ 0.2-1.5 Revealed by JWST

Jie Chen, Linhua Jiang, Shuqi Fu, Zijian Zhang, Danyang Jiang +3

20 pages, 17 figures. Submitted to ApJ

PDFThu 15 Oct
Molecular hydrogen (H$_2$) is the fundamental component of the molecular gas reservoir that fuels star formation and black hole growth. While prominent hot H$_2$ emission ($\gtrsim 1000$ K) has been explored in nearby galaxies, its excitation mechanisms at higher redshifts remain poorly constrained. We present a systematic study of hot molecular gas traced via ro-vibrational H$_2$ emitters at z~0.2-1.5 using JWST slitless spectroscopy. Exploiting JWST's great sensitivity to near-infrared H$_2$ ro-vibrational transitions such as H$_2$ 1-0 S(1), we build a sample of 144 H$_2$-luminous galaxies and characterize their multi-wavelength properties. About 96.5% and 80% of these galaxies are detected in the radio and far-infrared (FIR) bands, respectively, and their hot H$_2$ emission strongly correlates with the radio and FIR radiation. Based on radio excess, X-ray emission, and optical diagnostics, 62 sources (43%) exhibit significant AGN activity. The H$_2$ line luminosities of the sample show a sublinear dependence on radio luminosity, while the relation between the H$_2$ luminosities and total infrared luminosities is approximately linear after excluding the radio-excess sources. These scaling relations suggest that star formation provides a baseline contribution to hot H$_2$ excitation, while radio-excess sources require an additional contribution from AGN-related processes such as jet- or wind-driven shocks. These results reveal a multi-channel excitation scenario and shed new light on the interplay between star formation, AGN activity, and the interstellar medium during galaxy evolution.

TNGC-SIDM I: Evolution of massive cluster mergers under the self-interacting dark matter paradigm

Wonki Lee, Dylan Nelson, Myungkook J. Jee, Giulia Despali, Mark Vogelsberger +6

24 pages, 23 figures. To be submitted to A&A. Comments welcome

PDFThu 15 Oct
Merging galaxy clusters provide an ideal laboratory for testing the self-interacting nature of dark matter. In this paper, we present a pilot study of magnetohydrodynamical cosmological zoom-in simulations of two cluster mergers selected from TNG-Cluster, with $M_{\rm 200c} \sim 9\times10^{14}~M_\odot$ and $M_{\rm 200c}\sim 2\times10^{14}~M_\odot$. The simulations are performed with both standard collisionless cold dark matter (CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), assuming an isotropic, velocity-independent cross-section of $\sigma_{\rm T}/m_\chi = 1~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$, with an additional $0.5~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$ run for the low-mass system, and the mass resolution and galaxy formation model are unchanged from TNG-Cluster. Our results indicate that sample selection plays a key role in testing the possible self-interacting nature of dark matter using cluster mergers, and that double radio relic cluster mergers and massive clusters with evidence of an ancient major merger offer a promising testbed for this purpose.

Any colour you like: The diverse chromaticities of white dwarfs

Mark A. Hollands, Boris T. Gaensicke, Pier-Emmanuel Tremblay, Detlev Koester

Accepted for publication in RASTI. 32 pages, 20 figures

PDFThu 15 Oct
Main sequence stars have perceived colours ranging from orange to white to blue with increasing effective temperature. This is because their atmospheric opacities cause only minor deviations from blackbody colours when integrated over the broadband sensitivity curves of the human visual system. White dwarfs can have much denser atmospheres, reaching ~ 1 g/cm3 for the coolest known objects. If metals such as carbon, calcium, or sodium are present in these ultra-dense atmospheres, extreme broadening of atomic and molecular opacities can produce optical spectra that diverge significantly from blackbodies. We investigated how the combination of temperature and chemical composition can affect the perceived colours (chromaticity) of white dwarfs. While we found that most white dwarfs are similar in colour to blackbodies, we also demonstrate examples of known stars that would appear yellow, green, cyan, pink, or purple if viewed close enough for their stellar discs to be resolved. We examined the chromaticity distribution for the volume-limited 20 pc local sample of white dwarfs finding a median correlated colour temperature of ~= 6800 K, establishing that, on average, white dwarfs are indeed white in colour. Finally we show how white dwarf spectral models can be used to generate colour-accurate images of their stellar discs as they would appear from up close.

JWST observations of 47 Tucanae: I. The low-mass main sequence down to the hydrogen-burning limit

M. Scalco, R. Gerasimov, L. R. Bedin, E. Vesperini, M. Griggio +2

16 pages, 14 figures (1 figure in Appendix A), 1 table. Published in A&A (this https URL). Accepted on 22 June 2026

PDFThu 15 Oct
We present the first results of a series of studies based on JWST observations of the globular cluster 47 Tucanae (47 Tuc, or NGC 104) obtained with NIRCam. By combining new data with earlier JWST observations, we derive high-precision proper motions (PMs) over a multi-year baseline (of ~3 years) and construct a clean sample of cluster members extending to very faint magnitudes (F444W~24.25 mag, corresponding to ~0.077 solar masses, slightly above the estimated hydrogen-burning limit of 47 Tuc, ~0.074 solar masses) along the main sequence (MS). We investigated the presence of the low-mass MS kink previously identified in 47 Tuc using the new F090W and F444W photometry. No significant signature of the feature is found using these filters, consistent with its proposed physical interpretation as a consequence of the transition in carbon chemistry toward a methane-dominated equilibrium in very low-mass stars. A comparison with theoretical isochrones indicates that the stellar population near the bottom of the MS is likely dominated by oxygen-poor stars, in agreement with previous findings. Using artificial-star tests, we quantify the completeness of our sample and show that the requirement of PM-based membership significantly limits the detection of the faintest objects, indicating that current JWST observations of 47 Tuc are not sufficiently deep to reliably trace the cluster's brown dwarf cooling sequence. The mass function (MF) of 47 Tuc shows a structured behaviour, with a break at ~0.15 solar masses not associated with the onset of convection, and a mild but systematic radial variation consistent with mass segregation.

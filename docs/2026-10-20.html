The Star Formation Histories of Different Components of M31's Halo from Photometric Estimates of Fe and $α$ Abundances

Jihye Hong, Evan Kirby, Roman Gerasimov, Masashi Chiba, Yutaka Komiyama +4

19 pages, 12 figures, 3 tables, submitted to ApJ

PDFTue 20 Oct
We present the chemical-abundance distributions and star-formation histories of the M31 halo components using photometric Fe and $\alpha$ abundance estimates derived from the PAndAS broad-band $g$ and $i$ filters and the Subaru/HSC $NB515$ narrow-band filter. We selected about 76,400 candidate M31 giant stars and constructed a synthetic photometric training dataset based on model isochrones. We estimated elemental abundances and errors using random forest regression models. We identify various halo components, including about 32,500 stars in the “smooth" halo (where “smooth" refers to the complete sample with all the known substructures removed), and infer their star-formation histories from comparisons of their abundance distributions. From the radial abundance gradients, we find that [Fe/H] decreases while [$\alpha$/Fe] increases toward the outer halo. Including substructures further enhances the radial abundance contrast, suggesting that the smooth halo stars are accreted at relatively early times, before the progenitor galaxies produce large numbers of Type Ia supernovae. Both M31 and the Milky Way show similar radial gradient trends, but M31 exhibits higher [Fe/H] and lower [$\alpha$/Fe] values overall, suggesting that the smooth halo of M31 may possess a larger fraction of relatively younger stars associated with more recent merger events. Future photometric observations with additional narrow-band filters and larger spectroscopic samples are expected to provide a better understanding of the formation and evolution history of M31.

The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. I. Evidence from Low-angular-momentum, High-$α$ Stars Scattered Out of the Protodisk by Merger Events

Tiancheng Sun, Maosheng Xiang, Shaolan Bi, Jifeng Liu, Meng Zhang +1

19 pages, 10 figures, Published in AJ

PDFTue 20 Oct
Stars born in the high-$\alpha$ disk can later acquire low angular momentum through dynamical scattering during early merger events. Their age–metallicity distribution thus provides a fossil record of the history of both star formation and dynamical perturbations in the early Milky Way disk. Here we identify a sample of 2,078 such stars from more than two million Gaia DR3 subgiants with robust determinations of ages, kinematics, and abundances. From this sample we reconstruct their intrinsic age–metallicity distribution with a forward modeling approach. Our results confirm that these scattered stars are older and more metal-poor than the bulk of the high-$\alpha$ disk on average. In particular, the joint age–metallicity distribution reveals a robust two-component feature: a broad, dominant component with a peak age of $\sim$11.5 Gyr and ${\rm [Fe/H]}\simeq-0.59$, and an older and more metal-poor component with a peak age of $\sim$12.5 Gyr and ${\rm [Fe/H]}\simeq-0.86$. The presence of this two-component structure suggests that the high-$\alpha$ disk experienced episodic phases of star formation and chemical enrichment. The age distribution of the younger Splash component, compared with that of the high-$\alpha$ disk, provides evidence for a substantial dynamical perturbation at a lookback time of 10.8–11.5 Gyr, possibly associated with an early passage of Gaia–Sausage–Enceladus (GSE). The older Splash component, with a peak age of $\sim$12.5 Gyr, may preserve signatures of an earlier interaction, possibly associated with the proposed Low-energy–Kraken–Heracles (LKH) event.

Forecasting the Resolvability of Stellar Velocity Dispersions in Ultra-faint Dwarf Galaxies

Pierre Thibodeaux, Alex Drlica-Wagner, Alexander P. Ji, Chin Yi Tan, Ting S. Li +2

14 pages + 3 appendix, 14 figures, 1 table. To be submitted to Open Journal of Astrophysics

PDFTue 20 Oct
Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies (UFDs)—the smallest and most dark-matter dominated galactic systems—push the boundaries of our understanding of galaxy formation in the cold dark matter paradigm. As current and near-future imaging surveys continue to expand the known population of extremely faint systems, spectroscopic resources have become a major limitation in our ability to understand the physical nature of these systems. We present projections for the capability of Keck/DEIMOS, Magellan/IMACS, and future spectroscopic instruments (e.g., Magellan/Via and GMT/GMACS) to kinematically distinguish dark-matter-dominated UFDs from dark-matter-free star clusters in the ultra-faint regime. We simulate ensembles of mock observations of ultra-faint dwarf galaxies and apply a formal statistical framework, calibrated to observations, to systematically characterize the ability to resolve velocity dispersions as a function of satellite properties. Our projections suggest that current instruments will struggle to resolve many of systems that will be discovered by the Rubin Observatory. Near future instruments will be capable of resolving a majority of these systems, though they will fail for the faintest and most distant systems. Ultimately, we will require even more advanced instruments, with enhanced stability and velocity precision, to classify the full population of ultra-faint Milky Way satellites.

Probing the Assembly of Triangulum: Precision Metallicities and $α$ Abundances of M33's Star Clusters from APOGEE Integrated-Light Spectroscopy

Benjamin J. Gibson, Borja Anguiano, Sten Hasselquist, Gail Zasowski, Anil Seth +5

29 pages, 16 figures

PDFTue 20 Oct
The star cluster system of M33 provides an excellent opportunity to investigate whether galaxy formation physics is dependent on a galaxy's mass. Older globular clusters (GCs) trace hierarchical assembly and chemical enrichment, while younger clusters trace the recent star formation. By studying the M33 star cluster system as a whole, we can probe the formation physics of galaxies at a lower mass than the Milky Way, and look for signals of accretion. To that end, we present analysis of 37 M33 star clusters using high-resolution ($R\sim22,500$), near-infrared (1.5-1.7 $\mu$m), integrated light spectra from the APOGEE survey. We use full-spectrum fitting to obtain precision measurements of the clusters' kinematics, metallicities, and $\alpha$ abundances. We identify three populations of clusters from our measurements: young stellar clusters (~100 Myr) with broad Brackett absorption features; intermediate-age clusters (1-2 Gyr) that are metal-rich ([M/H]=-0.5 to +0.3) with roughly solar $\alpha$ abundance; and old (>5 Gyr), kinematically-hot and metal-poor GCs. These old GCs exhibit a broad range of kinematics (RV~-250 to -8 km s$^{-1}$), metallicities ([M/H]=-1.8 to -0.6) and $\alpha$ abundances ([$\alpha$/M]=+0.05 to +0.3), and we identify and discuss three GCs that show strong chemodynamical evidence of accretion into M33's halo. M33's GCs at [M/H]~-1.2 have a spread in [$\alpha$/M] of nearly 0.2 dex. This pattern is strikingly similar to the abundance distribution of Milky Way GCs, and indicates either that M33 and the Milky Way formed clusters similarly at early times, or that M33 hosts a significant population of accreted clusters in its halo.

The Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey (NGVS). XXIX. Demography of Nuclear Star Clusters

Max M. Kurzner, Patrick Côté, Laura Ferrarese, Chelsea Spengler, Eric W. Peng +13

47 pages, 27 figures, 3 tables, Accepted to ApJ

PDFTue 20 Oct
Nuclear star clusters (NSCs) provide insights into processes shaping galaxy cores. We use deep $u^*giz$ CFHT imaging from the Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey (NGVS) to study NSCs in Virgo cluster galaxies. Our parent sample consists of 3680 certain, likely or possible cluster members spanning more than six orders of magnitude in stellar mass. Using a self-consistent classification framework, we identify 1144 candidate nucleated galaxies: 804 high-confidence NSCs and 340 lower-confidence candidates. This is the largest and most uniform NSC catalog for any environment studied to date. We explore the nucleation fraction, $f_{\rm nuc}$, as a function of stellar mass, morphology and environment. Consistent with previous studies, we find stellar mass a key factor driving nucleation probability. However, host-galaxy morphology is also important, with the peak of the $f_{\rm nuc}$ distribution occurring at progressively lower masses as one moves from later to earlier morphological types. We interpret this shift as evidence that the more extended structures of late-type galaxies reduce the efficiency of GC inspiral at fixed stellar mass, while gas-rich secular processes and central star formation may contribute increasingly to NSC growth at higher masses. Environment plays a smaller role, with only a slight enhancement in high-density regions. We characterize the nonlinear relationship between NSC stellar mass and host-galaxy mass, and assess the role of selection effects. We conclude that NSC formation is best explained by a hybrid scenario, with inspiraling star clusters and in situ star formation contributing, and their relative importance dictated primarily by galaxy mass.

The Impact of an AGN on PAH Spectral Features in Galaxy Evolution Simulations: Continuum Dilution and Excitation of PAHs

Grant P. Donnelly, Desika Narayanan, Paul Torrey, J. -D. T. Smith, Helena M. Richie +2

Submitted to ApJ. 13 pages, 8 figures

PDFTue 20 Oct
Galaxies hosting an active galactic nucleus (AGN) show diminished power and non-standard spectral feature ratios from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) compared with normal star-forming galaxies. Proposed mechanisms for this difference include a combination of AGN-driven PAH grain processing, differential heating from the surrounding radiation field, and continuum dilution. These potentially competing factors are difficult to disentangle observationally. In this paper, we investigate the impact of the AGN radiation field on the excitation properties and potential dilution of the mid-IR emission features via constrained numerical experiments of idealized galaxies in simulations that include a self-consistent model for the evolution of dust. We find that, for the integrated spectrum of our simulated Milky Way analog and independent of grain-processing physics, the apparent PAH emission is progressively diminished as AGN luminosity is increased. For spectra within the central half kiloparsec, PAH features are entirely obfuscated when an AGN of any sampled luminosity is present. We additionally find that the UV radiation from an AGN is capable of impacting PAH band ratios, though note that we do not yet include a model for potential photodestruction of PAHs. Our model suggests that the inferred lack of PAHs via featureless mid-IR spectra from galaxies hosting AGN may simply owe to the direct continuum from the AGN overpowering mid-IR features from PAHs in the ISM of the host galaxy. Future work will investigate the relative role of AGN continuum dilution and PAH heating vs photodestruction/ionization of PAHs.

Granulation in Color: The Chromatic Stellar Flicker of LTT-9779 and WASP-121

William C. Waalkes, Nadiia Kostogryz, Valeriy Vasilyev, Rafael Luque, Alexander Shapiro +7

14 pages, 8 figures. Submitted to A&A

PDFTue 20 Oct
Granulation is a persistent feature of stars with outer convective layers and is the photospheric signature of near-surface convective energy transport. In disk-integrated observations, it produces stochastic brightness variations that contribute substantially to stellar variability on timescales of minutes to hours. High-precision, observations with JWST provide an opportunity to study the chromatic signatures of stellar granulation. This work aims to measure the amplitude and characteristic timescale of granulation-induced variability for solar-type stars and to investigate whether the granulation signal shows significant chromatic variation in the near-infrared. We accessed publicly available JWST/NIRISS SOSS time-series observations of LTT-9779 (G7V) and WASP-121 (F6V) and de-trended the light curves by fitting the transits, eclipses, and planetary phase curves. We computed power spectra from the de-trended light curves and fit them with a Harvey granulation profile. We first analyzed the broadband light curves to measure and fix the granulation timescale and then repeated the power-spectrum analysis in individual wavelength channels to measure the chromatic dependence of the granulation amplitude. For LTT-9779, we measure a broadband granulation amplitude of σ$=53.8^{+4.1}_{-4.0}$ppm at a timescale of τ$=4.8^{+1.5}_{-1.3}$min. For WASP-121, we measure σ$=59.3\pm4.3$ppm and τ$=6.3^{+2.1}_{-1.8}$min. We also measure peak oscillation frequencies of ν$=3382^{+166}_{-191}\mu$Hz and $1649^{+55}_{-69}\mu$Hz in the bandpass-integrated power spectra of LTT-9779 and WASP-121, respectively. In the spectroscopic fits, we find $\sigma$ decreases with wavelength. Our results demonstrate a new observational window on short-timescale stellar variability and provides a way to characterize chromatic stellar noise in transit light curves.

Reading Between the Lines: How Much Information Remains After Fitting a Stellar Spectrum?

Sven Buder, Tomasz Różánski

21 pages, 14 figures; submitted to the Open Journal of Astrophysics; code at this https URL comments welcome

PDFTue 20 Oct
What if we could model 100% of a stellar spectrum? Modern surveys infer stellar properties by comparing observed spectra with synthetic models, yet even the best fits leave structured residuals. We analyse 7087 GALAH survey spectra and ask what information remains after subtracting each best-fitting model. Across 13927 pixels, 4.5% of residuals exceed $3\sigma$, far above 0.3% expected from Gaussian noise. We map the amplitude, significance, sign, and recurrence of these residuals and identify 380 significant regions. Comparison with stellar atlases, line lists, diffuse interstellar bands, and sky diagnostics links most regions to plausible causes: 62% are candidate line-strength or line-profile mismatches, while others trace missing or unidentified lines, interstellar absorption, sky emission, or broader reduction structure. We then train data-driven models on the residual spectra themselves. Residual-only models recover $T_\mathrm{eff}$ to 80-150 K and [Fe/H] to 0.059-0.086 dex, depending on sample quality, and retain weaker, partly indirect information about elemental abundances. Extinction-related coefficients recover the strongest known interstellar features, whereas chromospheric activity leaves plausible Balmer-line signatures but is not recovered robustly out of sample. Most strikingly, the residual flux in 6% of wavelength pixels that were synthesised but excluded from the GALAH DR4 optimisation recovers stellar labels almost as well as the full residual spectrum and better than the remaining 94% alone. Residuals are therefore not merely imperfections to discard: they map missing line data, incomplete physics, unfitted astrophysical components, and observational systematics. Reading between the lines turns one spectroscopist's trash into another one's treasure, and provides a practical route towards extracting more of the information already present in stellar spectra.

PDFTue 20 Oct
We revisit EBG-1, a gravitationally lensed galaxy at $z = 9.244$, using newly obtained deep (14 hr) JWST/NIRSpec G235M ($R \sim 1000$) spectroscopy. EBG-1 is one of the bluest galaxies observed at $z > 9$. Along with its remarkably blue continuum of $\beta = -2.99 \pm 0.15$, we find very weak nebular emission from EBG-1. Ziwen Zhang, Cheng Jia, Huiyuan Wang, Enci Wang, Yangyao Chen +4

21 pages, 12 figures, 4 tables

PDFTue 20 Oct
The connection between galaxy structure, star formation activity, and large-scale environments provides important insights into the evolution of massive galaxies. However, the relative importance of different galaxy properties in predicting quenching and the environmental dependence of structurally distinct galaxy populations remain poorly constrained at cosmic noon ($1\leq z<3$). In this work, we investigate massive galaxies with $\log M_*/{\rm M}_{\odot}>10.5$ at $1\leq z<3$ using deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from the COSMOS-Web survey, combining measurements of central stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_1$), star formation activity, and galaxy clustering. Yujiao Yang, Maosheng Xiang, Yang Huang, Meng Zhang, Lanya Mou +2

19 pages, 10 figures, 2 tables. Accepted for publication in ApJ

PDFTue 20 Oct
Dwarf galaxies serve as fossils for understanding the early star formation history of the local Universe. In this work, we use stellar abundances for individual stars derived from the first data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1) to investigate the star formation history of the dwarf spheroidal galaxy Draco. We simultaneously fit the stellar metallicity distribution function (MDF) and the [Mg/Fe]-[Fe/H] trend using the galactic chemical evolution (GCE) model flexCE. Our best-fit models imply a low star formation efficiency (SFE) of $0.003$ to $0.007\,\mathrm{Gyr}^{-1}$, which is about ten times lower than values reported in the literature, but consistent with recent SFE estimates for ultra-faint dwarf galaxies. By combining recent literature measurements, we find that the SFEs of Milky Way dwarf galaxies scale with stellar mass as $\mathrm{SFE}\propto M_\star^{0.24}$. This is well in line with the observed outflow-stellar mass relation and with the Feedback in Realistic Environment (FIRE) cosmological simulations. Our results demonstrate the power of large-scale, low-to-medium resolution stellar spectroscopic surveys to deliver robust constraints on the early formation history of galaxies in the local Universe.

Gravity-mode main-sequence asteroseismology of 16 open clusters and associations

Gang Li, Haotian Wang, Anuj Gautam, J. S. G. Mombarg, Simon J. Murphy +9

26 pages, 16 figures, 2 tables. Accepted for publication in ApJS

PDFTue 20 Oct
Coeval stellar populations offer a unique advantage for studying stellar physics because their members share a common age and broadly similar initial chemical composition, allowing the free parameters in stellar models to be constrained more efficiently. Meanwhile, asteroseismology, through the study of pulsation modes that propagate through stellar interiors, provides powerful constraints on internal structure and the physical processes operating beneath stellar atmospheres. The combination of coeval-population studies and asteroseismology therefore provides an unparalleled laboratory for testing and improving stellar evolutionary and structural models. Using light curves from the Kepler and TESS space missions, together with membership constraints based on Gaia astrometry, we identify g-mode period-spacing patterns in dozens of early-type ($T_{\rm eff} \gtrsim 7000\,\mathrm{K}$) main-sequence stars across 16 stellar groups. From these patterns, we infer near-core rotation rates $f_\mathrm{rot}$ and measure the asymptotic spacing $\Pi_0$ of g modes, tracing both angular-momentum transport and the evolution of the convective stellar cores. Using our homogeneous set of g-mode stellar-group members spanning a wide range of ages, we show that near-core rotation in early-type stars decreases with stellar evolution. We compare our results with existing model predictions. Our $\Pi_0$ measurements still show discrepancies with current stellar models for reasons that remain unclear, as reported in several previous studies. We also report two g-mode pre-main-sequence pulsators and three pulsating blue stragglers. Our sample provides a useful basis for calibrating stellar models in future ensemble asteroseismic studies of coeval stellar populations.

PDFTue 20 Oct
Galaxy morphology is a fundamental tracer of galaxy assembly, but its characterization becomes increasingly uncertain in the early Universe. We investigate morphological classifications across different methodologies over the redshift range $0.6 < z < 8.0$, using JWST observations of 15,567 galaxies with 6,099 synthetic galaxies from IllustrisTNG. To quantify morphology without relying on visual labels or prescribed light profiles, we use an unsupervised-supervised hybrid machine-learning approach based on the non-parametric MEGG system, which combines the second moment of the light distribution, Entropy, Gini, and Gradient Pattern Analysis. Consistently Reproducing Early Galaxy Observations and 21-cm Signal during the Epoch of Reionization

Michele Bianco, Sambit K. Giri, Arghyadeep Basu\orcidlink, Tommaso Boschi, Aurel Schneider +2

17 pages, 12 figures, 2 tables

PDFTue 20 Oct
In the standard cosmological model, galaxies forming within dark matter haloes are the primary drivers of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR). The complex physics of galaxy formation introduces scatter in the relation between halo mass and star-formation activity, with implications for the production and escape of ionising photons. In this work, we implement a stochastic source model in our cosmological radiative transfer simulations. We calibrate our model against the high-redshift ultraviolet luminosity function observed by JWST and observational constraints on Lyman-continuum leakage to constrain the ionising escape fraction. We find that stochastic star formation redistributes ionising emissivity toward lower-mass haloes, producing a more fragmented ionisation topology and delaying the growth of the largest ionised regions relative to the deterministic, observationally calibrated model. However, when the two models match total ionising photon production, their global neutral fractions and 21-cm power spectrum remain nearly indistinguishable, despite small differences in the morphology of individual ionised regions. We find that these prescriptions produce distinct ionisation topologies and 21-cm statistical signatures, even when their global reionisation histories are similar. Our results highlight the importance of combining physically motivated galaxy formation prescriptions from hydrodynamical simulations with large-volume radiative transfer simulations to interpret forthcoming 21-cm observations and constrain the sources of cosmic reionisation.

Statistical Strong Lensing V. Inference with Microlensed Quasars

Shuo Zhang, Alessandro Sonnenfeld

12 pages, 5 figures. Submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFTue 20 Oct
Microlensing of strongly lensed quasars provides direct information on the stellar component of lens galaxies, and can help break the degeneracy between the stellar initial mass function (IMF) and the dark matter. However, a statistical framework to forward model microlensing data while accounting for strong lensing selection effects is still missing. We incorporated microlensing effects into the hierarchical model of Paper III, and investigated to what extent flux measurements of strongly lensed quasars can break the degeneracy between the stellar IMF and the dark matter distribution. We tested the method on a mock sample of 219 doubly imaged quasars, lensed by circularly symmetric lenses. When microlensing effects are modelled, the inference using image fluxes recovers the input foreground population parameters and provides tighter constraints than the inference without flux constraints. The improvement is clearest in the $\mu_\alpha-\mu_5$ plane, where $\mu_\alpha$ describes the mean stellar population synthesis mismatch parameter $\log\alpha_{\rm SPS}$ and $\mu_5$ describes the mean projected dark matter mass within $5\,{\rm kpc}$. The 68% credible interval on $\mu_\alpha$ shrinks from 0.16 dex to 0.09 dex, with the degeneracy between $\mu_\alpha$ and $\mu_5$ significantly reduced. By contrast, fitting the image fluxes without accounting for microlensing leads to large biases on the inferred parameters. This work is a first step towards an accurate modelling of microlensing data. Application to real lens samples will require dropping the circular symmetry assumption and adopting more realistic models for the microlensing magnification.

Quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) in the X-ray emission of accreting black hole binaries are commonly attributed to the relativistic Lense–Thirring precession of the inner accretion flow, in which a hot inner flow, tilted with respect to the black hole spin axis, precesses as a solid body while a cooler outer disc remains at its own, different tilt – a warped, Bardeen–Petterson-type geometry. Testing this picture requires a radiative-transfer model that self-consistently follows photons through such a system: from emission near the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO), through Compton scattering in a hot corona, to possible reflection off the misaligned outer disc, all under the full effects of light bending in Kerr spacetime. In this first paper of a two-part series, we present a general-relativistic Monte Carlo (GRMC) radiative-transfer model of a misaligned, warped, two-zone accretion system around a Kerr black hole. We describe the disc–corona geometry, the seed-photon prescription, the photon-transport engine – including the geodesic integration scheme, the coronal Compton-scattering module and the outer-disc reflection treatment – and the observer pipeline used to construct energy spectra, photon path-length and arrival-time distributions. We present the resulting energy spectra, light curves and spectral lags as functions of the black hole mass, corona size, outer-disc geometry, reflection albedo, photon-injection radius and observer inclination, and show how gravitational light bending systematically lengthens the path-length and arrival-time distributions of the escaping radiation. This model and its spectral-timing predictions form the foundation for Paper II, which extends the framework to track the full photon polarization state and presents the resulting energy- and precession-phase-resolved polarimetric signatures.

Accurate knowledge of radio-frequency (RF) losses in front of superconductor–insulator–superconductor (SIS) mixers is essential for optimizing millimeter- and terahertz heterodyne receivers. However, such losses are difficult to extract from conventional hot/cold-load measurements because the measured receiver noise combines contributions from the input optics, the mixer, and the intermediate-frequency chain. In this work, we present a numerical implementation of the straight-line, or intersecting-lines, method for estimating input-referred RF noise and optical loss from standard Y-factor measurements. Hot/cold IF spectra are acquired while sweeping the local oscillator (LO) pumping level from under-pumped to over-pumped operation, so that each IF frequency bin gives a family of linear relations between receiver output power and input load temperature. We evaluate all pairwise intersections between these lines and analyze the crossing cloud to obtain the effective RF input noise. We repeat the analysis across the IF band and multiple LO tunings which provides a frequency-resolved characterisation of the input losses over the full band. Our implementation turns the straight-line technique into a reproducible framework for receiver characterisation and relative optical-loss comparison in low-loss front-end configurations.

We investigate the effects of stochastic heating, magnetic inhomogeneity, and Coulomb collisions in coronal loops using a reduced kinetic model. Coulomb collisions thermalize suprathermal populations generated by intermittent heating and populate velocity-space regions inaccessible from the lower boundary in the collisionless limit. We consider two regimes: when heating varies faster than the electron crossing time, the plasma develops a multitemperature nonthermal distribution; when it varies more slowly than the proton crossing time, individual heated loops approach an isothermal state. In the collisionless limit, conservation of the magnetic moment restricts the accessible velocity space, producing a strong temperature anisotropy, with parallel temperature exceeding perpendicular temperature, and additional density depletion. We find that Coulomb pitch-angle scattering efficiently suppresses these effects under typical coronal conditions. In the short-time-scale regime, density and temperature profiles approach those obtained with a constant magnetic field, while gravitational and collisional filtering sustain the temperature inversion. In the strongly collisional long-time-scale regime, magnetic-field-induced anisotropy is nearly eliminated, and individual loops remain approximately isotropic and isothermal. An increasing temperature profile then emerges only after averaging over unresolved flux tubes with different temperatures. Thus, Coulomb collisions largely suppress the macroscopic kinetic signatures of magnetic-field inhomogeneity in the transition region and low corona.

We investigate the sensitivity of direct-Urca neutrino emission in hyperonic neutron-star matter to the structure and momentum dependence of baryonic transition form factors. Using the zero-temperature FSU2H relativistic mean-field equation of state, we derive the relativistic emissivity for general spin-$1/2$ baryon transitions with the complete six form-factor contribution. We compare conventional static flavor-SU(3) couplings with vacuum lattice-QCD-informed form factors, using direct lattice vacuum input for $n\rightarrow p$ and $\Lambda\rightarrow p$ and a hybrid construction for the remaining hyperonic channels. Evaluating the latter both at $q^2=0$ and at the kinetic momentum transfer sampled at the Fermi surfaces separates changes in the zero-momentum couplings from momentum-transfer effects. The sensitivity is strongly channel dependent. The nucleonic transition probes $q^2\simeq0$ and is only weakly affected, whereas hyperonic channels can sample substantially larger momentum transfers and receive correspondingly larger corrections. These modifications propagate to the integrated neutrino luminosity of stellar configurations. Our results estimate the possible scale of the sensitivity coming from momentum transfer dependence, absent from static-coupling treatments and motivate future first-principles studies of baryonic weak currents in dense matter.

We report the design and cryogenic characterisation of a broadband superconducting 90deg intermediate-frequency (IF) hybrid covering 4 to 20 GHz for sideband-separating (2SB) heterodyne receivers. The hybrid is implemented in thin-film niobium using capacitively loaded edge coupled transmission lines, avoiding the air-bridge interconnections required in Lange-coupler layouts while retaining a compact superconducting implementation with low loss and good quadrature balance. Cryogenic measurements using Vector Network Analyser (VNA) show amplitude and phase imbalance better than 0.6 dB and 6deg, respectively, across the full band. When used in a 2SB receiver, this level of IF balance is consistent with the observed image-rejection performance in the 10-20 dB range, for the full receiver. The design supports the development of wide-instantaneous-bandwidth 2SB receivers for millimeter and submillimeter observatories, improving spectral grasp for broadband line surveys and high-redshift line searches.

Mean-Field Backreaction in Single-Field Inflation

Matteo Braglia, Gabriele Franciolini, Nadir Ijaz, Marco Peloso

The validity of linear perturbation theory and the role of backreaction in single-field inflationary models that enhance power at small scales have recently been under debate. We investigate scalar backreaction semi-analytically in a mean-field approach within the decoupling limit, considering popular models with a transient ultra-slow-roll (USR) stage or an oscillatory feature (OF) in the potential. We find that ultraviolet (UV) modes in the Bunch–Davies vacuum can substantially affect backreaction estimates unless removed by adiabatic subtraction. Our mean-field treatment can reproduce recent lattice results, but reveals a spurious sensitivity to UV contributions in some cases. This sensitivity is reduced in the OF model when copious particle production separates the amplified physical modes from the UV vacuum tail, making lattice results more reliable in this regime. In USR, instead, adiabatically subtracted spectra yield milder backreaction on the background evolution than previously inferred.

Understanding the formation of alpha-Fe bimodality in the Milky Way using constrained cosmological simulations of the Local Group

Anna Genina, Ewoud Wempe, Amina Helmi, Hanneke C. Woudenberg, Simon D. M. White +4

25 pages, 18 figures, submitted to MNRAS

PDFWed 21 Oct
We use a novel suite of constrained cosmological simulations of the Local Group to investigate the formation of alpha–Fe bimodalities in stellar discs of Milky Way analogues. In agreement with past studies using the Auriga model, we show that in our simulations the high-alpha sequence forms in a merger-induced starburst, which further quenches star formation, allowing the new stellar populations to form at low [alpha/Fe]. Producing a bimodality that is prominent at large radii, with a significant [alpha/Fe] gap and overlapping metallicity as in the Milky Way, requires the quenching of star formation across all radii where the bimodality is observed. The quenching can be achieved either with a galaxy-wide starburst, or with a retrograde merger reducing angular momentum in the outer disc and allowing more efficient dilution by the metal-poor gas in the circumgalactic medium (CGM). We show that mergers can also tilt the disc, changing the direction of galactic outflows and enriching a larger volume of the CGM with galactic metals, leading to low [alpha/Fe] scatter in the subsequently formed low-alpha sequence. We argue that the slopes in [alpha/Fe] of the two alpha sequences are crucial in narrowing down the mechanism that leads to their formation. We additionally uncover an indirect role that mergers play in enhancing the accretion of metal-poor gas onto galaxies. The smoothly accreted gas can dominate the gas content in the CGM and aid in the formation of distinct chemical sequences, especially at the low-metallicity end.

A $\wedge$-like metallicity profile in the disk of the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy

Zixi Guo, Xiang-Xiang Xue, Jiang Chang, Chao Liu, Zehao Zhong +10

48 pages,29 figures, 2 tables. Nature Astronomy published

PDFWed 21 Oct
Unveiling the properties of the progenitors that formed the tidal structures of the Milky Way (MW) is essential for exploring the MW's assembly history. Such studies further afford a unique opportunity to dissect the formation and evolution of dwarf galaxies by contrasting surviving and disrupted systems. However, reconstructing the intrinsic structure of progenitors solely from the positions and velocities of their tidal debris remains a formidable challenge. Here, we decode the chemo-kinematic signatures embedded in the stellar streams of the Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf galaxy through N-body simulations. Our simulation reproduces a vertical metallicity gradient in the Sgr streams. We show that this feature is naturally explained if the stream originated from a disk component with a $\wedge$-like metallicity profile. Remarkably, similar metallicity profiles are identified in MaNGA survey data for a few (3/217) field dwarf galaxies, suggesting this feature is rare but not unique to Sgr. The vertical metallicity gradient of stellar streams preserves extra detailed galactic fossil records, enabling quantitative reconstruction of the peculiar structure of the satellite galaxy. The similar metallicity profiles of Sgr and MW raise the question of a possible special coevolution.

Probing the Optical/UV Continuum Variability of Active Galactic Nuclei with the Damped Random Walk Model

Xiang-Wei Ning, Wen-Jie Hu, Zi-Han Yuan, Yi Tang, Yan-Song Ma +3

22 pages, 9 figures, Accepted for publication in ApJ

PDFWed 21 Oct
Optical/UV continuum variability over baselines of up to 20 yr is investigated using the damped random walk (DRW) model for 1214 active galactic nuclei (AGNs) compiled from Y. Shen et al. and C. J. Burke et al. Among these, 115 AGNs with reliable DRW fitting and unbiased DRW parameters (timescale $\tau_{2500}$ and amplitude $\sigma_{2500}$ at the rest-frame 2500~Å) are used to examine the relations between the variability and the accretion properties. We found a significant correlation of $\tau_{2500}$ with black hole mass ($M_{\rm BH}$) and dimensionless accretion rate ($\dot{\mathscr{M}}$) of the form $\tau_{2500} \propto M_{\rm BH}^{0.50\pm0.07} \dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.27\pm0.04}$, while the dependence on the continuum luminosity at 5100~Å and redshift becomes insignificant after controlling for $M_{\rm BH}$. $\tau_{2500}$ also correlate with the mass accretion rate ($\dot{M}$) as $\tau_{2500} \propto \dot{M}^{0.41\pm0.04}$, which is consistent with the thermal timescale expected from the standard thin-disk model within $3\sigma$ uncertainties; while the average value is consistent with the expectations of the model, the scatter in the relation can be due to differences in the viscosity parameter ($\alpha$) or in disk temperature profiles. Assuming that \tauuv corresponds to the accretion disk thermal timescale, the disk-emitting radius at rest-frame 2500~Å ($R_{2500}$) is found to correlate with $M_{\rm BH}$ as $R_{2500} \propto M_{\rm BH}^{0.53\pm0.05}$. Using the microlensing-based $R_{2500}-M_{\rm BH}$ relation of C. W. Morgan et al., $\alpha$ is statistically estimated to be $0.04\pm0.01$.

Slowly Rotating Fully Convective Stars at $\lesssim$ 1 Gyr: Evidence from the Hyades and Praesepe

Yuxi Lu, Marc H. Pinsonneault, José G. Fernández-Trincado, Richard R. Lane, Alexandre Roman-Lopes +3

Data file included in .zip file

PDFWed 21 Oct
We report the discovery of 10 stars in the Praesepe and Hyades clusters with unusually slow rotation that are likely not field contamination. Even though our search was general, all slow rotators are in the fully convective domain. We find that the fraction of slow rotators in the clusters is more than $5\sigma$ above the distribution expected from field contamination. They provide a potential link between the period distribution of the young open clusters and the field. These stars could be a distinct birth population, or they could be an indication that the onset of strong magnetic breaking is earlier than previously realized in this domain. Photometric variability and X-ray luminosity suggest the later but more data is needed to confirm. Searches for similar populations in younger clusters will help determine when slow rotators first emerge and constrain the timescale and physical mechanisms governing angular-momentum loss in fully convective stars. The discovery of these stars also highlights the importance of long-baseline photometric observations for detecting the long rotation periods of low-mass stars.

The chemical evolution of the LMC from APOGEE abundances: a hybrid two-infall OMEGA+ analysis

Erik Gasteiger, Viola Hegedűs, Szabolcs Mészáros

Accepted for publication in A&A, 8 pages, 5 figures, 1 table

PDFWed 21 Oct
The Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) provides resolved stellar abundances and star-formation histories that can be used together to test models of dwarf-galaxy chemical evolution. We investigated if the non-monotonic APOGEE $[\mathrm{Mg}/\mathrm{Fe}]$-$[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]$ sequence can be explained by a hybrid history that combines a second gas-infall episode with a temporary increase in star-formation efficiency (SFE). We selected 4933 LMC red-giant-branch stars from APOGEE-2 DR17. We fitted an adaptively binned median Mg sequence with the two-zone open-box code OMEGA+. The formal fitting objective contained only the Mg sequence. The metallicity distribution function (MDF), one-dimensional abundance distributions, star-formation history (SFH), present-day mass partition, SFR, and supernova (SN) rate were retained as posterior diagnostics. The best-fitting model reproduces the Mg sequence with an RMS residual of 0.01 dex and also gives a close posterior match to O. It contains two delayed-tau infall episodes, with the second beginning at $t_2=10.39$ Gyr and with integrated mass ratio $M_2/M_1=0.159$, followed by a temporary Gaussian SFE enhancement centred at $t=11.91$ Gyr. The second infall is represented by a mixture of enriched and primordial gas. The model reproduces the timing of the late SFH enhancement and yields a present SFR within the adopted comparison range. A hybrid second-infall and SFE-enhancement history is a plausible scenario for reproducing the observed LMC Mg median sequence. Our fit explains the LMC evolution fairly well, but the remaining small MDF, mass, and SN-rate discrepancies suggest that a selection-function-aware treatment of the stellar distributions is required for a stricter comparison.

KMOS Spectroscopic Follow-up of 16 Star Cluster Candidates of the Sagittarius Dwarf Galaxy

C. O. Obasi, J. G. Fernández-Trincado, D. Minniti, M. Gómez, E. R. Garro +30

13 pages, 7 Figures

PDFWed 21 Oct
The Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf spheroidal galaxy hosts several known globular clusters and numerous additional cluster candidates identified toward its crowded central regions. Establishing the nature of these candidates is difficult because apparent spatial and astrometric structure can arise from the underlying Sgr population. We investigate sixteen Minni stellar-cluster candidates with KMOS spectroscopy to determine whether the observed stars are compatible with Sgr and whether any candidate field shows evidence for a distinct stellar system. We analyse H-band KMOS spectra of 341 targets from the VVVX-GalCen Spectroscopic Survey, deriving radial velocities (RVs) and empirical metallicities. We compare these with an independent Sgr spectroscopic sample and use VVVX photometry and Gaia DR3 astrometry to test for localized stellar excesses relative to local control fields. Of the 341 KMOS targets, 256 (75%) have RVs within the adopted Sgr interval, and their metallicities broadly overlap the Sgr comparison population. No field shows statistically compelling kinematic evidence for a distinct system after accounting for the multiple candidates examined. Minni 329 shows the strongest spatial excess, but this is not significant after multiple-testing correction and is not accompanied by significant central concentration. The KMOS targets predominantly trace the Sgr population, but Sgr compatibility alone does not confirm the individual candidates as distinct stellar clusters. None of the sixteen fields can be confirmed as a separate stellar system with the present data, although this does not establish that the original candidates are spurious. Deeper imaging and larger spectroscopic samples are required to determine their physical nature.

Orbital Parameters for 65 Wide Binary Systems: no evidence for gravitational anomaly at low acceleration

L. Pasquini, R. Saglia, F. Patat, L. Berni, D. Bossini +6

anbstract abridged

PDFWed 21 Oct
We use a Monte Carlo analysis to study whether wide binaries can be represented by bound Newtonian orbits, and, if this is the case, whether the distribution of the orbital parameters agrees with expectations. The potential orbits are classified according to the $\chi^2$ probability of representing star coordinates, parallaxes, velocity components and masses. We apply this analysis to 44 systems previously published, to which we add 26 wide binaries, consisting of twin low-mass (M) stars. We find orbital solutions for 65 systems, and derive the distribution of their orbital parameters. For one system composed of M-type stars, no Newtonian bound solution can be found; however, this pair is a member of the young Argus association and therefore not a suitable target. The remaining four systems that cannot be fit were already identified as likely disrupted binaries. The uncertainty in the separation between the components, given by the parallax error, is by far the dominant error source, and the main limitation of the present analysis, affecting especially the determination of the orbits' inclination angle and phase. Newtonian dynamics describe well our sample. When dividing the sample into two subsamples, according to the mutual acceleration, the eccentricity and periastron distributions agree with theoretical expectations; inclination and phase are distorted by the parallax-limited separation, more strongly in the high-acceleration (37 systems) than in the low-acceleration ($A_N < 10^{-9} m s^{-2}$, 28 systems) subsample, which is opposite to what a gravitational signal would produce. In particular the ratio between observed velocity difference and circular velocity does not show any excess for the low acceleration sample. When parametrized as a boost $\gamma$ parameter to the velocity, $0.95 < \gamma <1.05 (1 \sigma)$, fully consistent with Newton also for the low acceleration sample.

Inferring the shapes of low-mass galaxies at $z>6$ from FIREbox$^{HR}$. Where are the disks?

Haojie Geng, Robert Feldmann, Jorge Moreno, Xuejian Shen, James Bullock

22 pages, 13 figures

PDFWed 21 Oct
Context. The three-dimensional shapes of galaxies provide clues to their formation and evolution. Inferring intrinsic shapes from projected sizes and axis ratios requires assumptions about the population distribution of shape parameters. Aims. We test commonly adopted assumptions in Bayesian shape inference for high-redshift galaxies. Methods. We analyze FIREboxHR galaxies with $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=6.5-9.5$ at $z\sim6-15$. We measure intrinsic shapes from stellar particles using the minimum covariance determinant (MCD) estimator. We also create mock JWST/NIRCam images, measure half-light radii and axis ratios, and infer intrinsic distributions from the joint distribution of projected axis ratio $q$ and size $\log a$. Results. Low-mass galaxies are predominantly prolate, with a substantial spheroidal component. Intrinsic ellipticity $E$ and triaxiality $T$ are well described by Johnson SB distributions, while intrinsic size $A$ is approximately log-normal. We therefore model $(\mathrm{logit}(E),\mathrm{logit}(T),\log A)$ with a multivariate normal distribution and allow general correlations. This model recovers the particle-measured shape distributions more faithfully than a multivariate normal model in $(E,T,\log A)$. Triaxiality-related parameters remain difficult to constrain from projected data without large samples or informative priors. Conclusions. Bayesian forward modeling can recover intrinsic shape distributions when its population model adequately describes the underlying galaxies. An unsuitable distribution can bias inferences about morphological evolution at high redshift.

The ALMA REBELS Survey: The [C II] Luminosity Function of UV-Selected Galaxies at $z\sim7$

Thomas Herard-Demanche, Pascal A. Oesch, Rychard Bouwens, Jacqueline A. Hodge, Hiddo S. B. Algera +17

14 pages, 8 figures, 3 tables. Accepted for publication in MNRAS

PDFWed 21 Oct
Luminosity functions (LF) are some of the most basic quantities providing insights into galaxy evolution across cosmic time and enabling a direct comparison between observations and simulations. Here, we present the first [CII]158$\mu$m LF at $z\sim7$ from a sizeable UV-selected ALMA sample. Our analysis makes use of 27 galaxies with significant [CII] emission line detections in the epoch of reionization from the REBELS (Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey) ALMA Large Program and its pilot programs. We used these line detections and similar from ALPINE to calibrate UV luminosity-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ and stellar mass-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ relations at $z\sim7$. Given the challenge in precisely defining a selection volume for the REBELS sample itself, we rely on published UV LF and stellar mass function (SMF) determinations at $z\sim7$ and three complementary approaches to derive the [CII] LF. The UV LF-based method provides a lower limit on the [CII] LF, due to its missing the most dust-obscured systems. Our SMF-based estimate yields a Schechter function with a characteristic luminosity of $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}=10^{9.11^{+0.11}_{-0.27}} L_\odot$ and a steep faint-end slope. From $z\sim7$ to $z\sim5$, our inferred [CII] LF shows an evolution of +0.3 dex in normalisation and +0.1 dex in $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$. Our results indicate a general underprediction from models of the abundance of [CII] emitters at high-redshift and thus potentially the number densities of massive yet UV-faint systems implied by our SMF-based method. Additionally, they suggest that 63% of the star formation rate density occurs in massive [CII]-bright systems and that $\sim$60-90% of [CII] emitters at $z\sim7$ may be missed by bright UV selections. This highlights the importance of surveys focusing on especially massive, UV-faint sources at $z>6$.

Data-Driven Ages from Asteroseismology for FGK Dwarfs

Christopher Lam, Aida Behmard, Sarah Ballard, Andrew R. Casey, Jamie N. Tayar +1

Submitted to ApJ, currently in review

PDFWed 21 Oct
Age is the one of the most difficult stellar properties to constrain. However, upcoming surveys such as PLATO are about to revolutionize our ability to measure FGK dwarf ages with asteroseismology. Here, we use The Cannon to construct a data-driven model for inferring asteroseismic-quality age measurements from large spectroscopic survey observations and demonstrate that forthcoming asteroseismology datasets from future surveys can be leveraged, together with our model, to obtain stellar age samples larger than can be obtained by those surveys alone. We train The Cannon on 323 FGK dwarf stars with asteroseismic age labels from NASA Kepler photometry and high-resolution spectra from the Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment (APOGEE). Leviathan: a very massive, extended sub-millimetre galaxy spectroscopically confirmed at $\rm z = 4.05$

William M. Baker, Francesco Valentino, Jens Hjorth, Gabriel Brammer, Ugnė Dudzevičiūtė +3

4 pages, 3 figures, submitted as a A&A letter

PDFWed 21 Oct
We present Leviathan, a sub-millimetre galaxy (SMG) spectroscopically confirmed at $z=4.051$ with JWST/NIRSpec. Modelling 31 bands of photometry from the rest-frame ultraviolet to the millimetre, we find a stellar mass of $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)=11.34^{+0.15}_{-0.22}$, making Leviathan one of the most massive spectroscopically confirmed galaxies at $z>4$, yet consistent with $\Lambda$CDM for a star-formation efficiency of approximately 20%. Although SMGs are often thought to be building the dense cores of compact quiescent galaxies, Leviathan is extended, with a rest-frame $0.9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ half-light radius of 4.3 kpc. Spatially resolved SED fitting gives a half-mass radius of $3.1\pm0.4$ kpc, consistent with its size in rest-frame $1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ imaging. This is three times larger than quiescent galaxies of the same mass, but consistent with the size-mass relation of star-forming galaxies at $4<z<5$ extrapolated to $M_\star=10^{11.3}M_\odot$. Leviathan forms stars on the main sequence (approximately $400\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$) with dust spread across its disc. Both its stellar mass and dust are more extended than those of $z\approx3$ SMGs. Alongside GN20.2b, which has a similar mass and star-formation rate but is three times smaller, these show that massive dusty galaxies at $z\approx4$ can have very different stellar structures, and that not all have their stellar mass concentrated in a compact core.

The clumpy fraction of galaxies at $z=1-9$ with JADES: evidence for a mass-dependent transition in clump origin

Dávid Puskás, Sandro Tacchella, Phoebe Chapman, Floor van Donkelaar, Andrew J. Bunker +19

34 pages, 22 figures, 4 tables. Submitted to MNRAS

PDFWed 21 Oct
We measure the fraction of clumpy galaxies ($f_{\rm clumpy}$) over the redshift range $z=1-9$ and stellar masses $\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot}) \geq 8$ using JWST/NIRCam imaging from JADES. We define clumps as compact off-centre surface-brightness peaks with characteristic sizes of a few hundred parsecs, and develop a dendrogram-based clump-finding method with astrodendro to identify them directly in rest-frame UV, blue, and optical images. Clump-detection completeness is quantified through injection-recovery simulations, which we use to define ($M_\star$, $z$)-dependent 50 per cent completeness-limited $f_{\rm clumpy}$, alongside a probability-weighted completeness-corrected statistic for comparison. For the full sample, the blue and optical $f_{\rm clumpy}$ reach $\approx55$ per cent at $z\approx1-2$ and decline to $\lesssim5$ per cent by $z\approx8-9$, while the UV fraction remains higher at $\approx12$ per cent. At $z\approx1-2$, $f_{\rm clumpy}$ increases strongly with stellar mass, from $\approx35$ per cent in the lowest-mass bin to $\approx85$ per cent at $\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})\geq10$. Most clumpy galaxies host only one or two robustly detected off-centre clumps, while higher-multiplicity systems occur preferentially in massive, strongly star-forming galaxies at low to intermediate redshift. Comparison with major merger rates suggests that mergers could account for a substantial fraction of low-mass ($\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})<8.5$), high-redshift clumpy galaxies, while the observed fractions in more massive systems ($\log_{10}(M_\star/{\rm M_\odot})>9$) require an additional in situ clump-formation channel. Our results are consistent with a transition from merger-dominated clumpy morphologies at early times and low stellar masses to an increasing contribution from in situ clump-formation channels, such as disc instabilities, in more massive systems.

Resolving the extended interstellar medium of infrared galaxies I. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon emission

C. M. Lofaro, T. Diaz-Santos, L. Armus, V. Charmandaris, T. Lai +21

PDFWed 21 Oct
We investigate spatial variations in polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) properties and photoelectric heating efficiency in 67 nearby luminous infrared galaxies from the Great Observatories All-sky LIRG Survey (GOALS). Using mid-infrared spectroscopy from Spitzer and far-infrared cooling-line maps from Herschel, we extract spectra in concentric annuli and matched aperture grids out to about 14 kpc. PAH features are measured with the CAFE spectral decomposition tool. We trace photoelectric heating efficiency by combining PAH emission with cooling lines after isolating the neutral-gas contribution. In starburst galaxies, the PAH 11.3/7.7 ratio rises out to about 5 kpc while 6.2/7.7 remains nearly constant, indicating predominantly large, neutral PAHs. Strong AGNs influence average PAH charge states at 6-14 kpc as effectively as moderate AGNs at 1-6 kpc. Photoelectric heating efficiency increases with radius in both starburst and AGN-hosting galaxies as the radiation field weakens. In starbursts, higher efficiency is associated with more neutral PAHs, while AGN-dominated systems show the opposite relation. These results provide quantitative, spatially resolved constraints on the extent of AGN effects on dust properties in a population of luminous infrared galaxies.

Searching for Dense Perturbers in Cold Dark Matter Simulations

Xingyu Zhang, Hai-Bo Yu, Demao Kong, Ethan O.Nadler

PDFWed 21 Oct
Recently, several low-mass, dense perturbers have been inferred from astrophysical observations of gravitational lensing, stellar streams, and satellite galaxies. If interpreted as dark matter subhalos, their inner densities exceed the predictions of the standard cold dark matter (CDM) model. We systematically investigate this tension using a CDM-only zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way analog. We analyze more than 5000 subhalos with masses down to $10^6\,M_\odot$ and reconstruct their inner density profiles using a tidal truncation model. The simulated subhalos are then compared with the observed compact perturbers in terms of their density profiles, enclosed masses, concentrations, and projected cylindrical masses. Blue supergiants (BSGs) are crucial to understanding the evolution of massive stars. Although distinguishing between pre- and post-red supergiant (RSG) phases is challenging, stellar pulsations provide valuable constraints on their masses and evolutionary status. This study analyzes TESS light curves of six BSGs with high L/M to identify periodicities. We performed theoretical modeling of the radial unstable modes to characterize the strange modes. We aim to estimate asteroseismic masses, compare them with spectroscopic and evolutionary mass estimates, and assess the evolutionary stage of the stars. We employed the Lomb-Scargle periodogram and the weighted wavelet Z-transform to address quasiperiodic behavior and time-varying signals. A linear nonadiabatic stability analysis was performed to test for radial perturbations, generating eigenfrequencies that enable stellar mass estimation.

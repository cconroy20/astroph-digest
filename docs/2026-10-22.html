Discovery of a Broad-Line Active Galactic Nucleus at $z = 9.98$

Xingyao Cai, Akio K. Inoue, Masafusa Onoue, Suzuka Arai, Yoshinobu Fudamoto +10

14 pages, 5 figures, 1 table, submitted to ApJL, all comments are welcome

PDFThu 22 Oct
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has uncovered a previously unexplored population of faint active galactic nuclei (AGNs) in the early Universe. We present MoM-BL-z10 at $z=9.9837\pm0.0009$, identified through a systematic search for broad-line AGNs at $z\geq6$ using public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive. The PRISM spectrum exhibits a broad component in H$\beta$ at 5.35 microns. A narrow+broad Gaussian fit yields a broad H$\beta$ detection at S/N = 3.87, with FWHM = $3480^{+2040}_{-950}$ km s$^{-1}$ and $L_{{\rm H}\beta,{\rm broad}}=1.25^{+0.33}_{-0.31}\times10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$. The detection of [Ne III] $\lambda3870$ and the high [Ne III]/[O II] and [O III]/H$\beta$ ratios further support the AGN interpretation. Its rest-frame UV and optical continuum slopes, compact NIRCam morphology, and a tentative MIRI F770W detection suggest similarities to Little Red Dots (LRDs). The broad H$\beta$ detection enables the first virial SMBH mass estimate at $z\sim10$. We derive $M_{\rm BH}=3.6^{+5.8}_{-1.7}\times10^7 M_\odot$ and $\lambda_{\rm Edd}=0.11^{+0.10}_{-0.07}$ using the standard single-epoch virial calibration and quasar bolometric correction. We also consider an alternative scenario adopting the LRD-specific bolometric correction and assuming $\lambda_{\rm Edd}=1$, which yields $M_{\rm BH}=2.1^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\times10^6 M_\odot$. Under either scenario, the inferred SMBH mass at a cosmic age of only 470 Myr highlights the challenge of rapid BH growth in the early Universe, suggesting super-Eddington accretion from a stellar-mass seed or a more massive seed. As the highest-redshift broad-line AGN identified to date, MoM-BL-z10 provides a new observational benchmark for understanding the early growth of supermassive black holes.

A design concept for the High Resolution Imager on the Habitable Worlds Observatory

Vincent Van Eylen, Richard Massey, Alan Smith, Jerome Amiaux, Florence Ardellier +13

17 pages, 14 figures, Submitted to RASTI's HWO special issue

PDFThu 22 Oct
The Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) will be NASA's next flagship astrophysics mission, designed to search for signs of life on planets around nearby stars and conduct transformational science across astrophysics. Its High Resolution Imager (HRI) is not only the observatory's main imaging camera, but also provides grism spectroscopy and sub-microarcsecond astrometry, as well as the observatory's fine guidance, wavefront sensing and pupil imaging. We present a design concept for HRI, developed during concurrent design sessions by flowing down science requirements within the envelope of NASA's EAC-1 telescope architecture. Light from a 3'x2' field is collimated by two mirrors, then divided by a dichroic for simultaneous observations in an ultraviolet-to-visible arm covering 200-900 nm and a near-infrared arm covering 0.9-2.2 um. By minimising the number of reflective surfaces upstream of the dichroic, our optical design maximises throughput and stability, while reducing wavefront error. Readout modes can be chosen in orbit to image the entire field in exposures of at least 10 seconds, or in smaller windows for observations up to 1 kHz. Throughout, we emphasise the interplay between subsystems that couple design choices.

Unveiling the correlation between the UV 2175 Å dust attenuation bump, PAH emission, and gas-phase metallicity at cosmic noon

L. Arriscado, I. Shivaei, L. A. Boogaard, A. Mizener, D. Calzetti +15

Accepted for publication in A&A; 23 pages, 18 figures, 9 tables

PDFThu 22 Oct
The UV 2175 Å attenuation bump is one of the most prominent yet poorly understood features of galaxy attenuation curves. Its main candidate carrier is polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) molecules, which are more prevalent in high-metallicity environments. We present the first systematic study of the relationship between PAH abundance and the UV bump in 49 galaxies at cosmic noon (1.5 < z < 2.7), examining its dependence on stellar mass, star formation rate (SFR), and V-band optical depth. For 19 galaxies, we also investigate gas-phase metallicity using oxygen abundances. We measure the bump amplitude from VLT/MUSE spectroscopy and derive galaxy properties, including the PAH-to-dust mass fraction $q_{\rm PAH}$, through spectral energy distribution fitting with Prospector, combining JWST/NIRCam and JWST/MIRI photometry. Oxygen abundances are derived from strong-line diagnostics using Keck/MOSFIRE and JWST/NIRSpec data. We find a positive correlation between bump amplitude $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ (Spearman $\rho = 0.60$). UV bumps are rarely detected at low $q_{\rm PAH}$, supporting PAHs as their primary carriers. Both $E_{\rm bump}$ and $q_{\rm PAH}$ correlate with stellar mass, oxygen abundance, and optical depth, and anticorrelate with specific SFR, suggesting that PAHs and the UV bump are suppressed in low-metallicity, dust-poor, and/or intensely star-forming environments. This is consistent with PAH destruction in environments with limited dust shielding and/or strong radiation fields. A principal component analysis of $E_{\rm bump}$, $q_{\rm PAH}$, optical depth, specific SFR, and oxygen abundance explains 97% of the variance, suggesting that previously reported correlations with stellar mass reflect an underlying dependence on metallicity. Finally, variations in dust geometry may explain the wide range of bump amplitudes (0-0.5 mag) at high $q_{\rm PAH}$.

The MAGE Project: Size Evolution and Disk Formation in Massive Galaxies at Cosmic Dawn

Zhiyuan Yao, Avishai Dekel, Romain Teyssier, Rachel Somerville, Nir Mandelker +3

30 pages, 15 figures, submitted to MNRAS, comments are welcome!

PDFThu 22 Oct
We investigate size evolution and disk formation with a new suite of eleven cosmological zoom-in simulations, Massive Assembly of Galaxies in the Early universe (MAGE), run with RAMSES at $\sim10\,\mathrm{pc}$ resolution until $z\simeq9$. The target galaxies span stellar masses of $M_\star\simeq2\times10^8$-$3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ at $z=9$. The stellar half-mass radius grows steeply with stellar mass at low masses, turns over near $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$, and evolves weakly with redshift. The intrinsic UV sizes in MAGE overlap the compact end of observed distributions, including the compact strong-line systems, but not the more extended population at $z>9$. Dust, PSF-limited size measurements, and our overdense environments may contribute to this offset. In the simulations, stars dominate the mass within the half-mass radius above $M_\star\simeq5\times10^8\,M_\odot$, where the central circular speed exceeds a characteristic stellar-feedback scale. Outflows are then increasingly retained within the galaxy, consistent with gas recycling on galactic rather than halo scales, which sustains central gas supply and drives a compaction-like central buildup. The turnover in size at $M_\star\simeq5\times10^9\,M_\odot$ coincides with rapid growth of rotational support to $v/\sigma\simeq1.5$-$3$ in star-forming gas and stars younger than 10Myr, as the central circular speed exceeds the halo virial speed, which the star-forming gas velocity dispersion tracks. The subsequent low central gas fraction, declining central star formation, and younger outskirts suggest the onset of inside-out quenching.

FEAST: Spatially Resolved Star Formation History of the NGC 4485/NGC 4490 System and its Tidal Bridge from JWST/NIRCam Color Magnitude Diagrams

Giacomo Bortolini, Matteo Correnti, Angela Adamo, Michele Cignoni, Monica Tosi +17

27 pages, 16 figures, 2 appendices, accepted for publication in in The Astrophysical Journal

PDFThu 22 Oct
We present the spatially resolved star formation history (SFH) of the interacting, low-mass, gas-rich galaxy pair NGC 4485/NGC 4490, based on deep JWST/NIRCam imaging obtained as part of the FEAST program. Using resolved stars' near-infrared color-magnitude diagrams (CMDs) and the SFERA 2.0 CMD fitting code, we reconstruct the SFH of the system, including the tidal bridge connecting the two galaxies. We find that recent star formation is widespread across the system, extending beyond the main bodies of the galaxies into the tidal bridge and other off-center areas. In particular, a significant fraction of the recent star formation in NGC 4485 occurs outside its central region. All major regions of the system exhibit two prominent episodes of enhanced star formation, occurring around 0.5-1 Gyr and 10-30 Myr ago, with the tidal bridge showing the largest increase relative to its average star formation rate. These peaks in star formation activity occur a few hundred million years after the epochs of the first and second pericenter passages predicted by independent dynamical models, supporting a scenario in which repeated tidal interactions triggered the observed bursts through gas redistribution and compression. Our results place direct observational constraints on the formation history of NGC 4485/NGC 4490 and provide evidence for the important role of galaxy interactions in driving spatially extended, bursty star formation in low-mass galaxies.

A Little Red Dot emerging from a nitrogen-rich UV-luminous galaxy with Very Massive Stars

Alberto Torralba, Jorryt Matthee, Rui Marques-Chaves, Fabrice Martins, Benjamín Navarrete +11

18 pages incl. references, 15 figures. Submitted to A&A. Comments welcome

PDFThu 22 Oct
JWST is revealing abundant populations of high-$z$ galaxies with unforeseen properties: UV-luminous galaxies with high nitrogen enrichment, populations of very massive stars (VMSs; $>100\ M_\odot$), and early forms of massive black hole growth such as the Little Red Dots (LRDs). Here we present all these phenomena appearing simultaneously in COLA1, a luminous ($M_{\rm UV}=-21.3$) galaxy at $z=6.6$. New NIRSpec medium-resolution data reveal a blue UV continuum ($\beta_{\rm UV}=-2.5$) with strong emission lines including NIV] $\lambda1486$, stellar P Cygni profiles, and broad HeII $\lambda1640$. We fit the UV with stellar population models, and find that the models including VMSs (limited to $300\ M_\odot$) are statistically preferred. The best-fit stellar population has $M_*\approx10^8\ M_\odot$, and a young age of $2.4\pm0.8$ Myr. The rest-optical shows a redder continuum ($\beta_{\rm opt}=-1.1$) and broad Balmer lines with Balmer absorption akin to LRD spectra. By evolving the best-fit stellar population we find that the UV could dim by 1-2 mag in $\sim5$ Myr, and hence this system would be observed as a typical V-shaped LRD. We measure a supersolar $\rm log_{10}(N/O)=-0.07\pm0.05$ and subsolar $\rm log_{10}(C/O)=-0.64\pm0.11$; therefore $\rm log_{10}(N/C)=+0.58\pm0.12$. The independent density-sensitive NIV] $\lambda\lambda1483,1486$ and CIII] $\lambda\lambda1906/1909$ ratios require two zones with $n_e\gtrsim10^{6.5}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $n_e\lesssim10^{3.5}$ cm$^{-3}$, respectively. We estimate $\rm log_{10}(N/C)>1.3$ (2$\sigma$) for the dense zone, requiring extreme N/C enrichment mechanisms such as highly rotating $>100\ M_\odot$ VMSs, tidal disruption events, or supermassive stars. We speculate that runaway collisions of massive stars in a dense stellar cluster may have lead to the seeding of an intermediate-mass black hole resulting in the formation of an LRD.

Chemical abundance patterns at z=1-7: Carbon, Nitrogen, Neon, Sulphur, and Argon abundances from JWST spectroscopy

E. Cataldi, F. Belfiore, M. Curti, B. Moreschini, A. Marconi +20

19 pages, 9 figures; submitted to A&A

PDFThu 22 Oct
The relative abundances of chemical elements in the interstellar medium (ISM) trace the history of star formation and chemical enrichment in galaxies. We investigate the abundance patterns of carbon (C), nitrogen (N), neon (Ne), sulfur (S), and argon (Ar) in star-forming galaxies at z=1-7, combining ultra-deep JWST/NIRSpec observations from the MARTA survey with archival spectroscopy. We assemble 244 galaxies with [OIII]4363 detections and derive direct Te-based abundances. New empirical strong-line calibrations for N/O, Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O extend the analysis to an additional 514 galaxies. We find distinct abundance-metallicity trends. N/O is enhanced by 0.18 dex in median relative to local galaxies, with a stronger enhancement at low metallicity. Direct measurements reveal decreasing S/O and Ar/O with increasing metallicity, while strong-line measurements suggest a flattening at higher metallicities, especially for S/O. We also identify a stellar-mass dependence in the O/Ar-Ar/H plane, with lower-mass galaxies exhibiting lower O/Ar at fixed Ar/H, consistent with differences in star-formation efficiency and delayed SNe Ia enrichment. Ne/O remains approximately constant up to 12+log(O/H)=8.2, with a mild increase at higher metallicities and no significant redshift evolution. C/O tentatively increases with metallicity, broadly following the local relation. Finally, a forward model shows that observational selection affects the abundance ratios differently: it mainly restricts the accessible metallicity range for Ne/O and Ar/O, but preferentially selects galaxies with enhanced N/O at low metallicity, with a weaker effect for S/O. These results provide new constraints on chemical enrichment at high redshift and highlight the importance of accounting for selection effects when interpreting abundance-metallicity trends.

The Structural Anatomy of Galaxies: Environmental Dependence of Morphological and Quenching Evolution at 0.1 < z < 0.5

Can Xu, John D. Silverman, Qiusheng Gu, Daniela Galárraga-Espinosa, Andreas L. Faisst +10

30 pages, 17 figures, 8 tables. Submitted to ApJ

PDFThu 22 Oct
We investigate how cluster environments affect galaxy star formation, structure, and stellar populations using spectroscopically-confirmed galaxies in X-ray-selected eROSITA clusters, that have deep HSC-SSP imaging and DESI DR1 spectroscopy. At 0.1<z<0.5, we compare dust-corrected Ha star formation rates (SFRs), effective radii (Re), bulge-to-total light ratios (B/T), and Dn4000 between cluster satellites and a stellar-mass- and redshift-matched field control sample. We identify UVJ-quiescent galaxies and divide the UVJ star-forming population at log(sSFR_Ha)= -10.5 yr-1 into actively star-forming and Ha-low systems, the latter referred to as green-valley (GV) galaxies. Cluster–field differences depend strongly on stellar mass, projected cluster-centric radius, and galaxy population. Cluster star-forming galaxies show modest SFR suppression but weak structural offsets. The strongest coupled signatures occur among Ha-low UVJ-star-forming galaxies, particularly low-mass satellites at R<0.5R500, which have lower SFRs, smaller light-weighted sizes, larger B/T, and larger Dn4000 than matched field counterparts. Quiescent galaxies show weaker structural offsets but retain a positive Dn4000 residual. We quantify the joint signal using a Mahalanobis distance based on matched residuals in SFR, Re, B/T, and Dn4000 with a field–field reference covariance. The multivariate offset is largest for low-mass GV galaxies and decreases toward larger projected radius. The broader radial extent of SFR suppression relative to structural offsets suggests that star-formation suppression has a broader environmental reach than structural transformation. These results show that environmental quenching and structural transformation are most strongly coupled in the Ha-low transition population.

A combined HST and JWST analysis of the stellar population in Draco II

Axelle C. Raffin, Giacomo Cordoni, Helmut Jerjen, Gary S. Da Costa, Maria V. Legnardi +3

Accepted for publication in MNRAS. 15 pages, 18 figures

PDFThu 22 Oct
Draco II (Dra II) is one of the faintest and most metal-poor Milky Way satellites known, yet its nature as either an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy or an extended globular cluster remains debated. We perform a photometric analysis of the stellar population of Dra II reducing deep multi-band observations from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). We select Dra II member stars based on their proper motions and photometric quality criteria. For the first time, optical and infrared photometry are combined to produce a stellar catalogue reaching 50 percent completeness at $F814W = 26.5$ mag (corresponding to the absolute magnitude $M_{F814W} = 10.1$ mag). The colour-magnitude diagram confirms that Dra II hosts an old stellar population with an age of $13.5_{-1.5}^{+1.7}$ Gyr at a distance of $19.2\pm0.9$ kpc. We measure an $\alpha$-enhanced stellar population with a low mean metallicity of $[\rm Fe/H]_{CaHK} = -2.38 \pm 0.17$ dex and confirm the presence of an intrinsic metallicity spread of $\sigma_{[\rm Fe/H]} = 0.85 \pm 0.13$ dex. We identify 10 unresolved main-sequence binary stars in the HST catalogue with mass ratios $q > 0.5$ in the mass range $0.43<M_1/M_\odot<0.71$, corresponding to a binary fraction of $f_{bin}^{q>0.5} = 0.21 \pm 0.07$. This value is higher than those typically observed in globular clusters and lies near the upper range measured in open clusters. The stellar mass function is best modelled by a single power law with a slope of $1.53\pm0.26$ over the mass range $0.13$–$0.77\ M_\odot$. We find no evidence of mass segregation. Consistent results are obtained independently from the JWST catalogue. Combining these findings strongly suggests that Draco II is best classified as an ultra-faint dwarf galaxy rather than a globular cluster.

Ballistic mixing: stellar mergers drive internal mixing in extremely massive stars, with implications for proto-globular cluster pollution

J. Roman-Garza, C. Charbonnel, A. Rantala, T. Fragos, M. Gieles +1

9 pages, 8 figures

PDFThu 22 Oct
Extremely massive stars, with masses of $10^3$-$10^4$ M$_\odot$, have been proposed as potential sources of the chemical enrichment associated with multiple populations in globular clusters. Early on the main sequence, these stars reach central temperatures of 65 to 75 MK, enabling nucleosynthesis through the CNO, NeNa, and MgAl cycles, producing abundance patterns similar to those observed in the so-called cluster's second population. Stellar mergers are among the leading formation channels for extremely massive stars. In this work, we follow the formation of a $\sim6700$ M$_\odot$ star via 253 stellar mergers, drawn from a direct $N$-body star cluster simulation with properties similar to the densest star clusters observed at redshift $z\sim10$. We evolve each star as a 1D stellar model and construct the merger products from mixing and shock heating prescriptions calibrated to 3D simulations of parabolic collisions. We find that entropy-based mixing efficiently transports nuclear-processed material from the convective core to the envelope, while merger-driven heating promotes chemical homogenization. As a result, the stellar surface becomes enriched with H-burning products, producing abundance patterns consistent with those observed in globular cluster multiple populations and supporting the role of merger-formed extremely massive stars as potential sources of early chemical enrichment in proto-globular cluster-like conditions.

PDFThu 22 Oct
Nitrogen-rich (N-rich) field stars (likely second-generation escapees from globular clusters) provide a powerful, but currently sample-limited, probe of globular-cluster disruption in the Galactic halo. We exploit the fact that enhanced NH, CN absorption depresses optical u band flux, while the near-ultraviolet (NUV) band is largely insensitive to nitrogen enhancement while remaining sensitive to overall metallicity. Combining GALEX NUV photometry with (synthetic) SDSS optical photometry, we construct a [Fe/H]-dependent stellar-locus model for giants and select significant redder u-g outliers as N-rich candidates. The diverse kinematic and dynamical properties of our candidates suggest multiple origins. This scalable photometric approach enables systematic searches for N-rich halo stars and is well suited to forthcoming wide-field surveys such as CSST.

Enhanced Mass Transfer from Extended Shock-Supported Stellar Chromospheres

Soumyadeep Bhattacharjee, Jim Fuller, Kareem El-Badry

17 pages including appendix, 13 figures. Version after first PASP revision; resubmitting soon. All comments welcome

PDFThu 22 Oct
Mass transfer (MT) in binary systems is a crucial and complex process. Even before the donor's photosphere fills its Roche lobe, matter from its outer atmosphere can reach the inner Lagrange point, thus initiating interaction in the so-called optically thin phase. Most existing models account for a very tenuous atmosphere supported by thermal gas pressure, thus predicting very little MT until the donor fills its Roche lobe. However, recent hydrodynamic simulations of giant stars suggest that shock-driven outflows can create substantially thicker "chromospheres” extending to a few stellar radii. In this work, we derive a simple analytic model for a shock-supported chromosphere in a binary potential and calculate the corresponding MT rate. We also implement our prescription in the stellar evolution code MESA. We show that chromospheric MT rates can be orders of magnitude higher than prior estimates for optically thin MT, especially for stars high on the red giant or asymptotic giant branch. This allows for substantial accretion before Roche lobe overflow, both for systems that undergo stable mass transfer and for systems that go through common envelope evolution. Thus, for a given initial binary configuration, this may alter the final period and component masses compared to those predicted classically. Our model is similar to Wind Roche-lobe Overflow, but it is physically rather than empirically motivated. We discuss implications for various post-interaction binaries that can be tested with population synthesis studies and observed binary populations. Additionally, we have identified an inaccuracy in the current implementation of the optically-thin Ritter 1988 prescription in MESA, which we correct.

The diversity of late-time interaction in Type II supernovae

Luc Dessart, Wynn V. Jacobson-Galán, K. Azalee Bostroem

submitted to A&A

PDFThu 22 Oct
Studying core-collapse supernovae (SNe) beyond one year after explosion, when ejecta interaction with circumstellar material (CSM) progressively becomes the dominant power source, offers critical insights into the progenitor mass loss history. Using binary progenitors that underwent different levels of H-rich envelope stripping, we present radiative transfer calculations of the ejecta with CSM interaction in Type IIb, II-L and II-P SN scenarios covering epochs of 1.5 to 3-6yr, with a focus on the UV and optical properties. The impact of CSM interaction is evaluated for a cold-dense shell (CDS) of 0.1-2.0Msun (CDS velocity of ~5000-10000km/s) and shock powers of 1e39-1e41erg/s, producing a grid of about 200 models. We find that Lyalpha, MgII2800, and Halpha are typically the strongest individual lines, MgII2800 being the most constant relative to changes in time and in CDS mass. Lyalpha thrives at low CDS mass and is strongly attenuated otherwise. A large fraction of the emerging UV and optical flux is, however, influenced by a forest of Fe lines, which introduces strong optical depth effects. A myriad of other lines, generally forbidden, are predicted that each contain less than a percent of the total flux. N enrichment yield stronger N lines in the UV and optical. A subset of these models roughly match UV and optical observations of SNe 1993J, 1998S, 2013ej, and 2023ixf at 1.5-3yr, although matching the full range consistently is a challenge, in particular when dust must be invoked. Blue-red asymmetry of line profiles is typical of all lines in the UV, the more so at earlier times or higher CDS mass, even in the absence of dust. In our simulations, optical depth effects, associated with the gas or dust or both, quenches the emission of Lyalpha or [OIII]4958-5007A from regions within or below the CDS.

Data-driven characterisation of variable source light curves with multimodal representation learning

M. Madarász, G. Marton, M. Audard

20 pages, 13 figures, 12 tables, accepted for publication in A&A

PDFThu 22 Oct
Surveys such as the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) produce light curves for hundreds of millions of variables. Their characterisation traditionally relies on supervised classifiers with hand-crafted features and curated labels, limiting them to predefined taxonomies and individual surveys. We present a general, survey-agnostic framework that characterises variable sources without labelled training data, producing low-dimensional representations of light curve properties such as amplitude, periodicity, and asymmetry. A multimodal architecture combines two complementary views, a Transformer encoder on raw observations and a convolutional neural network on a generalised Lomb-Scargle periodogram. Each light curve is randomly split into two temporal halves as independent views, and the branches are trained jointly with VICReg to produce embeddings invariant across halves while keeping each dimension informative. We evaluate on 37 006 ZTF sources spanning 14 variable star classes in four broad physical categories. A linear classifier on the frozen 64-dimensional embeddings achieves 77.1% balanced accuracy for classes and 90.5% for broad categories. Combining both branches improves balanced accuracy by up to 14.9 percentage points over the best single modality, confirming their complementarity. Interpretable light curve properties are encoded on distinct dimensions, and within-class analysis reveals physical substructure unresolved by reference labels. Retraining on ZTF g and i bands without modification reproduces the main results, and concatenating multi-band embeddings improves balanced accuracy to 86.1%. The framework needs no labels, phase folding, or survey-specific preprocessing and applies directly to other surveys with different cadences and wavelength coverage.

Constraints on Reionization from Planck Low-$\ell$ EE Polarization and the Lyman-Series Forests

Garett Lopez, Anson D'Aloisio, Christopher Cain

33 Pages 13 Figures, 3 Appendices

PDFThu 22 Oct
CMB measurements tightly constrain the Thomson scattering optical depth ($\tau_{\rm es}$), but they still permit a broad range of degenerate reionization histories. Incorporating Lyman-series forest constraints on the end of reionization can alleviate the degeneracy and narrow the range of allowed histories. We explore this effect using forest measurements combined with full Planck likelihoods. We apply upper limits on the neutral fraction at $4.9\lesssim z\lesssim6.9$ derived from the dark pixel fraction, as well as a lower limit at $z=5.8$ from damping-wing-like features on the edges of long dark gaps in stacked forest spectra. We find that neither the choice of parametric vs. non-parametric reionization model, nor the choice among different forest data combinations, produces a significant shift in $\tau_{\rm es}$ or the other cosmological parameters. Instead, forest constraints primarily redistribute $\tau_{\rm es}$ in redshift. Combining dark pixel, damping-wing-like, and CMB measurements, we find $z_{50}=7.7^{+0.7}_{-0.8}$ and a 95% upper limit of $\Delta z_{90}<11.4$ for the midpoint and duration of reionization, respectively. The asymmetry of the reionization history ($A_z$) is not well constrained by these data. Stronger constraints can be obtained using Ly$\alpha$ forest measurements of the mean IGM photoionization rate, at the cost of requiring more detailed modeling. Using a new semi-analytic reionization model, we obtain (model-dependent) constraints of $z_{50} = 7.4^{+0.2}_{-0.3}$, $\Delta z_{90}=6.0^{+0.8}_{-1.0}$, and $A_z = 2.4^{+0.3}_{-0.7}$. We also find evidence that the ionizing emissivity of reionization sources declines after $z=7-8$, perhaps signaling a decline in the escape fraction and/or the onset of a feedback process. Our results motivate the development of forest likelihood products and fast forward models for its transmission statistics.

Gravothermal Evolution of Self-Interacting Dark Matter Halos across Knudsen Numbers

James Gurian, Suroor Seher Gandhi, Neal Dalal, Simon May

19 pages, 9 figures

PDFThu 22 Oct
If the dark matter self-interacts elastically via a light mediator, then at relative velocities above a scale $w$ dictated by particle physics parameters, the cross section scales as $\sigma \propto v^{-4}$. Depending on the value of $w$, the self-interaction cross section in low-mass halos could be (much) larger than has been previously studied extensively. We perform kinetic simulations of isolated self-interacting dark matter halos using the code KiSS-SIDM for a wide range of halo masses, concentrations, and cross sections from $50 \,\mathrm{cm}^2 \, \mathrm{g}^{-1}$ to $250{,}000 \, \mathrm{cm}^2 \, \mathrm{g}^{-1}$, including several velocity-dependent and fully differential (anisotropic) cross sections. We demonstrate that for idealized NFW halos the collapse time can be predicted from the Knudsen number at the scale radius of the halo. We perform similar calculations for halos extracted from cosmological simulations using either $N$-body (collisionless) or SPH (strongly collisional) dynamics, and then evolve cutouts with KiSS-SIDM. For these halos, the collapse times differ from the idealized NFW simulations by up to a factor of a few. Further, we calibrate a hydrostatic fluid model to these results, which solves the structure equations as a boundary value problem via two-point shooting. The fluid calculation predicts that for velocity-independent cross sections, a convective region of the halo develops late in the collapse. The collapse times agree well between the kinetic simulations and the fluid code, especially in the velocity-independent case. The density profiles agree well in some cases, which include all of the velocity-dependent cross section runs.

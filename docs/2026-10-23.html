The THESAN-ZOOM project: the delayed rise of Lyman continuum escape fractions in bursty high-redshift galaxies

Parth Mehrotra, Aaron Smith, Yuan Bian, William McClymont, Ewald Puchwein +11

26 pages, 24 figures. Comments welcome

PDFFri 23 Oct
The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at ~10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of ~16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$\alpha$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.

Impact of Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger revealed by a single cosmic clock: thick-to-thin disc transition, gas disc shrinking and bar formation

Natsuki Funakoshi, Daisuke Kawata, Jason L. Sanders, Ioana Ciucă, Robert J. J. Grand +3

23 pages, 14 figures, submitted to MNRAS

PDFFri 23 Oct
Whether the formation of the Galactic bar was connected to the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus (GSE) merger, the last significant merger experienced by the Milky Way, remains an open question. The bar formation epoch has been inferred from the star formation history of the nuclear stellar disc (NSD) traced by Mira variables, and the GSE merger epoch from the age-metallicity relation of red giants. However, these epochs rest on different stellar tracers with different age scales and cannot be compared directly. We recalibrate the Mira period-age relation onto the age scale of APOGEE red giants, with ages from a Bayesian neural network trained on Kepler asteroseismic ages. We fit the Gaia DR3 proper motions of 2394 O-rich disc Mira variables with an action-based quasi-isothermal distribution function whose parameters vary with period, and derive the period-age relation by matching their vertical velocity dispersion as a function of period to that of the APOGEE red giants as a function of age in Funakoshi et al. (2025). This places the NSD star-formation rise and peak at 8.5-10.5 Gyr ago on the red giant age scale, overlapping with the final stage of the GSE-induced starburst, thick-to-thin disc transition and the contraction of the star-forming gas disc. If NSD star formation traces bar formation, these coincidences suggest a possible scenario in which the GSE seeded the bar formation, while fresh gas supplied by the GSE and outer disc gas driven inward by its tidal torques, increased the baryonic dominance of the inner Galaxy.

Optical-Infrared Extinction Variation in Nearby Molecular Clouds

Marshall Hobson-Ritz, Sarv Parteek Singh, Eddie Schlafly, Lee Mundy, Alberto Bolatto

PDFFri 23 Oct
The dust extinction curve is an important probe of the properties of dust grains, particularly their size distribution. The properties of dust grains are expected to change significantly in dense molecular clouds due to shielding from interstellar radiation and increased frequency of grain-grain and grain-gas collisions. We measure the dust extinction curve shape parameter R(V) in 9 nearby, dense molecular clouds using data from the APOGEE spectroscopic survey, in combination with ten-band photometry from PS1, 2MASS, and WISE. We analyze the results to determine how the extinction curve varies in nearby molecular clouds. We find that most molecular clouds show a positive correlation between R(V) and E(B-V), but the strength of the correlation varies significantly between clouds. In addition, we find that the clouds with the strongest correlation tend to have very high dispersion in R(V) at fixed E(B-V), which tightens dramatically when clouds are subdivided into separate regions. This suggests that local environmental conditions beyond density shape the R(V)-E(B-V) relation.

Gone Without a Bang: A Search for Failed Core-Collapse Supernovae in ZTF and Predictions for Rubin and Roman

Shion Murakawa, Paul Draghis, Kevin Burdge, Ore Gottlieb, Aaron Householder +1

17 pages, 7 figures, submitted to ApJ

PDFFri 23 Oct
The core-collapse of massive stars (${\geq}10\ \rm M_\odot$) usually takes the form of a supernova, but in some cases may lead to a direct collapse, leading to a failed core-collapse supernova (FccSN). Wide-field time-domain surveys, such as the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF), are a useful tool to find such “disappearing" stars. We use a piecewise linear function model to search the entire ZTF optical photometric catalog for FccSNe, yielding 125 “faders" that dip significantly with a flat top and bottom flux. Although no candidates were definitively found, some of the sources exhibit promising behavior that may indicate a direct collapse event. Assuming a null result from the search, we calculate at 99% credibility that the maximum statistical rate of FccSNe in the Milky Way is 147 per century. To contextualize these findings, we also simulate the formation of stellar populations in Milky Way-like galaxies to estimate the expected number of FccSNe observable by ZTF. This simulation predicts ZTF to observe $0.234\pm0.006$ FccSNe per century, which aligns with the lack of definitive observations in ZTF. However, we predict that similar monitoring using the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's Wide Field Instrument (WFI) could enable population-level studies of nearby FccSNe.

The Milky Way's Early Disk Was Built in Bursts. II. The Multi-component Age-Metallicity Structure of the High-$α$ Disk

Tiancheng Sun, Maosheng Xiang, Jifeng Liu, Meng Zhang, Yaqian Wu

19 pages, 13 figures, Accepted for publication in ApJ

PDFFri 23 Oct
Whether the high-$\alpha$ disk experienced a continuous or episodic star-formation history remains an open question. Here we investigate the age–metallicity distribution of the high-$\alpha$ disk using approximately 145,000 Gaia DR3 subgiants with $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]>-1$, 6.5~kpc $\lesssim R\lesssim9.5$~kpc, and $|Z|\lesssim1.5$~kpc. We adopt a forward-modelling framework that accounts for individual age and metallicity uncertainties and corrects for sample selection effects. The reconstructed distribution reveals two components: a broad, dominant population peaking at $\sim10.6$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.38$, and an older, more metal-poor population peaking at $\sim12.4$ Gyr and $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]\simeq-0.79$. These features suggest an episodic history of star formation and chemical enrichment in the high-$\alpha$ disk, qualitatively consistent with cosmological simulations in which recurrent gas accretion drives variations in the star formation rate. The age of the older component is close to the $\sim12.3$~Gyr epoch inferred for the proposed Low-energy–Kraken–Heracles accretion event, suggesting a possible connection between this early interaction and enhanced disk star formation. There is tentative evidence that the dominant population is possibly composed of two sub-components with age distributions peaking at $\sim11.1$ Gyr and $\sim10.0$ Gyr, respectively. Together, these results favour a picture in which the high-$\alpha$ disk was built through recurrent episodes of enhanced star formation rather than a single temporally smooth phase, even for the regime of ${\rm [Fe/H]}>-1$.

Searching for substellar companion candidates with Gaia. V. M dwarfs within 15 pc studied with absolute astrometry, radial velocities, and direct imaging

F. Destriez, A.-M. Lagrange, G. Hébrard, F. Kiefer, P. Rubini +8

Accepted by A&A on October 19, 2026

PDFFri 23 Oct
In this work, we survey M-type stars within 15 pc observed by Gaia based on their colour, magnitude, and quality of astrometric data. We aim to detect new substellar companions using primarily Gaia astrometry and, when available, proper-motion anomaly (PMa) along with radial velocity (RV) and direct imaging (DI) data. We used the GaiaPMEX tool to search for bound companions around 582 M dwarfs. We identified all previously known stellar and planetary companions that were consistent with the Gaia excess signal. Then for all newly identified companions, we computed detection limits in the (mass, semi-major axis) parameter space using archival RV and DI data, to possibly rule out the presence of a stellar companion. The joint analysis of the parameter space allowed by astrometry and constrained by RV and DI provides tight constraints on the nature of the companion. We found 156 M dwarfs to have a bound companion within the Gaia sensitivity domain. The astrometric signals of 37 and seven of them are due to known stellar and substellar companions, respectively. We report 35 new substellar companions among which at least ten giant planets orbiting between 0.2 au and 2 au, with masses close to 1 M_Jup. We also report 77 substellar candidates, and provide detection limits using astrometry+RV+DI for 50 stars with known exoplanets and no significant Gaia excess noise. This work reveals a significant population of new substellar companions to M-type stars, challenging core accretion models. These results will be crucial inputs for estimating occurrence rates and studying the architecture of planetary systems around M dwarfs. Some new companions presented here, located beyond the water ice line, are prime targets for DI or coronagraphy observations with JWST or ELT.

AGB-star Mass Transfer Products in Stellar Streams as Tracers of Density in Dissolved Star Clusters

Newlin C. Weatherford, Madeleine McKenzie, Sam A. Usman

10 pages, 3 figures, submitted to ApJL. Comments welcome

PDFFri 23 Oct
The least dense globular clusters (GCs) appear to host the highest fractions of stars with chemical signatures of mass transfer from asymptotic giant branch (AGB) companions. Recent observations suggest the fraction may be even higher in stellar streams that are debris of tidally disrupted (preferentially low-density) GCs. We explore whether this is attributable to lower encounter rates in less-dense GCs allowing more of the relevant binaries to survive dynamical hardening, or outright disruption, long enough to undergo AGB mass transfer. We run a small suite of GC simulations varying initial density using the code CMC with its latest observationally motivated binary prescriptions. Using CMC's built-in COSMIC code for binary stellar evolution, we first identify primordial binaries that would, if left unperturbed, likely undergo AGB mass transfer via either standard or wind Roche-lobe overflow. We then check how many of those binaries survive GC dynamics in CMC long enough to experience AGB mass transfer. We find that wind Roche-lobe overflow is necessary to reproduce the fraction of (sub)giant stars in Galactic GCs that are likely products of AGB mass transfer (~1%) and that binary dynamics plausibly explain why this fraction appears lower in dense, intact GCs than stellar streams. The abundance of mass-transfer products (also collision products and binaries) in upcoming surveys of stellar streams without known progenitors will thus probe the densities of dissolved GCs—difficult to determine otherwise but key to improved stream modeling and predicted rates of many astrophysical transients.

Granulation-driven spectral irradiance variability of Sun-like stars

N.-E. Nèmec, G. Kopp, V. Witzke, N. M. Kostogryz

under review (A&A), 7 pages, 6 Figures

PDFFri 23 Oct
Granulation,caused by convection in low-mass stars like the Sun, introduces small amplitude variations in the stellar brightness on timescales of minutes to hours. Often referred to as flicker or microvariability this signal can impede detecting Earth-sized planets around Sun-like stars via stellar transits. Stellar flicker can also introduce uncertainties in determining stellar fundamental parameters and cause errors of up to 10% in the radius determination of transiting exoplanets. Both sources of uncertainties will propagate into planetary transmission spectra and resulting atmospheric retrievals. We present a method for modelling stellar flicker that enables scaling the flicker of Sun-like stars between different wavelengths and passbands to facilitate better solar-stellar comparisons. We use the 3D radiative magnetohydrodynamic code MURaM to simulate the granulation pattern on a Sun-like star and employ the radiative transfer code MPS-ATLAS for the spectral synthesis of the MURaM output, producing lightcurves of the granulation signal in various wavelengths and at different disc positions. Using these synthetic lightcurves, we establish scaling relations in the time and frequency domain between different wavelengths and passbands routinely used in stellar observations, such as the Kepler and TESS space telescopes. We find that the variability caused by granulation is very well correlated across wavelengths from 300 to 6000 nm, enabling scalings from one wavelength regime to another. We present the scaling sensitivities in different passbands and wavelength regimes that can be used to facilitate solar-stellar comparisons and help estimate stellar flicker in observations using photometric passbands and low-resolution transmission spectra.

Distinct stellar-parameter dependencies of granulation and mesogranulation in red giants

Anohita Mallick, Jharnesh Verma, Shatanik Bhattacharya, Shravan M Hanasoge

15 pages, 6 figures, 5 tables, including appendix. Accepted for publication in the Astrophysical Journal Letters

PDFFri 23 Oct
Granulation and mesogranulation represent different spatial and temporal scales of convection, but are commonly assumed to follow the same stellar atmospheric scaling relations in red giants. We test this assumption using the largest sample assembled for this purpose to date – 9,328 Kepler red giants spanning the red giant branch and red clump phase, cross-matched with APOGEE stellar parameters. We model each star's power spectrum background to obtain the radius-corrected amplitude and characteristic frequency of both components, and derive empirical scaling relations with effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity through Bayesian model comparison. Gaussian Process and symbolic regression provide independent validation. We find that granulation and mesogranulation are not self-similar: their surface-gravity dependencies are statistically indistinguishable, consistent with a shared envelope geometry, but their temperature dependencies diverge: mesogranulation's amplitude rises steeply with Teff, whereas granulation's shows no statistically justified dependence, and both frequencies respond weakly and similarly. We propose that this pattern is consistent with granulation forming in a thin near-surface layer, where radiation takes over from convection and the Mach number depends only weakly on Teff. Mesogranulation originates deeper, where convection carries the full flux and its Mach number and cell size increase with Teff. Metallicity raises both amplitudes but has little effect on the frequencies. Our results indicate that the self-similarity assumption underlying two-component background models does not hold, with direct implications for background modeling and its downstream application in Galactic archaeology.

The LBT Y$_{\rm p}$ Project VI: Gas-Phase Chemical Abundance Patterns of Metal-Poor Nebulae

Noah S. J. Rogers, Evan D. Skillman, Richard W. Pogge, Erik Aver, Miqaela K. Weller +5

33 pages, 15 figures, 2 tables, submitted to The Astrophysical Journal. Comments welcome

PDFFri 23 Oct
Through a combined rest-optical and NIR observing campaign, the Large Binocular Telescope Primordial He Abundance ($Y_{\rm p}$) Project has delivered a precision measurement of $Y_{\rm p}$. Given the stringent requirements to measure gas-phase He abundances, the optical spectra contain emission features from a plethora of metals in the Interstellar Medium (ISM). We present gas-phase N, O, Ne, S, Cl, Ar, K and Fe abundance patterns in 62 low-metallicity nebulae. We assess ionization correction factors for each element and select those that reproduce the observed ionic abundance trends. The Ne/O, S/O, and Ar/O abundances do not evolve with metallicity and agree with the solar ratios. Given the Type Ia enrichment of S and Ar, the solar S/O and Ar/O suggest extended star formation histories in local galaxies relative to high-$z$ galaxies at fixed O/H. We report the first ISM K abundances in low-metallicity extragalactic nebulae; both Cl/O and K/O are constant with O/H, indicating the production of these odd-Z elements follows that of $\alpha$ elements. Consistent with prior analyses, gas-phase Fe/O is observed to decrease with O/H due to dust depletion; correcting for this produces an average [Fe/O]$=$$-$0.80, implying Fe-deficient stars capable of producing hard ionizing spectra. Finally, the metallicity-insensitive log(N/O) plateau from 50 nebulae is $-$1.43$\pm$0.10, consistent with previous works. Five galaxies have N/O significantly enhanced relative to the primary plateau, and we discuss their properties, mechanisms that could bias measurements of N/O, and nucleosynthetic progenitors that can produce enhanced N at low metallicity.

A 13 Year Black Hole Microlensing Survey in Omega Centauri

Zack Freeman, Maximilian Häberle, Anil Seth, John Zaris, Matthew Whitaker +9

17 pages, 17 figures, submitted to MNRAS

PDFFri 23 Oct
Star clusters are born with massive stars that leave behind a large population of stellar mass black holes. These black holes may account for a large fraction of binary black hole mergers seen in gravitational waves as the dense environment enables retention of black holes and the formation of binaries. However, direct measurements of stellar mass black holes in globular clusters are limited, with just a few found in binary systems thus far. In this paper, we examine the possibility of detecting black holes through microlensing in the Milky Way's most massive star cluster, Omega Centauri. We take advantage of a unique dataset of nearly 200 single-band images taken over 13 years with the Hubble Space Telescope to look for the $\sim$1 year long events expected from microlensing of cluster stars by stellar mass black holes. We develop a novel cost function to fit the lensing signatures that we verify using synthetic events, quantifying our completeness. These synthetic events are based on a recent Monte Carlo simulation of Omega Centauri. We also use these synthetic events to train a neural network to most efficiently distinguish observed lensing events. Application of this algorithm to our observed data, and subsequent visual follow-up, show no convincing candidate black hole lensing events. Based on analysis of the simulation data and our completeness, we find that no events would be expected in the data contrary to the expectations from earlier work. We release the photometric dataset used in this work for future use.

A Robust Estimator for the Lyman-$α$ Forest 1D Power Spectrum at High Redshift

Molly Wolfson, Joseph F. Hennawi, Frederick B. Davies, Zarija Lukić

30 pages, 13 figures

PDFFri 23 Oct
The Lyman-$\alpha$ (Ly$\alpha$) forest flux power spectrum $P(k)$ is sensitive to a range of astrophysical and cosmological effects, including the thermal state of and large-scale fluctuations in the intergalactic medium and the nature of dark matter. Measuring $P(k)$ becomes increasingly challenging at high redshift, where fewer quasar spectra are available and higher Ly$\alpha$ optical depths result in lower transmitted flux. In addition, observational effects and missing data complicate standard Fourier-transform estimators. Here, we validate a likelihood-based estimator for $P(k)$. The method constructs the pixel-level covariance matrix of the transmitted flux field using precomputed $k$-space integrals that account for instrumental resolution and pixelization, enabling direct maximum-likelihood inference of $P(k)$. We test the estimator using mock Ly$\alpha$ forest spectra at $z \geq 5$, including analytic Gaussian realizations generated from known power spectra and forward-modeled simulation spectra incorporating observational effects relevant to high-redshift quasar surveys, such as finite spectral resolution, pixel sampling, noise, and missing data. We compare the recovered power spectra with both the input spectra and standard Fourier-transform estimates. We find that the likelihood-based estimator recovers the input power spectrum across the tested scales well within expected error and remains robust to the observational effects considered. This work establishes a framework for measuring Ly$\alpha$ forest clustering with a robust, nearly optimal power spectrum estimator, with particular focus on quantifying large-scale fluctuations that may arise from ultraviolet background fluctuations or the late stages of reionization.

Qiaoya Wu, Yue Shen

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) is carrying out a five-year spectroscopic survey designed to map the large-scale structure of the Universe and obtain spectra for millions of galaxies and quasars over a wide redshift range. We present a value-added catalog of spectral properties for 1,717,774 unique quasars (1,774,723 spectra) in the DESI Data Release 1. We perform a uniform spectral decomposition of the quasar continuum, host galaxy emission (at $z<1$), and \FeII emission, and measure the line properties of major broad and narrow emission lines from Ly$\alpha$ to H$\alpha$ covered by DESI spectra. We also provide single-epoch black hole mass estimates (from H$\beta$, \MgII, and \CIV), bolometric luminosities, and Eddington ratios for these quasars. We show that several emission-line ratios, including those involving coronal lines, vary systematically with Eddington ratio. The catalog is intended as a community resource for studies of quasar demographics, black hole accretion, emission-line physics, and the connection between the broad-, coronal-, and narrow-line regions.

Stellar oscillations and granulation are key probes of stellar convection, one of the most poorly understood processes in stars. Magnetic Activity as a Driver of Low-gravity-like Spectral Morphology in Late-M Dwarfs

Chia-Lung Lin, Daniel Apai, Benjamin V. Rackham, Prajwal Niraula, Mark S. Giampapa +5

39 pages, 13 figures, 10 tables

PDFFri 23 Oct
We present a systematic investigation of the connection between magnetic activity and the low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphology of late-M dwarfs. Our sample includes TRAPPIST-1 observed with JWST NIRISS/SOSS and 22 late-M dwarfs observed with IRTF/SpeX, together with flare observations from K2, TESS, JWST NIRISS/SOSS, and literature-reported X-ray luminosities. Using one-component PHOENIX/NewEra spectral modeling, we find that all targets are better reproduced by lower-surface-gravity model spectra than by models with the expected high gravities of field late-M dwarfs. The retrieved surface gravities moderately correlate with independently measured gravity-sensitive spectral indices, with the strongest trends seen for FeH$_z$ at 0.998~$\mu$m and FeH$_J$ at 1.2~$\mu$m, both showing statistically significant Pearson and Spearman correlations with $p<0.005$. They also show strong anti-correlations with flare luminosity and X-ray luminosity: for both relations, Pearson and Spearman coefficients are $<-0.9$, with $p \leq 1.0\times10^{-4}$, indicating that more active stars tend to have spectra favoring lower-gravity morphologies. Time-series spectra of TRAPPIST-1 during two large flares show no clear short-timescale variations in selected photospheric indices, suggesting that persistent small-scale magnetic activity may play a more important role than large flares in modifying the photospheric temperature–pressure structure. A simplified radiative-transfer experiment further shows that activity-associated heating can produce lower-gravity-like spectral features even when the input surface gravity is fixed. Our results suggest that underestimated surface gravities in late-M dwarfs may reflect atmospheres modified by frequent, small-scale magnetic activity, such as microflares, producing emergent spectra that mimic lower-gravity morphology.

Slow Massive-Star Formation and the O-Star ZAMS Deficit

Paolo Padoan, Samuel Shipkoff, Mark Gieles, Fabrice Martins

Submitted to MNRAS Letters

PDFFri 23 Oct
The scarcity of optically visible O stars near the theoretical zero-age main sequence (ZAMS) has puzzled astronomers for over four decades. We argue that it reflects prolonged accretion. If massive stars assemble over Myr timescales, as the inertial-inflow model predicts, core-hydrogen burning begins before accretion ends, and the stars become optically visible at the end of accretion on a birthline displaced from the standard ZAMS. We test this interpretation by comparing the hot-side envelope of Galactic O stars in the Kiel diagram with accreting stellar-evolution models, adopting a growth law $t_{\mathrm{form}}=\tau_0(m_{\mathrm{f}}/60 M_\odot)^\alpha$ with formation time $t_{\mathrm{form}}$ and final mass $m_{\mathrm{f}}$. The resulting birthlines reproduce both the displacement and the high-mass turnover of the observed envelope, with best-fitting parameters $(\alpha,\tau_0)=(0.52,4.3\,\mathrm{Myr})$, consistent with independent constraints from the luminosity functions of compact H II regions and OB stars. The two constraints have orthogonal degeneracy directions, so combining them narrows the allowed parameter space, yielding projected 90% confidence intervals $0.43<\alpha<0.60$ and $3.9<\tau_0/\mathrm{Myr}<5.0$. The inferred growth law agrees with cloud-scale simulations and the inertial-inflow model, and implies that mass assembly and main-sequence evolution overlap substantially in massive stars, with broader implications for stellar evolution and feedback.

A cosmological intercept tension in local distance ladders

Haotian Dong, Marek Biesiada, Zhoujian Cao, Shao-Jiang Wang

7 pages, 2 figures

PDFFri 23 Oct
The Hubble tension motivates direct tests of the internal consistency of the local distance ladder. We perform forward and backward consistency checks between Type Ia supernovae in Cepheid host galaxies and the Hubble-flow supernova sample using Pantheon+ data. Within flat $\Lambda$CDM, we impose Planck-CMB priors on one sample, infer the supernova absolute-magnitude calibration, and propagate it to the other sample to recover the Hubble constant, which should match the input value if the second and third rungs of the distance ladder align perfectly. We examine the impact of host-galaxy peculiar velocities using redshift corrections based on the Manticore-Local reconstruction. These corrections shift the recovered $H_0$ from $74.30^{+1.18}_{-1.20}$ to $71.37^{+1.23}_{-1.18}$ $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$ in the forward analysis and from $60.83^{+0.98}_{-0.97}$ to $63.25^{+0.95}_{-0.93}$ $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}$ in the backward analysis, bringing both results closer to the input CMB constraint. Comparing the cosmological intercepts of the magnitude-redshift relation inferred from two rungs of the distance ladder before and after peculiar-velocity corrections, we find that the significance of the discrepancy decreases from $6.8\sigma$ to $3.9\sigma$ in the forward check and from $7.3\sigma$ to $4.3\sigma$ in the backward check. Peculiar-velocity corrections therefore partially alleviate the intercept tension, while a residual discrepancy persists within the adopted framework. Our results highlight the importance of local velocity modeling and motivate further tests of sample selection and calibration systematics in assessing distance-ladder consistency.

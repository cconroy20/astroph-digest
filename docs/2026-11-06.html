The Pristine survey XXXI. A stream offset from the Stream: very metal-poor stellar debris trailing 100$^{\circ}$ from the LMC-SMC system

Akshara Viswanathan, Kim A. Venn, Julio F. Navarro, Daniel A. Boyea, Federico Sestito +21

Submitted to A&A. 12 pages + 7 pages appendix (10 Figures, 3 Tables). Comments are very welcome! Note: Abstract is shortened to comply with the arXiv character limit

PDFFri 6 Nov
Context: The stellar counterpart to the $200^\circ$-long HI Stream from the Clouds probes their tidally stripped stellar populations and constrains their interaction history and structure before infall into the Milky Way. Aims: We investigate the chemodynamics and possible origin of very metal-poor (VMP; [Fe/H] $<-2$) stars in the stellar Stream, targeting distant red giants selected from the Pristine–Gaia synthetic metallicity catalogue. Methods: We obtained high-resolution Gemini/GHOST spectroscopy of 33 candidate members, deriving radial velocities and metallicities. We combine these data with literature red giant members and assess their association with the Clouds using orbital integrations in a time-dependent Milky Way–LMC potential. We also compare their distribution with collisionless test particles initially placed in a diffuse LMC stellar halo. Results: We identify 12 high-confidence members based on energy and angular momentum: one intermediate metal-poor star ([Fe/H] $\sim-1.6$), ten VMP stars, and one extremely metal-poor (EMP) star ([Fe/H] $<-3$). The new VMP stars trace over $100^\circ$ of the trailing Stream, follow the Clouds' kinematics, and lie $\sim20^\circ$ from both the HI Stream and previously known, more metal-rich debris, suggesting a metallicity-dependent spatial distribution. Combined with the literature, they approximately double the known red giant sample and extend its metallicity range from [Fe/H] $\sim-0.5$ to $-4.3$. Orbital integrations support an association with the Clouds, while test particles reproduce the debris' broad spatial distribution. Conclusions: VMP and EMP stars contribute to the Clouds' tidal debris. They may originate in the extended stellar halos of the LMC or SMC, or in disrupted satellites. Their spatial and kinematic properties can constrain future simulations of the Milky Way–LMC–SMC interaction.

The Composition of M-dwarf Exoplanet Hosts: Atmospheric Parameters and O, Mg, Si, and Fe Abundances from IR Spectroscopy

Zachary G Maas, Natalie R. Hinkel, Gregory N. Mace, Cayman T. Unterborn, Catherine Pilachowski +3

23 pages, 11 figures, accepted for publication in The Astrophysical Journal

PDFFri 6 Nov
Determining M-dwarf abundances is an important step to discerning the chemical evolution of the Milky Way and characterizing exoplanetary systems, especially since these objects commonly host small exoplanets. However, a dearth of M-dwarf abundance measurements exist compared to Sun-like stars, partially due to their complicated optical spectra dominated by molecular absorption lines and low luminosities. To overcome these issues, we have obtained high resolution H-band spectra of 40 planet-hosting M-dwarf stars and 2 M-dwarfs in wide binaries with FGK primaries with IGRINS on Gemini South and on the McDonald Observatory 2.7m telescope, and NIRSPEC on Keck. Abundances were derived by comparing each observed spectrum to synthetic spectral grids generated with MOOG and MARCS atmospheric models. Effective temperatures were derived using OH and H$_{2}$O lines and [Fe/H] from Fe I and FeH lines. Both Mg and Si abundances were determined using atomic features. Overall, we find that our abundances are broadly consistent with previous literature M-dwarf abundances. We complemented our abundances analysis with UVW velocities and Milky Way stellar population membership probabilities and identified 2 potential thick disk stars from our kinematic and chemical analysis: GJ 1252 and L181-1 which was a previously known thick disk star. Kinematic stellar population probabilities and a low [Fe/H] abundance makes Kepler 42 a likely thick disk star, although uncertain Mg and Si abundances make classification difficult. Lastly, we have determined Si/Mg and Fe/Mg molar ratios for M-dwarf exoplanet hosts and find the abundance distributions leads to exoplanet core-mass fractions between 20 - 40$\%$.

On the importance of medium-resolution continuum spectroscopy for JWST's extragalactic legacy

Adam C. Carnall, Ho-Hin Leung, Struan D. Stevenson, Elizabeth Taylor, Abdurro'uf +79

White paper submitted to the "Multi-Cycle Treasury Program Concepts with JWST" call, Nov 2026

PDFFri 6 Nov
We argue for a significant investment of JWST time into an extremely deep medium-resolution NIRSpec MSA survey targeting a large, representative sample of high-redshift $(z>1.5)$ galaxies, specifically intended to obtain high-SNR continuum detections. This is only possible with JWST, and no planned future instrumentation has similar capabilities, meaning that obtaining such observations with JWST must be urgently prioritised. The vast majority of existing archival NIRSpec observations are not deep enough for continuum science, being focused on the detection of emission lines. Deeper continuum data opens up the ability to constrain a much wider range of key galaxy physical parameters, including stellar population ages and metallicities, stellar kinematics and neutral gas physical properties, inflows and outflows. Such measurements will provide crucial insight into quenching physics, galaxy dynamical masses, merger histories, pristine early star-formation, reionization and the physics of Little Red Dots, as well as enabling all of the emission-line science that can be achieved with shallower observations. To facilitate future survey planning, we present an empirically derived relationship between rest-optical continuum SNR in JWST NIRSpec medium-resolution data, rest-optical apparent magnitude and exposure time, which we show to be independent of redshift (and hence the grating used). We then provide a 1000-hour sketch survey plan based on achieving the continuum SNR $=20$/pixel necessary to constrain key physical properties and scaling relations for a large galaxy sample at $z>1.5$. We finally discuss NIRSpec sky subtraction strategies, which are of critical importance for continuum SNR.

Dust Survival in Lensed Quiescent Galaxies at $z>1$ Depends on How They Quench

Sarah Bodansky, Katherine E. Whitaker, Gourav Khullar, Michael D. Gladders, Keren Sharon +16

Submitted to ApJ. 20 pages, 11 figures

PDFFri 6 Nov
The interstellar medium of massive quiescent galaxies holds key clues to how they quench, yet remain poorly understood. Although dust is expected to be depleted rapidly ($\lesssim$100 Myr) after quenching, some quiescent galaxies host substantial dust reservoirs, raising the question of whether dust survives quenching or is replenished afterward. We present ALMA Band 6 continuum observations ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}$$>$250$\mu$m) of eight strongly-lensed, spectroscopically-selected quiescent galaxies at z$\sim$1-2.5 from the COOL-LAMPS and REQUIEM surveys, plus five archival REQUIEM systems. The sample spans more than two orders of magnitude in dust-to-stellar-mass ratio ($f_{\mathrm{dust}}<10^{-5}$ to $>10^{-3}$), and two galaxies host extended, resolved dust. Comparing $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ with time since quenching, roughly half the sample, preferentially at higher redshift, is consistent with rapid quenching and harsh dust destruction, while lower-redshift, more slowly quenched systems are consistent with milder destruction, possibly reflecting an evolving dominant quenching mode. The two most dust-rich galaxies exceed even the mildest model destruction tracks, with J1201-3952 retaining the highest $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ $\sim$1.8 Gyr after quenching. If these systems are truly quiescent, their dust masses approach maximum stellar yields, pointing to ISM grain regrowth and/or TP-AGB dust production. However due to our single-band continuum data, $f_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is uncertain ($\pm$0.3 dex systematic uncertainty from the unknown dust temperature) and we cannot rule out obscured star formation in these two galaxies. Our results suggest that dust survival depends on how galaxies quench: rapid, harsh quenching efficiently destroys dust, while slower, milder quenching can leave dust reservoirs that persist or are replenished long after star formation ceases.

DOWSING, Dust Observations With SCUBA-2 In Nearby Galaxies: I. Dust in M81

M. Uzuner, M. W. L. Smith, S. Eales, A. Chung, E. J. Chung +14

18 pages, 21 figures. Accepted for publication in MNRAS on 2 November 2026

PDFFri 6 Nov
We present a resolved far-infrared/sub-millimetre study of dust in the nearby spiral galaxy M81 using Herschel PACS/SPIRE and JCMT SCUBA-2 observations from 70 to $850\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. We model the dust emission using both single-temperature modified blackbody (SMBB) fits and point process mapping (PPMAP), comparing pixel-by-pixel SED-fitting with image cubes of dust surface density as a function of position and temperature. For fixed $\beta = 1.9$ and a dust opacity of $\kappa_{350\,\mu\mathrm{m}} = 1.92\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}\,\mathrm{g}^{-1}$, SMBB gives an integrated dust mass of $(1.77 \pm 0.09) \times 10^{7}\,M_\odot$, while PPMAP gives $(2.65 \pm 0.13) \times10^{7}\,M_\odot$. The higher PPMAP mass is expected because real lines of sight contain dust over a range of temperatures, whereas SMBB assumes a single effective temperature and can underestimate the dust mass. Both methods show enhanced dust surface densities along the spiral arms and a radially declining dust temperature, with the warmest dust in the central region. Variable-$\beta$ fits indicate only moderate spatial variation in $\beta$ for well-constrained pixels, but the strong $T$-$\beta$ degeneracy motivates a fixed emissivity index for the main analysis. Combining the dust maps with HI data, we find a positive radial gas-to-dust ratio gradient, with $d\log_{10}(\mathrm{GDR})/dR = 0.065\,\mathrm{dex}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$. Comparison with M81's metallicity gradient implies a declining dust-to-metals ratio, potentially consistent with more efficient grain growth in the inner disc. The star formation rate is modest, $\sim 0.27\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, and is strongly concentrated along the spiral arms. These results suggest that the dust, gas, and star-forming structures of M81 are strongly shaped by its spiral arms, while the central region hosts the warmest dust in the disc.

Is Morphology All You Need? Quantifying Imprints of Past Star Formation in the Present-Day Morphologies of Galaxies

Juan Pablo Alfonzo, Kartheik G. Iyer, Masayuki Akiyama, Camilla Pacifici, Abdurro'uf +8

36 pages, 16 figures. Submitted to ApJ

PDFFri 6 Nov
The next generation of galaxy surveys will bring a plethora of new observations, a vast majority of which will be observed with a few photometric broadbands, limiting the information we can infer from traditional SED fitting methods. To maximize the scientific value of these surveys, techniques that can go beyond traditional summary statistics in extracting information from galaxy observations are needed. Moving beyond integrated photometry, we introduce the Katachi-PRIME method to do spectral energy distribution (SED) based inference that jointly incorporates morphological and SED information. Katachi-PRIME uses implicit likelihood inference (ILI) to incorporate realistic morphological priors from gri broadband images, to make predictions of: stellar mass (M$_*$), specific star formation rate (sSFR), redshift (z), age (t$_{50}$), dust attenuation (Av), and gas-phase metallicity (12+log(O/H)) of the galaxy. We find that this improves constraints compared to traditional 5-band photometry. We then use the learned morphological priors to examine the extent to which morphology encodes information about past star formation, finding a stark dichotomy in how morphology encodes star formation history. While the stable morphologies of main-sequence and quiescent galaxies can predict past star formation out to $\sim 4-7$ Gyr and $\sim3-5$ Gyr, respectively, starburst and green-valley populations above and below the main sequence tend to experience structural disruptions that obscure all but the most recent star formation activity. Methods like Katachi-PRIME maximize the scientific value of future telescopes by providing broad-baseline SED-level property estimates by incorporating morphological information.

Survey of Surveys Data Release 3. Homogenised stellar parameters across nine spectroscopic surveys

A. Avdeeva, E. Pancino, A. Turchi, P. M. Marrese, S. Marinoni +10

Submitted to A&A. 20 pages, 17 figures, 5 tables

PDFFri 6 Nov
Large spectroscopic surveys have measured atmospheric parameters for millions of Milky Way stars, but differences in instruments, wavelength ranges, analysis methods, and assumptions introduce survey-dependent systematics that complicate their joint use. We aim to place effective temperature, surface gravity, and metallicity estimates from major spectroscopic surveys onto a common, externally validated scale, and to provide a homogenised catalogue for large-scale Galactic studies. We construct the calibration hierarchically. We first define an internal reference scale from the best-populated and best-overlapped surveys (APOGEE, GALAH, and LAMOST LRS), and model survey-to-reference corrections and their uncertainties with machine-learning regression. The scale is then extended to LAMOST MRS, Gaia RVS, RAVE, DESI, Gaia-ESO, and SEGUE, and anchored to high-quality measurements from the PASTEL database. The calibration reduces survey-to-survey offsets and improves consistency in independent overlap samples: independent calibration of GALAH and LAMOST LRS temperatures onto the APOGEE scale reduces their median offset from 44.5 K to 6.2 K. The final catalogue contains about 10.13 million stars, including 6.10 million in the recommended high-quality subset and 3.69 million in the metallicity-optimised subset. For the high-quality subset, Gaia benchmark stars give median residuals of 30 K in $T_{\rm eff}$, 0.03 dex in $\log g$, and $-0.02$ dex in [Fe/H]. The [Fe/H]-optimised subset gives median residuals of 0.003 dex against Gaia benchmark stars, 0.05 dex for globular clusters, and $-0.02$ dex for open clusters. As an example application, we confirm the presence of metal-poor stars on thin-disc-like orbits, including stars with [Fe/H] $< -2$, showing that the catalogue can be used to study rare Galactic populations on a consistent multi-survey scale.

Thorium and Europium production in the early Galaxy: Implications for nucleocosmochronology

L. Magrini, R. E. Giribaldi, M. Palla, S. Randich, L. Pasquini +15

15 pages, accepted for publication in A&A

PDFFri 6 Nov
The ages of the oldest stellar populations can be investigated through nucleocosmochronology, with the thorium-to-europium abundance ratio providing a promising chronometer. We assess the reliability of log(Th/Eu) as an age indicator by testing whether thorium and europium were co-produced in a constant ratio in metal-poor stars, and evaluate the uncertainties affecting stellar ages. We present new Th and Eu abundance measurements for eight TITANS reference stars observed with UVES at the VLT. Combining these measurements with homogeneous literature samples, we investigate the behaviour of log(Th/Eu) as a function of metallicity. We derive ages adopting a theoretical initial r-process production ratio and assess the effects of observational uncertainties and sample statistics. At low metallicity, the observed Th/Eu ratios show no significant dependence on metallicity after excluding stars with anomalous actinide abundances, supporting broadly uniform Th and Eu co-production in the early Galaxy. The derived ages are consistent with expectations for an old, metal-poor halo population, although individual uncertainties remain substantial. Higher spectral resolution is needed to improve Th measurements, while combining stars in coeval populations can provide additional statistical gains. Nucleocosmochronology offers an independent approach to dating the oldest stellar populations. Homogeneous samples of coeval stars, particularly in globular clusters, observed with future high-resolution multi-object spectrographs could provide stronger constraints on the early formation history of the Milky Way and the age of the Universe.

Cepheid Metallicity in the Leavitt Law (C-MetaLL) survey: X First spectroscopic characterization of Galactic Type II Cepheids and calibration of the Period-Wesenheit-Metallicity relations

T. Sicignano, M. Romaniello, A. Bhardwaj, V. Ripepi, M. Rejkuba +11

Article proposed for acceptance in Astronomy & Astrophysics. 12 pages, 8 figures, 8 tables

PDFFri 6 Nov
Type II Cepheids (T2Cs) are Population II pulsating stars that serve as distance indicators through their period–luminosity relation. Along with RR Lyrae stars and the tip of the red giant branch, they offer an independent route to the Hubble constant, helping assess systematic uncertainties in its determination. We aim to investigate the impact of chemical composition on T2Cs' period–luminosity relations by building a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of Galactic T2Cs with iron and oxygen abundances. We observed 80 Galactic T2Cs with UVES at the ESO Very Large Telescope. For 74 stars, we derived stellar parameters and iron and oxygen abundances through a self-consistent spectroscopic analysis based on equivalent widths of absorption lines. This sample nearly doubles the number of T2Cs with spectroscopic metallicity measurements available in the literature. We used these abundances to test theoretical predictions for the instability strip and, together with $Gaia$ parallaxes, to derive Period–Wesenheit–metallicity (PWZ) relations. In agreement with theoretical predictions, the metallicity coefficient ($\gamma$) is small and statistically consistent with zero in all relations, ranging from approximately $-0.02$ to $+0.17$ mag dex$^{-1}$. We also estimated distances to 22 Galactic Globular Clusters hosting T2Cs, finding agreement within uncertainties with independent literature estimates. Our results show that metallicity has, at most, a minor impact on T2Cs' pulsation properties and distance scale, supporting their reliability as Population II distance indicators.

The Milky Way-like radial structure in nearby galaxies

Yuze Zhao, Jianhui Lian, Zheng Zheng, Cuihuan Li, Jie Song +14

PDFFri 6 Nov
The Milky Way exhibits a distinctive plateau in its inner surface brightness profile around one effective radius, challenging the traditional view of a purely exponential disk. To explore the origin of this feature, we identify 65 nearby galaxies ($<50$ Mpc) with similar plateau structures and analyze their multi-band surface brightness and color profiles using SDSS, DESI, 2MASS, and Mephisto data. Of the 65 galaxies with Milky Way-like plateaus, 42 have bar classifications (25 barred, 17 non-barred). Barred galaxies show radial color variations more consistent with stellar population ageing, while non-barred galaxies align more with the dust reddening direction, suggesting dust attenuation contributes to plateau formation in non-barred systems. The broad range of color gradients among barred galaxies suggests bars influence plateaus through multiple pathways, including stellar population variations and dynamical stellar-mass redistribution. Environment may also contribute: both barred and non-barred galaxies tend to reside in denser environments than a comparison sample without plateaus. The low occurrence rate ($\sim$3.6%) may indicate that plateau structures mark a relatively uncommon evolutionary stage. The Milky Way, with both strong stellar population dependence and a persistent stellar mass profile feature, may represent an intermediate case where both stellar population variations and dynamical processes associated with the bar shape its plateau.

The ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST Survey: The Tully-Fisher Relation at $z = 4-6$

Diego A. Gómez-Espinoza, Juan Molina, Edo Ibar, Manuel Aravena, Roberto J. Assef +25

13 pages, 6 Figures, 2 Tables, Accepted for Publication in A&A

PDFFri 6 Nov
The Tully-Fisher relation provides a fundamental link between galaxy mass and their kinematics. Although its evolution is relatively well constrained up to cosmic noon, observational probes at higher redshifts remain scarce. In this work, we investigate the stellar and baryonic Tully-Fisher relations (TFRs) at $z=4-6$ by analyzing JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations of 18 main-sequence star-forming galaxies belonging to the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST survey. We apply a morpho-kinematic disk classification scheme, finding five disk-like galaxies and a survey disk fraction of $0.28\pm0.11$. These disk galaxies span stellar masses of $10^{9.5-10.5}\,M_\odot$, intrinsic rotational velocities of $91-127\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, and intrinsic velocity dispersions of $66-129\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. For both versions of the TFR, we find that the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST disks lie below the local relations and are likely offset from those reported at cosmic noon ($z\approx2.3$). By fixing the TFR slope to the $z=0$ value, we measure zero-point offsets for the stellar-mass TFR of $-0.96\pm0.18$ dex and $-0.50\pm0.18$ dex relative to the local Universe and cosmic noon, respectively. For the baryonic TFR, we find zero-point offsets of $-0.57\pm0.20$ dex and $-0.30\pm0.20$ dex with respect to the local Universe and cosmic noon relations. Our measurements are consistent with the redshift evolution of the zero-points of both TFRs being driven by the evolution of dark matter halo properties with cosmic time, modulated by a reduced contribution of dark matter to the dynamical mass on the galaxy scale.

Feng-Yuan Frey Liu, Derek J. McLeod, Xueqing Chen, Ben J. Sutlieff, Ho-Hin Leung +10

36 pages (including 9 pages of appendices), 18 figures, 11 tables, submitted to MNRAS

PDFFri 6 Nov
We present an analysis of JADES-GS-BD-5, an extremely cold Y dwarf identified in the JADES/GOODS-South field. Using multi-epoch JWST/NIRCam imaging spanning a $\simeq2$-yr baseline, we calibrate the JWST astrometry and obtain the first robust parallax measurement for a brown dwarf discovered in a JWST extragalactic deep field. We measure a proper motion of $\mu_{\rm tot}=49.8^{+0.3}_{-0.3}$ mas yr$^{-1}$ and a parallax of $\varpi=15.7^{+1.4}_{-1.3}$ mas, corresponding to a geometric distance of $d=64^{+6}_{-5}$ pc, $\sim3\times$ further than that of the previously known most distant Y dwarfs with parallax-measured distances. We hence determine a tangential velocity of $v_{\rm tan}=15.0^{+1.4}_{-1.3}$ km s$^{-1}$, consistent with a Galactic thin-disk object. We find no evidence for binary acceleration, significant photometric variability, or a plausible host. The $J$-band spectral morphology of JADES-GS-BD-5 is consistent with existing Y-dwarf templates, while its extremely faint luminosity and overall near-infrared spectral profile confirm that it is one of the coldest dwarfs discovered to date. We therefore adopt a classification of $>$Y1, consistent with a NIRCam colour-based proxy of cY1.5. The robust parallax-determined distance allows us to determine its bolometric luminosity, $\log(L_{\rm bol}/L_{\odot})=-7.08^{+0.09}_{-0.09}$. Evolutionary modelling places JADES-GS-BD-5 in the planetary-mass regime and makes it a rare benchmark for the study of cold giant-planet atmospheres. Current atmospheric models fail to reproduce its SED in detail, pointing to the importance of disequilibrium chemistry and vertical mixing. Finally, we also report additional proper motion measurements for eight of the other nine known JADES Y-dwarf candidates, highlighting JWST extragalactic surveys as a powerful route to building a statistically useful, parallax-calibrated sample of Y dwarfs.

We present CosmicGrain, a dust physics module built on Gadget-4 that models explicit dust superparticles that experience resolved gas-dust drag and radiation pressure, undergo collisional heating and cooling with gas, and are further shaped by subgrid treatments for grain growth, thermal sputtering, coagulation, shattering, supernova shock destruction, and astration. GetGaia 2.0: automatic membership and systemic motions of stellar systems with Gaia

Andrés del Pino

22 pages, 17 figures, 8 tables. Submitted to A&A. The code will be available at this https URL

PDFFri 6 Nov
We present GetGaia 2.0, a public Python package that retrieves the Gaia data around a stellar system and selects its member stars automatically. The membership is a probabilistic model fitted by expectation maximization that combines the astrometry of every source, with its full covariance, the color-magnitude diagram, the sky distribution and, when available, the line-of-sight velocities, from Gaia or from an external catalog. The local systematic errors of the Gaia reference frame are measured on the quasars of each field. The code returns the systemic proper motion (PM) and parallax with their full covariance, a detection significance, a contamination estimate and the structural parameters of the system. We validate it on synthetic catalogs, where it recovers the members with 90-100 percent purity, and run it on Gaia DR3 for the known dwarf galaxies of the Local Group within 850 kpc and for 95 Galactic globular clusters, in tiers that add one piece of literature information at a time. It measures the systemic PMs of 55 dwarf galaxies and 93 globular clusters at a significance of at least $3\sigma$; 29 of the dwarfs and 86 of the clusters are recovered from their position alone. The dwarfs include every Milky Way satellite within 300 kpc brighter than $M_V=-5.4$, ultra-faint systems down to $M_V=-0.8$, and nine galaxies at 370-820 kpc. The PMs agree with the published values within 0.1 mas/yr for 47 of the dwarfs and for all the clusters (median differences of 0.032 and 0.009 mas/yr), with uncertainties equal to those of dedicated studies, and none deviates by more than $3\sigma$. The Gaia line-of-sight velocities alone give the systemic velocity of 71 clusters, within 1 km/s of spectroscopy. GetGaia 2.0 delivers, without manual intervention, the systemic motions and the members of the stellar systems within the reach of Gaia, and is ready for the forthcoming Gaia data releases.

We present the results of multi-epoch very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) observations of the sub-parsec-scale northern jet component of 3C 84 (known as the N1 component) at 7 mm from 2016 to 2021. Although a linear fit yields a small proper motion of $\mu_{\rm{Dec}}=0.054\pm0.019~\rm{mas}~\rm{yr}^{-1}$, corresponding to an apparent velocity of $\beta_{\rm{app}}=0.062\pm0.021$, this displacement is comparable to the observed positional scatter, and the hypothesis that N1 exhibits systematic motion is not statistically supported.

What observations are needed to advance our theoretical understanding of galaxy formation at high redshift?

H. Katz, R. S. Somerville, R. Schneider, B. E. Robertson, F. Pacucci +96

Theory white paper on high-redshift galaxy formation submitted to the STScI call for Multi-Cycle Treasury concept white papers as part of the Genesis Initiative (this https URL)

PDFTue 10 Nov
Although significant progress has been made over recent decades, a comprehensive and predictive framework for galaxy formation remains elusive. Starting from cosmological initial conditions, current models cannot a priori accurately predict key galaxy properties such as their structure, size, star formation histories, chemical abundance patterns, or supermassive black hole mass. This challenge stems from the vast range of spatial scales involved, as numerical simulations must simultaneously account for sub-AU processes nearby stars and black holes in addition to Mpc-scale cosmological gas inflows/outflows and environmental effects. Consequently, simulations must rely on subgrid models and approximations. As a result, theoretical progress in galaxy formation remains fundamentally driven by observational data and the iterative testing of model predictions against empirical results. Here we highlight five key open problems in theoretical galaxy formation that require dedicated observations of the high-redshift Universe, beyond what can currently be achieved through GO programs.

A DOLPHOT/Euclid Study of the Resolved, Faint Outer Milky Way Globular Cluster AM-1

Daniel R. Weisz, Alessandro Savino, Andrew E. Dolphin, Meredith J. Durbin, Abigail J. Lee +1

Accepted for publication in PASP. 24 pages, 12 figures, 3 tables. Photometry code and PSFs available at this http URL

PDFTue 10 Nov
We present a resolved star study of AM-1, a distant ($D \simeq 120$ kpc), faint ($M_V \sim -5.1$) Milky Way (MW) globular cluster, based on Euclid Quick Data Release 1 (Q1) imaging. To perform photometry of the Euclid VIS and NISP imaging, we develop a Euclid-specific module for the crowded field stellar photometry package DOLPHOT. We construct and present empirical point spread function (PSF) models for VIS and NISP, along with a photometric calibration, entirely from the public Q1 images. Our photometry reaches $5\sigma$ point source depths of $I_E, Y_E, J_E, H_E = 27.1, 25.3, 25.5, 25.3$, $\sim$1-1.5 mag deeper than the public Q1 catalog, as expected for dedicated resolved star PSF photometry. We resolve the cluster to $\sim$1 mag below its main sequence turnoff in all bands. The Euclid photometry agrees with color-magnitude diagram (CMD) modeling of archival HST/WFPC2 imaging and with the 2MASS-anchored VISTA Hemisphere Survey to $\lesssim0.02$ mag in $I_E$, $J_E$, and $H_E$, with a $\sim$0.07 mag offset in $Y_E$. We find that AM-1 hosts an old ($\sim$10.6 Gyr), intermediate-metallicity ([Fe/H] $\sim -1$) population, with a present-day stellar mass of $\sim 2\times10^4\,M_\odot$ and $M/L_V \simeq 2.2$, and has a half-light radius of $\sim$12-16 pc with a round, low-concentration profile. These affirm earlier studies placing AM-1 among the most extended, diffuse outer-halo clusters, which dynamical work identifies as accreted. We describe and publicly release DOLPHOT v2.1, the Euclid module, and its empirical PSFs, which together enable Euclid resolved star studies ranging from faint MW systems such as AM-1 to more luminous galaxies throughout the Local Volume. We anticipate further refinements, including spatially varying PSFs, with Euclid Data Release 1.

Investigating the timescales traced by dynamical ages in young stellar systems

Isabella Armstrong, Alison Sills, Claude Cournoyer-Cloutier

Accepted for publication in ApJ. 15 pages, 8 figures

PDFTue 10 Nov
Stars form in clustered environments, and then the stars may congregate into star clusters. The timescale on which individual stars form is not necessarily the same as the timescale on which star clusters assemble. Therefore, developing methods to probe these different timescales is a crucial part of understanding star cluster assembly and early evolution. Dynamical ages can be constructed from the kinematics of individual stars in clusters in the solar neighborhood using Gaia data. Using a star formation simulation, we test five different dynamical age methods. We divide dynamical ages into two approaches: methods which trace the size evolution of the cluster, and methods which trace the trajectories of individual stars. We confirm that size methods cannot accurately derive ages in bound clusters whereas methods tracing individual stars can be applied to both bound and unbound systems. We find that different dynamical age methods trace different epochs of star cluster formation and assembly: The method using stars with the oldest traceback ages provides an upper limit on stellar ages and the method using the median traceback ages is the best proxy for isochronal ages. Methods which trace the size evolution of the cluster best trace the onset of cluster expansion. Our work suggests that the median traceback time of stars moving away from the cluster provides the most reliable proxy for isochronal age, and that this method can be applied to any expanding system.

Tidally-induced vertical asymmetries in the LMC bar and disk

Shuyu Wang, Marie Schölch, Mercè Romero-Gómez, Óscar Jiménez-Arranz, Anthony G.A. Brown

16 pages, 24 figures, accepted by A&A

PDFTue 10 Nov
Past observations and simulations have shown that the bar in the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) may be tilted with respect to the disk plane, and that the outer LMC disk could be warped. These studies propose that the tilted bar and the warp in the LMC are induced by the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) pericentric passages. We analyze and quantify the effect of the SMC passage on the vertical position and kinematic asymmetries of both the bar and disk, as a function of different impact parameters or initial conditions. We used seven representative simulations from the KRATOS suite of $N$-body simulations of the LMC, SMC and Milky Way (MW) system for this analysis. To classify bar particles and analyze the bar and disk separately, we adopted a classification method based on persistent homology. To quantify the vertical asymmetries in the bar and disk of the LMC-like galaxy, we applied Fourier decomposition. We constructed Gaia DR3 mock catalogs to study whether the observed vertical asymmetries in Gaia DR3 are caused by astrometric uncertainties. We detect strong $m = 1$ vertical asymmetries in the bar and disk of the LMC-like galaxy for all interacting simulations after the pericentric passage of the SMC-like galaxy, indicative of a warped bar and disk. We also detect $m=2$ vertical asymmetries in the bar of the LMC-like galaxy for some simulations, indicative of a buckling bar. We conclude that the SMC passages induce vertical asymmetries in both the bar and the disk of the LMC, compatible to those measured in Gaia DR3. Tidal interactions naturally give rise to a warped and buckling LMC bar, and a warped LMC disk. Comparison between our results and Gaia DR3 suggest that models with a higher mass ratio between the SMC and the LMC may be favorable for reproducing the observed tilt of the LMC bar, and that the most recent SMC pericentric passage occurred at most 210 to 300 Myr ago.

The impact of MUSE sky subtraction on unresolved stellar population end products: Study cases of NGC 1097 and NGC 1433

Camila de Sá-Freitas, Fuyan Bian, Patrícia Sánchez-Blázquez

Main: 6 pages, 6 Figures. Appendix: 4 pages, 6 images. Accepted in A&A

PDFTue 10 Nov
One of the main steps in data reduction for ground-based astronomy is sky subtraction. For extended objects, it is necessary to observe dedicated sky frames, usually with airmass values similar to the target's, using different pointing strategies. However, it is not well documented how different observing strategies can affect the science end product. In particular, with the use of the MUSE/VLT, this can yield distinct stellar population products, such as age, metallicity, and α-enhancement. To address this, we considered two case studies seeking to investigate: (i) the sky stability in observations taken consecutively over a period of 2 hours with thin sky transparency and airmass values of 1.25-1.05, and (ii) the impact of different sky conditions on the same target. Among our main results, we find that for galaxies with 0.0014 < z < 0.0077, the sky line [NI] at λ 5200 coincides with the Mgb bandpass, which is essential for metallicity measurements, highlighting the need for proper sky subtraction. In addition, for regions with relative fluxes above 10 with respect to the sky in NGC1097, sky frames with variation up to ~18 % still retrieve consistent stellar population properties - in this case, this corresponds to flux values of >1500 x 10^-20 erg/s/cm^2 and Johnson-V ~19.0. In contrast, when comparing data from different nights for NGC1433, fainter regions suffer greatly from distinct sky conditions, especially in metallicity measurements, which range within +/- 0.2, strongly affecting the interpretation of stellar mass assembly and the origin of the gas (i.e., mergers might bring metal-poor gas, while an in-situ burst of star formation can yield metal-rich stars). This study is the first of its kind and highlights the importance of proper sky subtraction, providing insights regarding data limitations and observational strategies for future observations with MUSE.

Could globular clusters in the vicinity of the galactic centre have contributed stars to the nuclear star cluster in the past?

Kalambay M., Kuvatova D., Ishchenko M., Berczik P., Omarov Ch. +1

10 pages, 5 figures, 2 Tables

PDFTue 10 Nov
The results of direct N-body simulations of three Milky Way bulge globular clusters - Terzan 2, Terzan 4, and Terzan 5 - evolving in a time-varying galactic potential derived from the IllustrisTNG-100 cosmological database over 8 Gyr are presented. Incorporating full stellar evolution and compact remnant formation, we quantify the probability and efficiency of stellar accretion onto the Galactic nuclear star cluster (NSC) during close pericentre passages of these three clusters. To parametrize the effect of dynamical friction in the dense central environment, we introduce a velocity reduction factor (Vred) ranging from 1.00 to 0.10. It was found that all three clusters contribute stars to the NSC even in the absence of velocity reduction, with a combined bound stellar count of 730 stars and a total accreted mass of 278 Msol at Vred = 1.00, growing to 5420 stars and 1175 Msol at Vred = 0.10. Terzan 4 is the dominant contributor across all velocity reduction factors, reflecting its more frequent and closer pericentre passages near the Galactic centre. The mass distribution of accreted stars reveals a bimodal structure: a low-mass component below 1 Msol whose capture is independent of Vred, and a prominent high-mass tail extending to 20 Msol corresponding to neutron stars and black holes, which grows with decreasing Vred. Notably, Terzan 4 is the only cluster for which white dwarfs are also captured by the NSC at every Vred value. Our results suggest that the Terzan family represents a more significant source of compact remnants for the Galactic NSC than previously studied clusters, while confirming that tidal stripping of surviving globular clusters alone cannot account for the total NSC mass.

The Connection between Halo Assembly History and the Stellar Distribution II: A Radially Stratified Fossil Record of Past Halo Mass

Shuo Xu, Song Huang, Conghao Zhou, Alexie Leauthaud, Benedikt Diemer

12 pages, 6 figures

PDFTue 10 Nov
The spatial stellar distribution in massive central galaxies preserves a stratified fossil record of their host halos' assembly history. Using a halo-mass-complete sample from the IllustrisTNG-300 hydrodynamical simulation at $z=0.4$, we quantify this connection with two diagnostics. The fossil correlation $\tilde{\rho}(z)$ measures how strongly a present-day galaxy property retains information about the progenitor halo mass at each redshift. The rank retention fraction $f_{\rm ret}(z)$ measures how many halos selected by that property today were among the most massive halos at each epoch. The central stellar mass at different galactocentric radii exhibits non-monotonic fossil correlations with past halo mass, peaking at intermediate redshifts that systematically shift from $z\sim1$ in the inner regions to $z\sim0.7$ in the outer stellar halos. These peak redshifts are robust from group to cluster scales. Partial correlations with halo accretion rate show that each peak marks the end of the epoch when that component's buildup was sensitive to halo accretion. The rank retention fraction shows that the annular stellar masses of the central galaxy beyond 10 kpc are less biased tracers of the most massive halos at intermediate redshifts than richness. This stratified fossil record originates mainly from the ex-situ phase of galaxy assembly. The outer stellar halo also traces the satellite population at intermediate redshift better than present-day richness. These results establish that the stellar profile of massive central galaxies encodes a radially stratified fossil record of halo assembly history.

Classical Cepheids in the 4MOST era I: Methodology for 4MOST variable stars and NLTE abundance gradients in the Milky Way

R. Albarracín, M. Bergemann, N. Storm, G. Guiglion, R. I. Anderson +7

24 pages, 19 figures, Accepted for publication by A&A

PDFTue 10 Nov
Classical Cepheids (CCs) are key distance indicators and tracers of young stellar populations in the Milky Way. Their period-luminosity-metallicity (PLZ) relation also makes them calibrators of the Hubble constant $H_0$. Despite their importance, chemical composition, especially non-LTE (NLTE) effects, remains a significant source of systematics in PLZ applications. In preparation for the large CCs sample expected from the 4MIDABLE-HR (4MOST Consortium Survey 4: Milky Way Disc and Bulge High-Resolution) survey, we present a method for deriving Cepheid atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances in NLTE. Using the Payne artificial neural network (ANN) trained on NLTE spectra, we derive atmospheric parameters and abundances for 17 elements: O, Na, Mg, Al, Si, Ca, Ti, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Sr, Y, Ba, Eu in NLTE and C and Cr in LTE. We apply the new method to archival spectra of Cepheids and derive radial chemical gradients in the Milky Way. We derive robust NLTE stellar parameters and abundances for Cepheids spanning metallicities from $\sim -0.87$ to $\sim +0.35$ dex. For stars with multi-epoch spectra, we find that NLTE abundances are consistent across the pulsation cycle, whereas LTE values show larger dispersions and biases. The resulting abundance distributions and gradients align well with Galactic disk expectations from chemical evolution models. We confirm that the gradients flatten in the outer disk beyond $R_{\rm GC} \sim 15\,\mathrm{kpc}$ and provide the first NLTE gradients for Co, Ni, Y, and Eu. We show that our NLTE astrophysical analysis yields robust solutions for atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances across spectra at the resolving power of 4MOST-HR. This enables large, homogeneous studies with upcoming surveys such as 4MOST 4MIDABLE-HR and provides a solid basis for tightening constraints on Galactic chemical evolution models and Cepheid distance-scale work.

The hierarchical structure of the Milky Way I. A model-agnostic clustering of 1 billion stars in 5D

William H. Oliver, Tobias Buck

16 pages, 13 figures, 1 table

PDFTue 10 Nov
The Milky Way exhibits a hierarchical structure shaped by mergers, accretion, and internal dynamics. Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) enables a global view of this structure, but requires scalable, model-agnostic methods to extract multi-scale clustering patterns. We aim to characterise the hierarchical clustering structure of the Milky Way in a model-agnostic way and to assess how the resulting structures relate to existing catalogues. We apply the hierarchical density-based clustering algorithm AstroLink to a locally magnitude-complete subsample of over one billion Gaia DR3 stars with five-dimensional astrometric information. The data are corrected for selection effects and transformed using a distance contraction and feature scaling. We compare the resulting cluster hierarchy to catalogues of open clusters, globular clusters, dwarf galaxies, and stellar streams using custom availability-aware metrics and a bi-directional best-match framework. At a significance threshold of $S = 3.8\sigma$, AstroLink identifies 7410 statistically significant clusters spanning a wide range of morphologies. Compact systems are often recovered as reciprocal matches with many also exhibiting internal substructure, while extended structures are typically fragmented or partially recovered. A substantial fraction of clusters have no counterpart in the comparison catalogues, these may be new structures but additional physical checks are needed to verify this. A model-agnostic, hierarchical clustering approach can recover the multi-scale structure of the Milky Way at Gaia DR3 scale and provides a unified framework for comparing heterogeneous catalogues and identifying candidate structures.

The Cool Circumgalactic Echo of the Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relatio

Zeyu Chen, Enci Wang, Huiyuan Wang, Houjun Mo, Luis C. Ho +8

20 pages, 13 figures. Submitted to ApJ

PDFTue 10 Nov
The stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR), a key prediction of galaxy formation models, peaks at $M_* \sim 3 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$. This scale is often attributed to circumgalactic baryonic feedback processes, however, direct observational evidence linking the two remains scarce. Using Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) galaxy–quasar pairs, we map the cool circumgalactic medium (CGM) using Mg II $\lambda\lambda2796, 2803$ absorption with stacking technique across nearly three decades in stellar mass. The radial Mg II absorption equivalent-width profile, $W_{\rm MgII} \propto D^k$, becomes progressively shallower with increasing stellar mass, following $k = 0.70\log(M_*/M_\odot)-9.12$. Crucially, we find that the $W_{\rm MgII}$ in the CGM scales tightly with the SHMR. The $W_{\rm MgII}$ peaks at the same characteristic mass as the SHMR, coinciding with a transition of the cool CGM from super-virial to sub-virial kinematics. Low-mass halos show excess non-gravitational motions consistent with recycled accretion and feedback-driven circulation, whereas massive halos show halo-centric heating that may maintain star formation quenching. This study bridges the gap between cool CGM and SHMR, showing that peak star formation efficiency coincides with the most abundant cool CGM reservoirs.

The Tierras Observatory is a fully robotic 1.3-m telescope at the Fred Lawrence Whipple Observatory in southern Arizona. Tierras achieves sub-part-per-thousand (ppt) photometric precision on two timescales: intra-night, to detect and characterize transits of planets around nearby mid-to-late M dwarfs, and night-to-night across an observing season, to characterize the long-term variability of their host stars. Forward modeling linear variable filter data: High resolution PAH and Paschen-$α$ maps from SPHEREx

Andrew K. Saydjari, Connor Hainje, Benjamin Dodge, Philipp Frank, David W. Hogg +1

34 pages, 16 figures

PDFTue 10 Nov
Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission is a powerful tool for the study of the interstellar medium (ISM) in both Galactic and extragalactic contexts. It can reveal ionization state, radiation field, metallicity, composition, and ISM structure as sculpted by processes like turbulence and stellar feedback. Here we use NASA's SPHEREx to produce clean, high-resolution PAH maps at spectral resolution R $\sim$ 35 (compared to WISE at R $\sim$ 2). Prior treatments of the SPHEREx linear variable filter data seek to convert them to more conventional band-averaged maps that can leave striping artifacts associated with spatially variable wavelength sampling, especially at emission lines. In contrast, we deliver a scalable forward-modeling method capable of generating high-quality all-sky SPHEREx emission-line maps at 8.5 arcsec FWHM angular resolution (on 6.4 arcsec pixels), while naturally producing maps without visible striping. We reformat the data into a pixel-level database, mask stellar contamination, and infill missing pixels with a conditional, data-driven, anisotropic Gaussian process. We apply our method to produce 3.3 $\mu$m and 3.4 $\mu$m PAH and Paschen-$\alpha$ emission maps of a set of fields spanning a range of Galactic and extragalactic conditions including the Rosette nebula, the Corona Australis molecular cloud, M33, and M31. We demonstrate the quality and synergies of our SPHEREx 3.3 $\mu$m PAH maps by comparisons with other available gas and dust tracer maps and achieve sensitivities up to $\sigma(I_{3.3}) = 2.7 \times 10^{-8}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ sr$^{-1}$. We estimate that a two-year extension of the SPHEREx survey, with modest modifications to the scanning strategy, would match or exceed the PAH sensitivity of WISE over the whole sky.

We present a spectroscopic database and analysis of satellite streaks in Public Data Release 1 (PDR1) from the Hobby-Eberly Telescope Dark Energy Experiment (HETDEX), covering 6778 observations and $\sim$90 deg$^2$ of sky from January 2017 to July 2024. We detect 527 streaks across 492 observations, a contamination fraction of 7.3% that rises to monthly values above 20% by 2023–2024, although the streaks mask only 0.09% of the survey area. Of the 527 streaks, 475 (90.1%) are identified with a cataloged object using archival two-line elements: 258 (54%) lie in low or Earth orbit and 217 (46%) are geostationary or highly elliptical. We adopt JWST grism data in five lensing cluster fields and their parallel fields from the CANUCS program: ABELL-370, MACS-J0416, MACS-J0417, MACS-J1149, MACS-J1423. Impact of the large scale environment on the baryonic scaling relations of disc galaxies: Insights from the GASP survey

C. Bacchini, A. Marasco, B. Vulcani, A. Moretti, J. Fritz +5

14 pages (+ appendices), 4 figures. Accepted by A&A. Galaxy atlas available at this https URL. 5281/zenodo.23061246

PDFTue 10 Nov
The Tully–Fisher relation (TFR) and the specific angular momentum–mass relation (SAMR) link the baryonic content of a disc to its kinematics and dynamics. Hence, these scaling relations should bear the imprints of any process removing baryons or altering their kinematics, such as those acting in clusters of galaxies. We test this using the MUSE survey "GAs Stripping Phenomena in galaxies" (GASP), which targets field, group, and cluster members at $z \approx 0.05$ and various stages of ram pressure stripping. For the 81 discs with favourable inclinations, we measure the distribution and kinematics of stars and ionised gas, and their specific angular momenta. Within their scatter, the stellar and baryonic relations show no significant dependence on the large scale environment: the TFRs have slopes $\alpha \approx 3.6$ and $\approx 3.5$ for any kinematic tracer, and the SAMRs have $\alpha \approx 0.65$–$0.75$, as predicted by tidal torque theory. The same holds for the ten S0-S0/as in our sample, consistent with the "faded spiral" formation scenario. Only the gas SAMR shows an environmental signature, with ram pressure stripped galaxies lying $\approx 0.16$~dex below unperturbed ones. This offset is mostly due to their enhanced star formation rate, and thus higher gas mass when inferred through the Schmidt-Kennicutt law, rather than to kinematic perturbations or disc truncation. Our results suggest that, within the area probed by MUSE ($\lesssim6$ disc scale lengths), these discs have preserved the angular momentum acquired at formation not only from cluster processes and pre-processing in filaments, but also from mergers and ejective feedback acting in any environment.

Szymon Petyniak, Stefano Bellotti, Aline Vidotto, Paul Cristofari, Jean-Francois Donati +2

Accepted for publication in A&A

PDFTue 10 Nov
M dwarfs are the most abundant group of stars in the Galaxy and they are expected to host a large number of exoplanets orbiting in the habitable zone. It is believed that stellar winds of M dwarfs can have a substantial effect on these exoplanets and on the observations of these systems. However, a systematic investigation of how the stellar winds of M dwarfs varies on timescales of a few months to years and how this impacts exoplanets around these stars is missing. We aim to investigate how variability of magnetic field properties of M dwarfs affects their stellar winds, which is essential for future studies of star-planet interactions (SPIs) and planetary habitability. Such variability might shed light on whether SPI signatures are going to repeat. We have compiled a total of 18 magnetic field maps for 6 M dwarfs, with masses between 0.1 and 0.6 $M_\odot$, and rotation periods between 2.7 and 175.6 days. We use these maps as input of 3D magnetohydrodynamic stellar wind simulations. We derive the mass-loss rate, the angular momentum-loss rate, and the Alfvén radius. For the stars with confirmed exoplanets (DS Leo, YZ Cet, and GJ 1151) we investigate the variability of stellar wind properties at the orbit. Our results follow the observational relation between mass-loss rate and X-ray flux. Due to the time-varying Alfvén surface, exoplanets orbiting DS Leo and YZ Cet move in and out of the sub-Alfvénic regime, affecting the nature of star-planet interactions and prospects for their detection.

Faint Thermonuclear Transients from the Double Detonations of Common White Dwarfs

Samuel J. Boos, Lars Bildsten

9 pages, 4 figures, 1 table; submitted to ApJ

PDFTue 10 Nov
The double detonation model is a viable channel to explain many observed Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), with thin helium shelled white dwarf (WD) progenitors of 0.9–1.1 M$_\odot$ reproducing normal SNe Ia observables. Recent work has also shown that all C/O WDs $\leq$ 1.0 M$_\odot$ can sustain a helium shell detonation that triggers a detonation of the underlying core. As WDs of lower masses (0.5–0.7 M$_\odot$) far outnumber those at higher mass, it is important to explore their double detonation outcomes. To that end, we conducted multidimensional double detonation simulations of low mass C/O cores with very thin helium shells, finding explosion outcomes that differ non-trivially from those at higher masses. Not surprisingly, their cores are incompletely burned with low yields of radioactive elements. Though still formally unbound in isolation, some of the ejecta could remain bound to a nearby companion. In addition, the offset initiation of the core detonation wave and its failure to burn through the entire core lead to a very asymmetric distribution of $^{56}$Ni that creates dramatically viewing angle-dependent light curves and spectra. We find that the peak bolometric luminosities for the same explosion vary up to nearly 2 magnitudes over the sky, and that the spectra and light curves from these models are within the range of faint SNe Iax. In particular, our 0.50 M$_\odot$ models agree well with the photometry of SNe Iax 2008ha and 2024vjm.

This bound holds for any potential, applies to the largest value of the coupling when the coupling depends on the field, and is reached exactly when the field comes to rest within the observed range. For the DESI DR2 best fits, the dark matter must lose about 5-7% of its mass by $z\simeq 0.8$, which requires $\beta_{\rm min}=0.047$-$0.058$; including the uncertainties of the data, the public DESI DR2 chains give the 95% lower limit $|\beta|>0.025$-$0.038$. We also apply the bound to published fits of coupled quintessence: all of them respect it, and those with a constant coupling reproduce only a shallow version of the crossing. Since the coupling cannot be smaller than $\beta_{\rm min}$, two known effects of the coupling have a minimum size: an extra attraction between dark matter particles of at least a few tenths of a per cent of gravity, and a lensing signal at least a few per cent weaker than the clustering of dark matter would suggest.

CMB Bounds on Primordial Black Holes via Radiation Capture: Linear Perturbations

We investigate the gravitational interaction between primordial radiation, consisting of photons and ultra-relativistic neutrinos, and primordial black holes (PBHs), and study its imprint on the anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background (CMB). Assuming that PBHs are immersed in the cosmic radiation background, we introduce a covariant energy-momentum transfer four-vector to describe the gravitational capture of radiation by PBHs. We extend the background-level framework of M. Farhang and S. M. S. Movahed [Phys. Rev. D 114, 083533 (2026)] to an inhomogeneous Universe at linear order by consistently modifying the perturbed continuity and Euler equations to account for both energy and momentum transfer. We use the imprint of this interaction on the CMB to place novel constraints on the PBH abundance, $f_{\rm pbh}$. We show that these constraints are up to two orders of magnitude tighter than those obtained from background-only analyses and exhibit a strong dependence on the PBH mass. Using Planck temperature and polarization data, we constrain the PBH fraction to $f_{\rm pbh} \lesssim 0.05-0.002$ for $M$ in the range $10^{15}-10^{17} M_\odot$. Furthermore, sensitivity forecasts for a cosmic-variance-limited survey with $\ell_{\rm max}=7000$, assuming a fiducial $f_{\rm pbh}=0$, yield projected bounds of $f_{\rm pbh} \lesssim 10^{-2}-2\times 10^{-4}$ over the mass range $10^{13}-10^{17} M_\odot$.

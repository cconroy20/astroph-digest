The meaning of Milky Way mass estimates in the presence of the LMC

Leandro Beraldo e Silva, Gurtina Besla, Nicolás Garavito-Camargo, Monica Valluri, Kathryne J. Daniel +6

Submitted to ApJ, 26 pages (main text), 22 figures - comments welcome

PDFWed 18 Nov
The LMC's recent arrival perturbs the observed kinematics of the Milky Way's (MW) stellar halo, complicating equilibrium-based MW mass estimates at Galactocentric distances $r\gtrsim 30$ kpc. Using N-body MW+LMC simulations, we show that the MW stellar halo can be used to disentangle the total MW+LMC, current LMC, and pre-infall MW mass profiles, by exploiting the LMC's orbital path and the LMC-induced motion of the MW's center of mass ("reflex motion"). Tracers in regions crossed by the LMC longest ago (Galactic coordinates $\ell >0^\circ, b>0^\circ$) are approximately relaxed and trace the total MW+LMC mass. Since the reflex motion is primarily latitudinal, a radial-velocity-only estimator ($M_{v_r}$) recovers the total MW+LMC mass with $\lesssim 5\%$ errors across $30<r/{\rm kpc}<200$, without any reflex-motion correction. Subtracting mass estimates in the region of the LMC's future orbit ($\ell < 0^\circ, b >0^\circ$) from those in its recent-orbit region ($\ell > 0^\circ, b <0^\circ$), which maximizes the LMC-induced velocity dipole, recovers the LMC's current mass profile with $10-20\%$ errors, but this requires tracers at $r\gtrsim 100-120$ kpc. Subtracting this from the total MW+LMC mass recovers the MW's pre-LMC infall mass at $r\gtrsim 80$ kpc with $5-10\%$ errors. Applying $M_{v_r}$ to DESI-MWS DR1 BHBs at $\ell >0^\circ, b>0^\circ$ and $50<r/{\rm kpc}<60$, we obtain a total MW+LMC mass $M(r<60\,{\rm kpc}) \approx 7.5^{+3.0}_{-2.2}\times 10^{11}\,M_\odot$. Observationally, recovering the LMC and pre-infall MW mass profiles requires 6D samples at $r\gtrsim100$ kpc, and a virial mass to within $\sim10-20\%$ requires $r\gtrsim120$ kpc, both achievable with Gaia DR5 astrometry for halo giants targeted by DESI Run 2 and 4MOST. Rather than treating the LMC-induced disequilibrium as an obstacle, our framework exploits it to weigh both galaxies.

The Lives and Deaths of Red Supergiants

Emma R. Beasor

Accepted for publication. Invited review for the Universe special issue "The Lifecycle of Massive Stars" edited by Jacco van Loon

PDFWed 18 Nov
Red supergiants (RSGs) represent a brief yet pivotal phase in the evolution of massive stars, encompassing the final stages of nuclear burning for many core-collapse supernova progenitors. Having expanded to 100 times larger than their main-sequence sizes, they occupy the cool, luminous extreme of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram. Characterised by extended convective envelopes, complex and dynamic atmospheres, and, sometimes, substantial mass loss, RSGs play a critical role in shaping both their own evolutionary outcomes and the circumstellar environments into which they ultimately explode. Despite decades of study, fundamental aspects of RSG physics remain poorly constrained, including the mechanisms driving mass loss and the connection between observed RSG populations and the properties of Type II supernovae. Recent observational advances have transformed the field, revealing episodic mass-loss events, asymmetric circumstellar structures, and signs of rapid evolutionary change in individual objects. Alongside these advances, studies of Type II supernovae and their progenitors have raised important new questions about the final stages of massive star evolution, including the possibility of rapid changes in the years immediately preceding core collapse. Motivated by these developments, this review aims to describe our current understanding of how RSGs live and die, from their observed properties through to their role as supernova progenitors, and where that understanding remains incomplete.

Shocks and Trails in the Circumgalactic Medium Driven by Runaway Supermassive Black Holes

Kung-Yi Su, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère

15 pages, 8 figures, 1 table

PDFWed 18 Nov
Runaway supermassive black holes (SMBHs) have long been predicted. RBH-1, discovered with HST and confirmed by JWST, provides the strongest evidence yet for a runaway SMBH: a bow shock and linear trail in the circumgalactic medium (CGM) of a $z=0.96$ galaxy. The bow shock is $\sim$kpc wide, the trail is $\sim 60$ kpc long, and the SMBH apparently moves at $\sim 1,000$ km s$^{-1}$. Using controlled hydrodynamic simulations, we show that the SMBH's gravity alone cannot produce a significant bow shock, but a comoving wind source can sustain one similar to RBH-1's. The wind must either be active or have shut off less than $\sim 3$ Myr ago. The required recent wind momentum flux is $6\times10^{32}\,{\rm dyn}\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$, where $n_{\rm CGM}$ is the CGM density. Assuming the wind reaches peak strength only during the last $\sim 6$ Myr, the minimum integrated wind mass is $3.5\times10^5\,M_\odot\,(n_{\rm CGM}/10^{-3}\,{\rm cm}^{-3})$. A $2\times10^7\,M_\odot$ SMBH accreting at $1\%$ Eddington could power the wind. Supernova feedback from a star cluster carried by the SMBH is an alternative, although the required mass and momentum fluxes $\gtrsim 60$ Myr after ejection are difficult to reconcile with a standard initial mass function. For a hot ambient CGM at $\sim 10^6$ K, the quasi-linear trail constrains the 1D velocity dispersion to $\sigma_{\rm 1D}\lesssim 30$ km s$^{-1}$, above which it becomes strongly disturbed. Runaway SMBH trails therefore offer a new probe of turbulence in the diffuse CGM, in regimes where its kinematics are otherwise inaccessible.

Non-monotonic Age-metallicity Relations Across the Galactic Disk Revealed by APOGEE Red Clump Stars

Chun Wang, Yang Huang, Haibo Yuan

15 pages, 6 figures, accepted for publication in ApJ

PDFWed 18 Nov
Using APOGEE red clump stars and open clusters with precise ages, we investigate the age–metallicity relation (AMR) across the Galactic disk as a function of Galactocentric radius $R$ and vertical height $|Z|$. For stars older than $\sim$6–8 Gyr, the AMR shows little dependence on either $R$ or $|Z|$ within the uncertainties, suggesting rapid and well-mixed chemical evolution in the early disk. In contrast, younger populations exhibit a clear negative radial metallicity gradient, with the inner disk being more metal-rich than the outer disk, consistent with inside-out disk growth since $\sim$6–8 Gyr ago. Most importantly, at fixed location, the AMR for ages $\lesssim6$–8 Gyr is non-monotonic: [Fe/H] decreases toward younger ages, reaches a minimum at intermediate ages, and then rises again. Beyond $R\sim11$ kpc, the relation becomes even more complex, exhibiting an additional reversal and a distinct metallicity peak at an age of $\sim2$ Gyr. Such complex behavior cannot be explained by continuous local enrichment alone, but instead points to the combined effects of a multi-phase gas-accretion history and radial migration. Episodes of metal-poor gas inflow can dilute the interstellar medium and reset its chemical evolution, while subsequent enrichment raises the metallicity again; radial migration further reshapes the observed AMR by mixing stellar populations formed at different radii and metallicities. A toy model incorporating inside-out growth, two gas-inflow episodes, and radial migration qualitatively reproduces the observed trends, highlighting the combined roles of episodic gas accretion and radial migration in shaping the chemical evolution of the Galactic disk.

A PhEW Good Simulations: I. Galaxy Properties

Lisiyuan Yang, Neal Katz, Evan Scannapieco, Aditi Vijayan

PDFWed 18 Nov
Although galactic outflows driven by stellar feedback are widely implemented in modern cosmological simulations, the interactions between them and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) remains poorly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we conduct a suite of cosmological simulations that use the PhEW (Physically Evolved Winds) subgrid model, which analytically tracks the exchange of mass, metals, momentum, and energy by winds with the ambient CGM using an algorithm based on high-resolution cloud-crushing simulations. Our results show that even without AGN feedback, our simulations are able to accurately reproduce the observed galactic stellar mass functions (GSMFs) and stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relations at $z\gtrsim 1$, after which the massive galaxies in our simulations start to overproduce stars. Our simulations also match the observed relations of $M_{\mathrm{HI}}-M_*$, $M_{\mathrm{H_2}}-M_*$, $Z_*-M_*$ and $Z_{\mathrm{ISM}}-M_*$. Taken together these results suggest that AGN feedback is much less important for galaxy formation and evolution than previously believed. However, like found in many previous simulations, the specific star formation rates (sSFRs) of our galaxies are significantly lower than those observed at $z\gtrsim1$ at all stellar masses. Significantly, we find that our results are robust against changes in the resolution and volume of our simulations, without the need to retune our wind parameters.

Spectro-Spatial Survey Fusion

Georgios N. Vassilakis, Miles Cranmer, Vasily Belokurov

18 pages, 12 figures; Submitted to MNRAS

PDFWed 18 Nov
Imaging and spectroscopy constrain complementary information about the same sky but are usually processed separately. We introduce Spectro-Spatial Survey Fusion, a framework that unifies these measurements by fitting a continuous spectral surface-brightness field jointly through each instrument's response. Adapting from Gaussian splatting in computer vision, we represent the field as a sum of adaptive spatial Gaussians, each carrying a spectrum built from a shared, learned dictionary of templates. We demonstrate this on 400 Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) $ugriz$ exposures and 800 fiber spectra across an $84^{\prime}\times84^{\prime}$ region of COSMOS. The joint fit closely reproduces the spatial structure and broad spectral diversity of galaxies, stars, and quasars. For fitted SDSS observations, the summed predicted flux in source-selected pixels agrees with the data to within 2 percent in every band, and 96 percent of fitted spectra agree to within 10 percent in integrated flux. Sparse fiber spectra constrain a dictionary shared by every Gaussian component, so a model spectrum can be queried anywhere in the imaged area, including where no spectrum was fitted. Without refitting, the field predicts withheld SDSS images and spectra, and selected Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC) images and Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) spectra, which never enter the fit. The field predicts broad spiral-arm patterns in HSC images of two selected galaxies and DESI continuum shapes for galaxies without a fitted fiber. This framework provides a route to combining surveys so that spatial and spectral information from different observations and instruments can constrain a common representation of the sky.

Brown dwarfs and stars are dynamically indistinguishable as companions in AU-separation binaries

Ben Pennell, Johanna Müller-Horn, Sahar Shahaf, Hans-Walter Rix, Kareem El-Badry +1

Submitted to A&A November 16th, 2026. Comments welcome

PDFWed 18 Nov
Brown dwarfs occupy the mass range between stars and planets, populations whose dominant formation channels differ, which leave measurable differences in the orbital eccentricity and host-star metallicity distributions. We assemble a sample of astrometric binaries with main sequence primaries and brown dwarf companions at $\mathcal{O}$(AU) separations from $\textit{Gaia}$ DR3. The observable photocenter motions for such binaries are degenerate between a genuinely low-mass companion and a near-equal-mass binary. We break this degeneracy using $\textit{Gaia}$ XP spectrophotometry and follow-up FEROS spectroscopy, which removes removes ${\sim}70\%$ of initial low-mass-function candidates as likely near-twin binaries and leaves residual contamination of only a few percent. The resulting 368 systems, with masses and metallicities determined from the XP spectra and a selection function forward-modelled with $\texttt{Gaiamock}$, form the largest homogeneous sample of brown dwarf companions with astrometric orbits. We find the eccentricity distribution to be well described by a normal distribution. This is comparable to eccentricities of stellar companions at the same periods and higher than those of giant planets, with no significant dependence on companion mass ($\alpha=-0.13\pm0.09$) or period ($\beta=0.10\pm0.05$). The host-star metallicity distribution is likewise stellar-like, extending to [M/H]$=-0.5$, in contrast to the predominantly super-solar metallicity hosts of giant planets. After accounting for selection, the companion-mass distribution over all brown dwarf masses is consistent with an extension of stellar-binary demographics. Brown dwarf companions at AU separations down to at least $20\,M_J$ therefore appear to form predominantly through a binary-star-like channel. A possible transition to planet-like formation at lower masses cannot be located with the present sample.

FastSpecFit: Spectrophotometric Modeling of 38 Million Extragalactic Targets from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Second Data Release

John Moustakas, Jeremy Buhler, Dirk Scholte, Jessica Aguilar, Steven Ahlen +65

Submitted to AJ; 73 pages, 13 figures, 7 tables

PDFWed 18 Nov
Optical spectrophotometry provides a direct window into the physical conditions, kinematics, and formation histories of galaxies and quasars. FastSpecFit is an open-source Python software package that forward-models each Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) spectrum jointly with its broadband photometry, convolving stellar-population synthesis and emission-line templates with each fiber's native spectral resolution. This forward-modeling approach preserves DESI's uncorrelated pixel statistics, fits a typical spectrum in ~0.1 s of single-core time, and scales to tens of millions of objects, with uncertainties estimated via Monte Carlo resampling. FastSpecFit measures stellar velocity dispersions, non-parametric star-formation histories, stellar masses, star-formation rates, dust attenuation, K-corrections, rest-frame magnitudes, and fluxes and equivalent widths for 46 emission lines, including separate narrow and broad components for the Balmer lines. We describe these algorithms in detail, validate the measurements against independent codes, and present the resulting value-added catalog for DESI Data Release 2, which provides spectrophotometric and physical properties for 38 million extragalactic targets at 0<z<4. We also release a companion photometry-only catalog and document the catalogs previously released for the DESI Early Data Release and Data Release 1.

Briana Wirag, Jack E. Birkin, Justin S. Spilker, Dazhi Zhou, Manuel Aravena +15

PDFWed 18 Nov
SPT0311$-$58 is comprised of two clumpy dusty star-forming galaxies inhabiting a $z=6.9$ protocluster core. Within a $16$ kpc diameter, there are 26 spectroscopically confirmed members. The 12 highly dust-obscured clumps were identified by their rest-frame [CII] emission, and the 14 less obscured clumps in the rest-frame ultraviolet (UV). We present new NIRCam and MIRI imaging from JWST of the central regions, which respectively trace the rest-frame UV and near-infrared. Combining this imaging with supplementary data from the Hubble Space Telescope and Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) provides the most complete picture of SPT0311$-$58 to date. Using 14 photometric bands and ALMA dust continuum to model the spectral energy distribution of SPT0311$-$58, we present well-informed stellar masses and star-formation rates (SFR) on local ($\sim1.5$ kpc) and global scales. Additionally, we compare our measurements with the star-formation histories (SFH) of five massive quiescent galaxies from the Early eXtragalactic Continuum and Emission Line Science (EXCELS) survey. We find that SPT0311$-$58 has a higher SFR than expected from the SFHs of the EXCELS galaxies at $z=6.9$, but is otherwise a plausible progenitor to massive quiescent galaxies.

The Role of Galactic Shear in Regulating Local Star Formation Efficiency: A Combined MaNGA DynPop and ALMaQUEST Study

Kai Zhu, Shude Mao, Michele Cappellari, Sara L. Ellison, Tao Jing +6

23 pages, 6+2 figures, 1 table. Accepted for publication in ApJ

PDFWed 18 Nov
We study the local regulation of star formation efficiency (SFE) in star-forming regions of nearby galaxies using spatially resolved data from the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) and the ALMA-MaNGA QUEnching and STar formation (ALMaQUEST) surveys. We define the local SFE as $\Sigma_{\rm SFE} \equiv \Sigma_{\rm SFR}/\Sigma_{\rm H_2}$, where $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$ is the star formation rate surface density derived from dust-corrected $\rm H\alpha$ emission, and $\Sigma_{\rm H_2}$ is the molecular hydrogen surface density derived from CO measurements. Combining these data with dynamical properties from the MaNGA DynPop project, we use random forest regression to identify the dominant factors of $\Sigma_{\rm SFE}$. Galactic shear, quantified by the Oort parameter $A_{\rm Oort}$, emerges as the dynamical property most strongly associated with lower SFE on average, exceeding the predictive power of mass surface density (either total or stellar), Toomre stability, and mid-plane pressure. However, these quantities provide only modest predictive power and primarily trace the radial variation of SFE. After removing the shear-related radial trends, only weak correlations remain between SFE and other local galactic properties, implying that the remaining azimuthal variations are likely governed by cloud-scale processes below our resolution limit. Furthermore, the relationship between SFE and shear varies among galaxies and weakens in gas-rich regions, suggesting that gas density may modulate the impact of shear. This behavior is consistent with competition between shear-induced cloud stabilization and shear-driven cloud-cloud collisions. Overall, galactic shear appears to regulate the large-scale radial distribution of SFE, while cloud-scale interstellar medium physics may drive the remaining variation.

Flash UV Spectroscopy: Decoding the Ultraviolet Diversity of Young Core-Collapse Supernovae with the Hubble Space Telescope

W. V. Jacobson-Galán, R. J. Yete, L. Dessart, J. Sollerman, M. R. Siebert +43

37 pages, 29 figures, submitted to ApJ

PDFWed 18 Nov
We present multi-wavelength observations and modeling of the largest sample to date of core-collapse supernovae (SNe) with Hubble Space Telescope (HST) ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy within < 2 weeks of first light. Our total sample contains early-time UV spectroscopy from 12 Type II SNe (SNe II), 3 Type IIb SNe (SNe IIb), 3 Type IIn SNe (SNe IIn) and 1 Type I super-luminous SN (SLSN-I). This sample includes 11 objects with unpublished UV spectroscopy from HST Cycles 31 and 32 "Flexible Thursdays" target of opportunity programs, increasing the number of core-collapse SNe with early-time HST UV spectra by a factor of 2.4. The early-time near-UV spectra of most sample SNe II show similar Doppler-broadened P-Cygni profiles of metals (Mg, Fe, Ti, Cr), though with notable diversity in line velocities and absorption strengths. Comparison to non-Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (nLTE) radiative transfer model grids at similar phases indicates that most sample SNe II are consistent with low circumstellar material (CSM) densities at the time of UV observations (e.g., $\dot M = 10^{-6}$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, $v_w = 50$ km/s) and standard kinetic energies (e.g., $E_k = 0.6-1.2$ B), while two objects, SNe 2024faq and 2023ixf, show UV/optical evidence for interaction with more confined, higher-density CSM. These results demonstrate the potential of early-time UV spectroscopy to constrain the outermost SN ejecta structure, explosion energetics/composition, and progenitor mass-loss histories of core-collapse SNe.

A Single-Shot Method for the Absolute Polarization Angle Calibration of CMB Telescopes with Artificial Sky Sources via Stellar Diffraction Encoding

Dillon J. Bass, Bradley R. Johnson

15 pages, 4 figures

PDFWed 18 Nov
Absolute polarization angle calibration of cosmic microwave background (CMB) telescopes at the $\lesssim 0.1^\circ$ level is needed to prevent instrumental mixing of $E$ modes into $B$ modes from biasing constraints on primordial gravitational waves and cosmic birefringence. Artificial polarized sky sources like drones, balloons, or satellites can provide calibration references, but transferring their emitted polarization angle from the payload frame to the celestial frame remains a challenge. We propose a calibrator architecture, applicable to any such source, in which a single dual-use grid-optic sets the polarization axis of the emitted millimeter-wave signal and also diffracts starlight into a co-aligned star camera. The resulting diffraction spectra record the grid's orientation relative to the stars, so that it can be measured directly on the sky without fine alignment between the camera and grid-optic. We describe the geometry and physics of this concept and show that it significantly simplifies obtaining the calibration angle the observatory needs, because precise payload attitude is no longer needed and the received polarization is insensitive to small line-of-sight errors. In simulations of 1000 real star fields that include camera-grid misalignment, lens distortion, lateral chromatic aberration, atmospheric refraction, and stellar colors, a single exposure recovers the polarizing grid-optic's orientation in the celestial frame with an uncertainty of $\simeq 0.002^\circ$, fifty times below the $0.1^\circ$ goal. We also discuss the hardware tolerances a physical calibrator must meet, which future work will test. Our results establish the feasibility of this approach and motivate development of a dual-use grid-optic.

Neutron-Rich Disks, Nickel-Rich Ejecta: Can Direct-Horizon Collapsars Explain Early $r$-Process Enrichment?

Danat Issa, Ore Gottlieb

23 pages, 10 figures (5 in appendix). Comments welcome. Submitted for publication

PDFWed 18 Nov
Very massive stars above the pair-instability mass gap may collapse directly into black holes surrounded by massive disks. These "direct-horizon collapsars" accrete at $\dot{M} \gtrsim 10\,M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and have been proposed to eject a substantial amount of $r$-process material, powering "super-kilonovae". We present the first three-dimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of direct-horizon collapsars with M1 neutrino transport, following a $\approx 150\,M_\odot$ helium core from collapse through the escape of magnetically driven outflows. Extreme accretion rates neutronize the disks to $Y_{\rm e} \approx 0.1$-$0.25$ and, at early times, allow them to satisfy the gravitational-instability and cooling criteria for fragmentation.

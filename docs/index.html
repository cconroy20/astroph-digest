Beyond the Inner Slope $γ$: A Schwarzschild-based Probe of the Core-Cusp Problem in the Milky Way Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies

Rin Miyazaki, Masashi Chiba, Shunichi Horigome

29 pages, 11 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to AAS journals

PDFMon 16 Nov
We present a spherical Schwarzschild orbit-superposition framework for constraining the dark matter density profiles and stellar orbital structures of the Milky Way dwarf spheroidal galaxies. We model the line-of-sight velocity distribution with a Gaussian mixture whose parameters vary smoothly with projected radius through a mixture density network trained on unbinned stellar velocities. This approach reduces the effect of statistical fluctuations inherent in velocity moments on their kinematic constraints. Their covariance and smoothing-induced correlations are included, and the halo parameters are sampled with Markov Chain Monte Carlo. Mock tests for this model show that the density profiles and slopes near the half-light radius are generally recovered well, although the inferred velocity anisotropy shows some systematic deviations. Applying the model to Sculptor, Draco, and Ursa Minor, we find that the enclosed mass $M_h$ and logarithmic density slope $S_h$ at the projected half-light radius are less sensitive to the adopted two different priors than the asymptotic inner slope $\gamma$, consistent with earlier studies. We further compare the inferred slopes at the three-dimensional half-light radius $r_h$ and $0.5r_h$ for a given stellar mass with FIRE-2 $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) cosmological simulations including baryonic feedback. This comparison avoids introducing halo masses or virial radii that are not directly observable. The inferred slopes are broadly consistent with FIRE-2 $\Lambda$CDM predictions, although current uncertainties and the limited SIDM sample do not yet allow a robust distinction between dark matter models.

Predicting Individual Dark Matter Halo Concentrations from Their Assembly Histories

Dao-Zhou Wang, Weipeng Lin, Weishan Zhu, Tian-Cheng Luan

Submitted to ApJ, 15 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables. Comments and suggestions are welcome

PDFMon 16 Nov
We present an empirical model for predicting concentration parameters of individual dark matter halos in cold and warm dark matter (WDM) cosmologies over z=0-5. Constructed using a large suite of cosmological N-body simulations, our model separately incorporates cosmic expansion (linear growth factor), mass scale (linear density fluctuations), halo mass assembly histories via the Extended Press-Schechter (EPS) scaled formation variable, plus a WDM damping term tied to the half-mode mass. It works for both virial and 200 $\rho_{crit}$ halo definitions. Across all tested cosmologies and redshifts, our global logarithmic fit gives concentration scatter ~0.09 dex for relaxed halos and ~0.09 to 0.10 dex for all halos. While relaxed halos maintain nearly constant scatter near 0.09 dex, the scatter of all halos at z>3 increases from 0.10 to 0.18 dex, revealing substantial violent-relaxation impacts on high-redshift halo structure. At fixed mass, concentration scatter is dominated by assembly history. We extract the scaled formation variable's probability distribution function (PDF) from merger trees and find departures from standard EPS theory. This PDF is narrower in WDM than LCDM due to suppressed small-scale perturbations and rapid, delayed monolithic collapse near the WDM truncation scale. Monte Carlo realizations with our formulae reproduce concentration distributions and match our prior peak-concentration model, whereas standard EPS underestimates concentrations from delayed-formation bias. Our framework offers a transparent, computationally lightweight tool to predict individual halo concentrations across diverse cosmological models.

The ISM-CGM Transition of a Star-forming Dwarf Galaxy Resolved in Ionized Gas Emission

Chloe Neufeld, Pieter van Dokkum, Imad Pasha

15 pages, 4 figures, 1 table, submitted to ApJ

PDFMon 16 Nov
The circumgalactic media (CGM) of dwarf galaxies are expected to be tightly coupled to their star formation, as low virial temperatures imply a CGM rich in cold accretion and shallow potential wells allow for more effective feedback. In this work, we use Keck/KCWI and KCRM to spatially resolve the ionized gas at the boundary between the interstellar medium (ISM) and CGM in dwarf galaxy UGC 6578 (M* = 10^7.8 Msun), out to ~0.1 Rvir. We discover a clear transition between the ISM and CGM at 0.033 Rvir, where the Halpha surface brightness profile changes from an exponential to a shallow power law, marking the first such detection at M*/Msun < 10^8. BPT diagnostics and ionization budget calculations indicate that the gas in the inner CGM is likely ionized by the galaxy's own star formation activity rather than shocks or the UV background. Spatially resolved emission maps also reveal a kinematically distinct, low-metallicity clump embedded in the inner CGM, and we discuss the possibility that this is a direct detection of ongoing gas accretion. This work serves as a pilot study for the Baryon Recycling in Dwarf Galaxies Emission Survey (BRIDGES), which will combine spatially resolved KCWI observations of the ISM-CGM interface in low-mass galaxies with wide-field observations of diffuse ionized gas out to the virial radius with the MOTHRA array.

Properties of near-surface turbulence in hot, massive stars: Statistical signatures from multi-dimensional models of O-type stars

Cassandra Van der Sijpt, Florian Driessen, Jon Sundqvist, Nicolas Moens, Dwaipayan Debnath

Submitted to Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFMon 16 Nov
Variability of massive stars indicates the presence of complex, multi-D physics near the surface. Recent advancements in multi-D radiation-hydrodynamical modelling of massive star atmospheres show strong turbulence triggered by the iron opacity bump. We analyze statistical properties of sub-surface turbulence in multi-D simulations of O-star atmospheres. Particularly, we study potential dependencies of turbulent properties on global stellar parameters. Using 2D O-star models, we studied several statistical properties of the turbulence that develops in these models. We compute autocorrelation functions of density and velocity to study typical eddy length scales. Moreover, we compute kinetic energy power spectra to analyze the energy distribution and compare this to known results from established turbulence theories. We find anisotropy of the turbulent eddies in velocity space. Moreover, this anisotropy increases with classical Eddington factor. This anisotropy disappears in density space where all models show nearly isotropic structures and no dependence on stellar parameters. The kinetic energy power spectra exhibit an inertial range with a cascade exponent that has an average value of -2.57. This exponent does not show any clear dependence on stellar parameters. Importantly, this energy cascade is significantly steeper than those in known turbulence theories such as Kolmogorov turbulence with exponent -5/3 and Burgers turbulence with exponent -2. This additional steepening can potentially be attributed to radiative damping which increases with increasing wavenumber. Sub-surface turbulence in hot, massive stars is anisotropic and radially dominated in velocity space and its energy cascade is steeper than both Kolmogorov turbulence and Burgers turbulence. Although not rigorously proven here, we suggest that this steepening is due to the scale-dependent effect of radiative damping.

Properties of stellar disks and spheroids in simulated galaxies

Valeria A. Cristiani, Julio F. Navarro, Mario G. Abadi

10 pages, 8 figures. Accepted for publication in Astronomy and Astrophysics (A&A)

PDFMon 16 Nov
The optical morphology of a galaxy is determined by the relative importance of their disk and spheroidal components. Although the scaling laws of disk-dominated or spheroid-dominated galaxies have been studied in some detail, less attention has been paid to the properties of each component in galaxies where both coexist. We use a dynamical decomposition of 3452 galaxies from the TNG100 simulations with stellar masses in the range $10 \leq \log_\mathrm{10}(M_*/M_\odot) \leq 12$ to study how, at given mass, the properties of a spheroid are altered by the presence of a disk, and vice versa. We define spheroids as the non-rotating component made up of all stars in retrograde motion, plus an equal number of co-rotating stars mirrored in angular momentum and energy. Disks simply consist of the remainder of (co-rotating) stars. We find that disk properties (rotation speed, size, formation time) depend sensitively on the mass of the spheroidal component and therefore of the total mass of the galaxy in which both reside. At given mass, disks rotate faster, are more extended, and form their stars earlier as the mass of the spheroidal component increases. On the other hand, at fixed mass, the properties of spheroids are less sensitive to the presence of a disk. Roughly speaking, the classical bulges of simulated disk galaxies closely resemble ellipticals of similar stellar mass. The apparent structural invariance of spheroids to the presence of a disk is somewhat surprising, but it likely results because disks form out of high angular momentum material which surrounds the spheroid and thus have less dynamical influence on a spheroid's structure.

Euclid Data Release 1 (DR1) : Performance of the redshift measurement and spectroscopic analysis

V. Le Brun, M. Moresco, M. Bethermin, S. Quai, N. Borghi +94

24 pages, 11 figures

PDFMon 16 Nov
The European Space Agency's Euclid mission has issued its first major data release(Data Release 1, DR1), providing a transformative view of the extragalactic sky through deep photometry and near-infrared slitless spectroscopy. This paper presents the Euclid DR1 spectroscopic data set and details the evolution of the spectroscopic processing function (SPE PF) pipeline, which is responsible for the automated analysis of 1-dimensional spectra acquired by the Near-Infrared Spectro-Photometer (NISP). The SPE PF pipeline workflow comprises spectroscopic classification, redshift determination, and the systematic measurement of spectral features. Emission line fluxes are derived using two independent methodologies: direct integration (DI) and Gaussian fitting (GF). For high-quality samples selected in the EDF-N, we find a 98.5% agreement between Euclid and DESI spectroscopic redshifts. This success rate increases to 98.9% when incorporating pipeline probability and reliability estimators. (abridged)

Detectability limits of Ce abundances in Gaia RVS

S. Vitali, A. Escorza, T. Signor, P. A. Palicio, M. Abdul-Masih +6

Recommended for Publication in A&A

PDFMon 16 Nov
Gaia DR3 provides medium-resolution Radial Velocity Spectrograph (RVS) spectra for millions of stars, but the extent to which they contain recoverable cerium (Ce) information across stellar parameter space remains unclear. We assess Ce detectability in Gaia RVS spectra and the reliability of DR3 Ce abundances ahead of the larger DR4 catalogue. We compute synthetic spectra across a stellar-parameter grid representative of the Gaia RVS sample. We combine the Cayrel equivalent-width criterion and the Cramér-Rao bound to map Ce detectability as a function of stellar parameters and signal-to-noise (SNR). We also quantify continuum-placement errors and compare the predicted detectability boundary with the Gaia DR3 GSP-Spec catalogue. Ce is reliably detectable primarily in cool giants at [Fe/H] $\gtrsim$ 0.5 dex and with at least [Ce/Fe] $\gtrsim$ 0 at an average SNR of 50, and the detectability region expands toward higher gravities at increasing SNR. Continuum-placement errors of $\gtrsim$1% become a significant source of bias, and at $\gtrsim$2% they completely prevent Ce detection. The criterion we present identifies where RVS Ce abundances, including those from DR4, can be robustly exploited. Outside our detectability region, these abundances should be interpreted cautiously, whether derived by GSP-Spec or by supervised methods. Currently, depending on the assumed continuum-placement uncertainty, between $\sim$40% and $\sim$70% of the published DR3 Ce measurements fall within this region; however, the rest are not reliable. Our findings are particularly important for metal-poor stars, for which the Ce lines in the RVS range are simply not detectable.

Effects of Environment on the Star Formation Histories and Shapes of Simulated Galaxies

Francisco J. Mercado, Brian A. Giraud Calderon, Jorge Moreno, Coral Wheeler, Jose A. Benavides +4

26 pages, 13 figures, 1 table. Submitted to ApJ

PDFMon 16 Nov
We investigate how environment is connected to the formation times and three-dimensional stellar shapes of galaxies using the FIREbox cosmological volume simulation. We analyze 1720 resolved galaxies at $z=0$ spanning stellar masses $M_\star\approx5\times10^6$ to $5\times10^{11}\,M_\odot$ and environments ranging from isolated galaxies to groups in $\sim10^{13}\,M_\odot$ halos. We quantify environment using the Perturbation Index (PI), which measures the cumulative tidal influence of neighboring galaxies relative to each galaxy's own binding force. We measure formation times from cumulative star formation histories and shapes from intrinsic stellar axis ratios. Formation times depend most strongly on stellar mass, but below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, galaxies in more strongly perturbing environments formed their stars earlier, most clearly in the late-time star formation of low-mass galaxies. Galaxies in these environments also have larger minor-to-major axis ratios at all masses: more spherical at low and intermediate mass and thicker along the minor axis at high mass. A partial-correlation analysis shows that, below $10^8\,M_\odot$, the environment-shape correlation is no longer significant at fixed formation time, whereas between $10^8$ and $10^{10}\,M_\odot$ a modest correlation remains. Above $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, the weak environment-shape correlation is not explained by formation time. Below $10^{10}\,M_\odot$, high-PI galaxies also have lower halo masses at fixed stellar mass; at fixed halo mass, environment is only weakly correlated with formation time and shape, whereas formation time and shape remain correlated. We therefore predict that, at fixed stellar mass, dwarf galaxies in stronger tidal environments should appear rounder in projection, with the difference largely tracking the ages of their stellar populations.

Multipoles in the IllustrisTNG simulation galaxies and implications for Strong Gravitational Lens modelling in Time-delay Cosmography

Abdullah Al Zaif, Suhail Dhawan, Vasily Belokurov, Erin E. Hayes

PDFMon 16 Nov
Time-delay cosmography is a powerful independent probe of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), yet reaching the sub-2$\%$ precision required to resolve the Hubble tension demands a rigorous treatment of mass model systematics. We use realistic massive elliptical galaxies from the IllustrisTNG hydrodynamic simulation suite to characterise higher-order angular mass complexity in the form of multipoles ($m=1,3,4$) and quantify their impact on $H_0$ inference. We find that these multipole perturbations are common and emergent features of galaxy assembly; in 51$\%$ of our total samples ($54\%$ TNG100-1 galaxies and $85\%$ for TNG50-1 galaxies), the addition of multipole terms to a standard elliptical power-law model is preferred by the Bayesian Information Criterion. Ignoring these structures in lens modelling introduces systematic biases in $H_0$: for perturbations of $\sim2\%$, the biases are of the order of 1–2$\%$ for $m=1$, 4–5$\%$ for $m=3$, and 3–6$\%$ for $m=4$ multipole. While the $m=4$ (boxy/discyness) mode induces a predictable directional bias, odd-order modes ($m=1,3$) introduce significant stochastic scatter. We demonstrate a degeneracy where unmodelled internal multipoles can be effectively absorbed by the external shear component. This combined with low signal-to-noise observations can create “hidden biases” where formally perfect fits to imaging data ($\chi_\nu^2 \approx 1$) could harbour significant cosmological errors. Our results provide an astrophysical basis for multipole priors and highlight the necessity of moving beyond rigid parametric models to achieve high-accuracy cosmography in the era of Euclid and LSST.

2D and 3D unified RHD atmospheres and wind models of early B-supergiants including approximate NLTE effects

Dwaipayan Debnath, Jon O. Sundqvist, Nicolas Moens

PDFMon 16 Nov
B supergiants are evolved massive stars with low surface gravities and lower effective temperatures, with substantial radiation-driven winds. Generally, they have been studied assuming 1D spherical symmetry. We present the first multi-D unified atmosphere and wind models for B supergiants, especially investigating the effects of approximate NLTE ionization and subsurface turbulence on the atmosphere and wind dynamics. We computed multi-D box-in-a-star RHD unified atmosphere and wind simulations of 2D/3D early B supergiant models using a hybrid opacity approach. We calculated line opacities from approximate NLTE populations, including line scattering. We calculated the flux-, energy-, and Planck-mean line opacities separately from our large line database using Sobolev escape probabilities, assuming two-level atoms. The models develop a highly turbulent atmosphere with time-averaged turbulent velocities of ~ 40 - 55 km/s at the photosphere in the 2D simulations, and ~ 25 km/s in 3D. In the cooler B1, B1.5 models, the wind acceleration is more gradual than in the hotter B0 model, leading to a significantly higher maximum wind speed in the B0 model. Further, the approximate NLTE formalism strongly alters the ionization stratification in the wind compared to LTE, modifying the line force. Mass-loss rates obtained in the models are of order 10^-6 - 3 x 10^-6 solar masses per year. Including turbulent pressure in hydrodynamically consistent NLTE CMF steady-state 1D simulations with comparable parameters can produce higher mass-loss rates and lower terminal wind velocities than in corresponding models without turbulent velocity, in overall qualitative agreement with our multi-D simulations. Our calculations suggest that in the lower surface-gravity B supergiants, in addition to modifying the photospheric density scale heights, turbulent pressure also influences global properties of the wind.

Precise Deep-Learning Reconstruction of Galaxy Cluster Mass Maps and Substructures from Optical Observations

Atulit Srivastava, Weiguang Cui, Daniel de Andres, Guillaume Bonnet, Romeel Davé +4

PDFMon 16 Nov
Accurately mapping the total mass distribution within galaxy clusters, including dark matter, is fundamental to understanding structure formation and constraining cosmological parameters. Conventional mass-mapping techniques rely predominantly on gravitational lensing and often produce smooth mass distributions based on analytical profiles, potentially missing important internal substructures. We introduce a complementary, simulation-based inference approach to reconstruct detailed galaxy cluster mass maps and resolve their internal substructures directly from optical imaging. We trained deep learning models on realistic hydrodynamical simulations of galaxy clusters to infer their mass distributions from optical observations. We subsequently applied this framework to observations of real galaxy clusters obtained with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) as part of the Cluster Lensing And Supernova survey with Hubble (CLASH) programme. The reconstructed mass maps successfully reproduce the overall shapes and radial mass profiles of the CLASH clusters, showing strong agreement with independent gravitational lensing measurements. Furthermore, the subhalo mass functions derived from the reconstructed maps exhibit power-law behaviour broadly consistent with predictions from numerical simulations. Our results demonstrate that combining optical imaging with deep learning provides a scalable and physically meaningful approach to mapping galaxy cluster mass distributions. This framework opens a new avenue for exploiting upcoming wide-field imaging surveys to systematically investigate dark matter substructures across large cluster samples, complementing traditional gravitational lensing techniques. Moreover, the model is inherently flexible and can incorporate multi-wavelength observations, including X-ray and Sunyaev–Zel'dovich data, to further improve total mass predictions.

PDFMon 16 Nov
Understanding the main drivers of galaxy formation and the connection between observable properties and evolutionary pathways is a central goal of extragalactic astronomy. Observations provide single-epoch snapshots of galaxies at different stages of their development, whereas cosmological simulations allow us to trace the full formation histories of individual systems within a theoretical framework. Extracting information from these datasets remains challenging. Our aim is to use machine-learning techniques to unveil physical properties from the simulation datasets. We apply two unsupervised ML techniques, dimensionality reduction and clustering, to identify galaxy evolution key parameters. We use UMAP as our dimensionality reduction tool and k-means as our clustering method. Our results indicate that the diversity of galaxy formation histories in IllustrisTNG can be organized into a small number of characteristic evolutionary pathways linking galaxy accretion histories and present-day structural and star-forming properties.

PDFMon 16 Nov
Hydrodynamic simulations are instrumental in the analysis of observations such as galaxy clustering used for the estimation of cosmological parameters. However, for these purposes, the simulations need both a large box size and high resolution, which is computationally prohibitive. In this work we use the connection between galaxies and their host dark matter halos to populate dark-matter-only simulations, using data from the FLAMINGO simulations suite. We employ the Extremely Randomized Trees algorithm to model the connection between the mass profile, history and environment of dark matter halos and the probability distribution of the stellar mass of the galaxy contained in the halo. The effect of vertical mixing on oxygen sequestration by refractory elements in the atmosphere of giant planets

Paolo Simonetti, Diego Turrini, Eugenio Schisano, Stavro L. Ivanovski, Romolo Politi +7

12 pages, 9 figures. Submitted to A&A

PDFMon 16 Nov
The most widely used route to study the formation history of giant exoplanets is the C/O ratio, usually derived from H$_2$O, CO, CO$_2$ and CH$_4$. However, refractory elements can sequester a significant fraction of O, causing the observed C/O ratio to be overestimated with respect to the intrinsic one. Previous calculations of this bias relied on chemical equilibrium models. Here we quantify the impact of vertical mixing using the 1-D chemical kinetics code VULCAN, expanded with a Mg-Si-Fe network including the condensation and settling of enstatite, forsterite and metallic iron. We compute 168 models spanning equilibrium temperatures $T_{eq} \in$ [700, 1500] K, eddy diffusion coefficients $K_{zz} \in$ [$10^6$, $10^{10}$] cm$^2$ s$^{-1}$ and metallicities $Z \in$ [0.9, 7.2] $Z_\odot$, derived self-consistently from planet formation simulations. We find that vertical transport causes magnesium silicates to destabilize already at $T_{eq} \sim 900$ K regardless of metallicity, releasing oxygen to H$_2$O and to minor gas-phase carriers such as SiO and Mg(OH)$_2$. Therefore, while equilibrium models predict a nearly constant C/O overestimation up to 1100-1300 K, in disequilibrium it drops sharply between 900 and 1200 K. In this transitional range the C/O overestimation is controlled by $K_{zz}$ and is partially degenerate with metallicity, while outside it is robust against both mixing intensity and the probed atmospheric layer, ranging from 14.5-23% at 700 K to 4.5-7% at 1500 K. Metallic iron condensation prevents Fe from sequestering oxygen, lowering the overestimation by about one third with respect to previous estimates. Finally, CH$_4$, CO$_2$ and the CH$_4$/CO ratio are most sensitive to $K_{zz}$ in the transitional range, and can provide a constraint on vertical mixing.

Asteroseismology of blue straggler stars in $ω$ Centauri. Stellar collisions as a possible formation scenario

Jadwiga Daszynska-Daszkiewicz, P. Gora, P. Walczak, A. Miszuda, W. Szewczuk +2

Accepted for the publication in A&A Letter, 4 pages, 5 figures plus 5 pages of the Appendix

PDFMon 16 Nov
Context. Blue stragglers are intriguing stars that appear more massive and younger than expected from single-star evolution, yet their evolutionary histories remain elusive because of the multiple possible formation channels. A promising and poorly explored avenue for addressing this problem is asteroseismology, which provides the most stringent constraints on internal structure and evolution. Aims. We investigate five SX Phoenicis stars from the core of the globular cluster ω Centauri that pulsate in two radial modes and occupy the blue-straggler region of the colour-magnitude diagram. Seismic models are constructed assuming a collisional origin and compared with our previous seismic studies based on single-star evolution. For comparison, we also explore a binary-evolution scenario involving mass transfer for one of the stars. Methods. The evolution of the parent stars is computed with MESA. The collision is then modelled with the MMAMS code, which reconstructs the merger remnant using an entropic variable. The subsequent evolution is followed with MESA up to the current age of the cluster. Finally, we construct seismic models reproducing the observed frequencies of the two radial modes. Results. We present the first seismic modelling of SX Phe stars (blue stragglers) based on the collision-formation scenario. Convective-core overshooting is essential for rejuvenation and for obtaining seismic models in the main-sequence phase of evolution, but it is not always sufficient. Whether a merger remnant undergoes rejuvenation depends in a complex way on the masses of the parent stars, metallicity, and collision time. We identify three evolutionary behaviours: no, delayed, and immediate rejuvenation. The collisional seismic models imply younger ages and higher masses than the seismic models based on single-star evolution.

As the number of external legs increases, the number of distinct tree topologies grows exponentially, whereas the number of algebraically distinct amplitude products grows considerably more slowly. As an application, we consider ultra-slow-roll inflation followed by a transition to a slow-roll attractor. For transitions without a potential step, we derive an explicit all-order expression for the vertex amplitudes and establish their positivity and dependence on the sharpness of the transition. When a potential step is included, we prove that all nonlinear vertex amplitudes remain positive for upward steps, whereas downward steps allow cancellations at specific parameter values. These cancellations can eliminate entire families of tree contributions, including configurations in which the leading local bispectrum and trispectrum vanish simultaneously while higher order correlations remain nonzero.

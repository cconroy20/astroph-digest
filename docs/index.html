Quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon: from individual element abundances to star formation histories

M. Longhetti, F.R. Ditrani, D. Bevacqua, G. De Lucia, A. Gallazzi +8

8 pages, 2 figures, published on New Astronomy

PDFThu 5 Nov
Understanding the formation and early evolution of massive quiescent galaxies is crucial for a complete picture of galaxy formation in a cosmological context. Such a comprehensive picture can be obtained through measurements of individual elemental abundances, which encode the timescales and physical conditions of their star-formation and assembly histories. While extensive work at low and intermediate redshift has revealed strong correlations between stellar mass, metallicity, and abundance ratios, studies at z ~ 2-3 remain extremely limited. Their faintness and compactness make medium-resolution near-infrared spectroscopy for detailed chemical analysis extremely challenging. We present a science case to measure full elemental abundance patterns in a representative sample of massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon, exploiting rest-frame optical absorption features. We show that deriving robust stellar population constraints for these systems requires spectra with S/N ~ 20 per resolution element, together with a spatial resolution comparable to their ~ 0.1 arcsec sizes. We show that such requirements can only be met with a 40m class telescope equipped with a powerful adaptive optics system. In this context, SHARP-NEXUS, in combination with MORFEO-MCAO capabilities, emerges as the only viable ELT instrument capable of enabling this science, providing both the sensitivity and angular resolution needed. Although massive quiescent galaxies are rare at these redshifts, their study can efficiently exploit the instrument's multiplexing by targeting galaxy overdensities and coordinating with surveys of star-forming galaxies. Such synergistic strategies, proven feasible in high-redshift clusters and by multiplex-sharing facilities like 4MOST, would allow SHARP-NEXUS to open a new window on the chemical enrichment of the earliest massive galaxies.

Constraining the Milky Way Mass profile with data driven distribution function using DESI DR2 halo stars

Yanrui Zhou, Jiaxin Han, Wenting Wang, Zhaozhou Li, Alexander H. Riley +55

23 pages, 11 figures. This paper is a DESI Collaboration Key Project publication

PDFThu 5 Nov
We employ a novel data-driven dynamical modeling method, the empirical distribution function (emPDF), to constrain the Milky Way mass profile using a large sample of halo stars from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Milky Way Survey (DESI MWS). The method does not assume any functional form of the tracer distribution. Deviations from the steady-state assumption and the effects of observational errors are further tested and calibrated using a number of realistic mock observations (AuriDESI). Applying the calibrated method to two classes of tracers in DESI, we derive a best-constrained virial mass and concentration of $\log M_\mathrm{200c}/M_{\odot}=12.05_{-0.12}^{+0.21}(_{-0.18}^{+0.26})$ and $\log c_\mathrm{200c}=1.20_{-0.16}^{+0.11}(_{-0.24}^{+0.20})$ for BHBs. For RR Lyrae stars we derive $\log M_\mathrm{200c}/M_{\odot}=12.15_{-0.14}^{+0.22}(_{-0.19}^{+0.26})$ and $\log c_\mathrm{200c}=1.16_{-0.12}^{+0.09}(_{-0.18}^{+0.16})$. The uncertainties quoted outside and within brackets are statistical and total errors including systematic errors calibrated from AuriDESI, respectively. The two tracer populations provide overall consistent measurements of the MW halo mass profile. Combining the halo star constraints with the independent constraint from satellite dynamics, we derive a robust and precise final measurement of the MW halo parameters of $\log M_\mathrm{200c}/M_{\odot}=12.12_{-0.05}^{+0.05}(_{-0.04}^{+0.06})$ and $\log c_\mathrm{200c}=1.16_{-0.03}^{+0.05}(_{-0.07}^{+0.05})$, with correlation coefficients of $\rho=-0.90(-0.54)$.

Mock catalogues for the DESI DR2 Milky Way mass modeling effort

Alexander H. Riley, Amanda Byström, Gustavo E. Medina, Namitha Kizhuprakkat, Andrew P. Cooper +52

PDFThu 5 Nov
The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) survey is an ongoing campaign to obtain millions of spectra, including for stars within the Milky Way. One of DESI's Key Projects for its second data release (DR2) is to measure the mass of the Milky Way using a variety of techniques applied to six-dimensional kinematics of distant halo stars. We present mock surveys generated from the Auriga cosmological simulations (AuriDESI) that are used to validate these techniques in several companion studies. The AuriDESI tracer catalogues are tailored to reflect key features of the DR2 Key Project dataset, including the selection of RR Lyrae and blue horizontal branch stars, uncertainties on line-of-sight velocities and proper motions, and variation in observational completeness within the survey footprint. We discuss how well the resulting AuriDESI tracer catalogues compare to their observed DESI counterparts, including suggesting improvements that can be generalized to similar mock survey efforts within the community. The AuriDESI tracer catalogues are publicly available on Zenodo.

A distribution-function estimate of the Milky Way mass profile from outer halo stars in DESI DR2

Gustavo E. Medina, Thomas M. Callingham, Monica Valluri, Alexander H. Riley, Ting S. Li +63

This paper is a DESI Collaboration Key Project publication. To be submitted to OJAp. 23 pages, 11 figures

PDFThu 5 Nov
We present measurements of the Milky Way mass using blue horizontal-branch (BHB) and RR Lyrae (RRL) stars from the second data release of DESI (DR2), covering its first three years of operations. We employ 279 BHBs and 213 RRLs at $\sim\!50$–$80$ kpc from the Galactic center, selected from catalogs roughly twice as large as those of DESI DR1, used in our previous mass measurement. We apply the Galactic Mass Estimator (GME), a hierarchical Bayesian framework that assumes power-law forms for the potential and the tracer density, and accounts for distance and velocity uncertainties. Validating the method on 24 AuriDESI mock catalogs (six Auriga halos at four viewing angles), we find that it overestimates the enclosed mass by $\sim\!15\%$ at 80 kpc, and correct our results accordingly. We obtain $M(<80\,{\rm kpc}) = 0.64^{+0.06}_{-0.06}$ and $0.53^{+0.05}_{-0.05} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$ for BHBs and RRLs, respectively, while including Sagittarius stream stars lowers these masses by $\sim\!10$%. Extrapolating to 100 kpc, we obtain $M(<100\,{\rm kpc}) = 0.74^{+0.07}_{-0.07}$ and $0.62^{+0.06}_{-0.07} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$, and anchoring an NFW halo to our GME constraints at 65 kpc yields virial masses of $M_{200} = 1.12^{+0.14}_{-0.12}$ and $0.89^{+0.11}_{-0.10} \times 10^{12}\,M_\odot$. BHBs yield systematically higher masses than RRLs across the full radial range, reflecting the impact of tracer choice even within a single method. This work is part of a wider DESI DR2 Key Project effort to measure the Milky Way mass and constrain systematics arising from different samples and methodologies.

DESI DR2 Results III: The Mass of the Milky Way

C. Aganze, J. Aguilar, S. Ahlen, C. Allende Prieto, E. Armengaud +94

This paper is a DESI Collaboration Key Project publication. 25+12 pages, 14+2 figures, 5+1 tables. Submitted to ApJ

PDFThu 5 Nov
We present measurements of the mass of the Milky Way using distant halo stars selected from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Data Release 2 (DR2). We apply four dynamical modeling methods to 10,036 RR Lyrae (RRL) and blue horizontal branch (BHB) stars with 6D phase space coordinates that span from $\sim$10 to 95 kpc from the Galactic center, the largest samples of their kind. All methods are applied to mock surveys based on cosmological simulations that include generating individual tracer stars, convolving their phase-space coordinates with DESI and Gaia DR3-like uncertainties, and tailored models of the survey selection functions. By comparing the performance on these mocks, we quantify how systematic biases from both methodology assumptions and galaxy disequilibrium impact each estimate. The mock-based systematics, which differ by method and tracer, are used to correct the individual estimates and to derive joint estimates using a rigorous formalism that accounts for per-method biases and residual correlations between methods. We obtain a joint estimate of the mass within 80 kpc to be $M(r < 80 \mathrm{\,kpc}) = 6.36^{+0.53}_{-0.48} \times 10^{11}$ M$_\odot$ and a virial mass estimate of $M_\mathrm{200c} = 1.19^{+0.14}_{-0.12} \times 10^{12}$ M$_\odot$, with similar results when combining only RRL-based or BHB-based measurements. These results provide a roadmap for future DESI efforts, as well as those in other spectroscopic surveys, to reliably constrain the mass of our Galaxy.

She Sees Shells by the Stellar Shore: A Kinematic View of NGC 253's Stellar Halo

Katya Gozman, Eric F. Bell, Richard D'Souza, Mario Mateo, John I. Bailey III +2

33 pages, 12 figures, accepted to ApJ

PDFThu 5 Nov
The stellar halos of nearby galaxies are fossil records of ancient mergers, providing insight into the formation and growth of these systems. These halos often contain substructure in the form of streams, shells, and debris fields that make up dynamically young remnants of past accretions. These remnants can be probed with resolved-star spectroscopic observations, giving insight into the kinematics of bygone satellite companions. Such observations have been made for the Milky Way (MW) and Andromeda, but are much more challenging and expensive to do outside the Local Group. The Local Volume has an abundance of MW-mass galaxies with richly structured halos, such as NGC 253. Leveraging the power of Magellan's M2FS fiber-fed multiobject spectrograph, we have obtained medium-resolution spectra of 225 stars centered on NGC 253's southwestern shell. These spectra enabled us to make the first-ever measurement of the resolved-star stellar kinematics for 89 stars in NGC 253's halo. We also detect substructure in several ways and identify regions likely to be accreted and in-situ, finding among them a low-velocity region coincident with the previously reported SW shell we believe was accreted recently, and an in-situ component that traces the outer disk. After subtracting NGC 253's systemic velocity, we measure median line-of-sight velocities of $-34_{-12}^{+16}$ km/s and $167_{-14}^{+16}$ km/s for the accreted and in-situ populations, respectively. This work highlights the utility of kinematics to differentiate between accreted and in-situ stars, and acts as a pathfinder for future observations with next-generation 30 m telescopes and wide-field multiobject spectrographs.

The impact of M dwarf stellar convection on exoplanetary atmospheric signatures. A 3D radiation-hydrodynamics simulation analysis for high-resolution spectroscopy

A. Chiavassa, M. Brogi, T. Masseron, R. Van den Broeck, L. Bigot +1

Accepted for publication in Astronomy and Astrophysics

PDFThu 5 Nov
M dwarf stars represent prime targets for the detection and characterization of small, temperate exoplanets; however, their complex activity and convective surfaces pose significant challenges for high-resolution spectroscopic studies. This work aims to investigate the impact of stellar convection on high-resolution spectroscopic observations of M dwarf stars and develop an integrated approach combining 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations, spectroscopic modeling, and transit analysis to mitigate stellar noise. We employ 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations of M dwarf stellar convection to generate synthetic spectra. We compare the synthetic spectra to actual observations of GJ 3470. We model a high-resolution transit sequence of the Neptune-size GJ 3470 b, including centre-to-limb variations (CLVs) and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect (RME), and process the sequence via principal component analysis (PCA) to de-trend stellar effects. Comparison of synthetic spectra with actual observations shows that current H2O line lists remain inaccurate, with modest agreement in line strength and position for the chosen K-band interval. During the transit of GJ 3470 b, the amplitude of CLVs+RME is comparable to exoplanet signals. Current PCA can in principle de-trend such effects, but its behaviour is highly dependent on the copresence of telluric and planetary signals. While we are able to correctly detect the exoplanet with no evident bias from stellar residuals, this result is limited to a narrow spectral interval and a specific orbital configuration. This study demonstrates that 3D radiative hydrodynamical simulations are essential for accurately modeling convection-induced spectral variability in M dwarfs. Our integrated framework establishes a foundation to obtain unbiased measurements from future ELT observations of exoplanets orbiting M dwarfs.

The impact of Population III stars on the elemental enrichment of ultra-faint galaxies in the FIRE-3 zoom-in simulations

Pratik J. Gandhi, Earl P. Bellinger, Marla Geha, Andrew Wetzel, Coral Wheeler +5

22 pages, 13 figures. Submitted to ApJ

PDFThu 5 Nov
The observed stellar mass-metallicity relation for ultra-faint galaxies in the Local Group (LG) is in tension with many theoretical predictions, suggesting the need for modified or additional channels of elemental enrichment at low metallicities. We present a suite of FIRE-3 cosmological baryonic zoom-in simulations of isolated ultra-faint galaxies run to z=0 (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 30 Msun, 0.1 pc) and LMC-mass environments (maximum baryonic resolution ~ 110 Msun, 0.5 pc), with different sub-grid models for Population III stars. Under our Pop. III model(s), star particles forming below a critical metallicity Z_crit represent an initial mass function (IMF) consisting of only massive stars (8-100 Msun). We examine how various stellar feedback processes impact the star formation histories (SFHs) and elemental abundances of ultra-faint galaxies. We find that our Pop. III model variants introduce an effective metallicity floor in ultra-faints at z=0, at Z_floor ~ 1e-5 Zsun to 1e-3 Zsun or [Fe/H]_floor ~ -7 to -5 depending on the model. However, simply including the Pop. III model does not systematically guarantee that all (or even most) ultra-faints enrich up to [Fe/H] ~ -2.5 to fully resolve the tension with the observed mass-metallicity relation, even though we obtain some galaxies at that level. Crucially, the ultra-faints that do enrich in [Fe/H] to this level observed in the LG have extended SFHs after Pop. III enrichment and significant iron enrichment from white dwarf (Type Ia) supernovae, indicating that iron enrichment from Pop. III alone is not sufficient. We also examine the ways in which early-time Pop. III feedback impacts the metallicity distributions and [Mg/Fe] abundance ratios in ultra-faint galaxies, and make a prediction for the stellar mass - [Mg/H] metallicity relation from our simulations.

Mock CSST NUV-NIR imaging of TNG50 galaxies with resolved stellar populations

Jing Tang, Ran Li, Yang Chen, Chao Liu, Alessandro Bressan +3

14 pages, 13 figures, 5 tables, accepted for publication in A&A

PDFThu 5 Nov
Aims. We aim to develop a framework for producing CSST-like mock images of resolved stellar populations from simulated galaxies, demonstrated here using two TNG50 galaxies as illustrative test cases. Methods. Starting from simulated galaxies from TNG50, we use the population synthesis code TRILEGAL to sample individual stars from star particles, retaining bright stars explicitly and treating the remaining unresolved light, including faint stellar light and nebular emission from star-forming regions, as a diffuse background component of the galaxy. Based on the dust distribution inferred from the gas mass and metallicity of gas cells, we use the radiative transfer (RT) code SKIRT to construct the dust model. From this model, we compute the line-of-sight extinction for individual stars, while image-level transmission maps for the diffuse light are derived through RT simulations. For young stars still embedded in their natal molecular clouds, we additionally include birth-cloud attenuation. The resulting dust-attenuated stellar catalogues and diffuse-light images are then processed with the CSST simulation pipeline, which applies PSF convolution, sky background, and instrumental noise. Results. Our framework produces mock CSST images for both a spiral galaxy and a dwarf irregular galaxy from TNG50 in the seven photometric bands. The mock images combine resolved stars, unresolved diffuse light, and dust attenuation within a unified framework, and include the main observational effects expected for the survey. The images are provided with known astrometric and photometric calibration information, and are intended as realistic image-level inputs for subsequent source detection, photometric recovery, and completeness analyses of resolved stellar populations. The framework is designed to be adaptable to other hydrodynamic simulations and observing configurations.

Investigating the initial conditions of globular clusters via $N$-body simulations: I. half-mass radius and stellar density

Ruoyun Huang, Baitian Tang, Long Wang, Holger Baumgardt, Xia Li +3

PDFThu 5 Nov
Born out of the turbulent, dense gas of the early Universe, globular clusters (GCs) represent a significantly different star-formation mode compared to the local Universe. To quantify the initial cluster conditions, we perform the long-term dynamical evolution of GCs using the high-precision direct N-body simulation code PeTar. As the first step in a series of studies, we investigate the two low-mass outer-halo GCs, Eridanus and Whiting 1. By comparing simulated number density profiles with observations, we derive best-fit initial half-mass radii of $2.0 \pm 1.0$ pc for Eridanus and $2.5 \pm 0.5$ pc for Whiting 1, corresponding to initial surface densities of $2592.03 M_\odot pc^{-2}$ and $226.35 M_\odot pc^{-2}$, respectively – 2-3 orders of magnitude higher than their present-day values. The absence of multiple populations (MPs) in Whiting 1 and young massive clusters (YMCs), in contrast to the MP-hosting Eridanus, supports the scenario that MP formation requires higher initial surface density and/or lower metallicity. Using the simulations as a template, we identify five high-probability extra-tidal candidates of Whiting 1 from Gaia DR3 and APOGEE DR17, which exhibit coherent kinematics and chemical abundances. Our results demonstrate that the diffuse states of low-mass GCs are the product of long-term dynamical expansion and tidal stripping, and that their primordial configurations were significantly denser.

The dwarf galaxy NGC 185: the flagship of Andromeda's dwarf galaxies for the study of evolved multiple stellar populations

Paolo Ventura, Chiara Ventura, Richard D'Souza, Flavia Dell'Agli, Matteo Correnti +4

12 pages, 12 figures, accepted for publication on Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFThu 5 Nov
Reaching galaxies at 10-15 Mpc with HST and JWST requires star formation history (SFH) diagnostics that do not rely on the faint main sequence turn-off. We investigate whether horizontal branch (HB), red clump (RC), and red and asymptotic giant branch (RGB, AGB) stars can fill this role for dwarf elliptical galaxies, using NGC 185 as a test case. We use stellar evolution models of low- and intermediate-mass stars as inputs to population synthesis simulations that reproduce the colour distribution and luminosity function of stars in the (mF606W-mF814W, mF814W) diagram. The study of the stars populating the HB is used to derive the distance modulus and reddening, the average mass loss suffered by the stars during the ascending on the RGB and the SFH occurred during the ancient epochs, before 7 Gyr ago. The luminosity function of the RC gives us constraints on the recent SFH (<8 Gyr). The distribution of HB stars yields a reddening E(B-V)=0.20 mag and a distance modulus DM=24.10 mag for NGC 185. The average mass lost by low-mass stars during the RGB phase is 0.22 +- 0.01 Msun. Star formation was concentrated at early epochs, with half of the stars formed more than 11 Gyr ago. The morphology of the HB indicates essentially no metallicity spread among stars older than 8 Gyr, whereas the slope of the RGB suggests that part of the stars younger than 5-6 Gyr are slightly enriched in metals. Once RGB mass loss is adequately constrained, evolved stars can provide a reliable SFH for dwarf elliptical galaxies well beyond the reach of MSTO-based methods. We discuss the implications for more distant systems and the remaining uncertainties in the underlying stellar models.

ReveaLLAGN 2: Exploring the Nuclear Gas and Stellar Kinematics of Nearby LLAGNs with JWST/NIRSpec IFU

Antoine Dumont, Nadine Neumayer, Anil C. Seth, Torsten Böker, Jonelle L. Walsh +5

Accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics

PDFThu 5 Nov
Low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (LLAGN) represent the most common mode of black hole accretion in the local Universe, yet their nuclear structure, stellar and gas kinematics remain poorly constrained at parsec scales. We investigate the nuclear regions of eight nearby representative LLAGN, the ReveaLLAGN sample: M87, M104, M94, M81, NGC1052, NGC4258, NGC5128 and NGC4395. Using JWST/NIRSpec data, we probe the stellar, molecular hydrogen, hydrogen recombination, and high ionisation coronal lines kinematics and their spatial distribution, exploring how detectability and spatial distributions of these lines vary across different Eddington ratios. The stellar LOSV maps reveal a slowly rotating disk in the nuclei of all our targets, characterised by an anti-correlation with the h3 Gauss-Hermite moment. Across the sample, the stellar velocity dispersion rises toward the central black hole. Only in M104 and M94 does the velocity dispersion display a more intricate pattern that departs from a simple disk configuration, indicating the presence of multiple kinematic components in these galaxies. Across the sample, the gas distribution and kinematics exhibit rotation that is frequently decoupled from the stellar rotation, suggesting that the stellar gravitational potential is decoupled from the current accretion state. The gas kinematics are often visually aligned with the radio jet, presenting broader velocity dispersions along the jet direction. Line-ratio maps indicate that the dominant excitation mechanism is photoionisation by the central AGN, with mechanical heating from shocks becoming important only at higher Eddington ratios. Across the sample, we detect coronal lines in all LLAGN when using nuclear-aperture spectra, and we can obtain spatially resolved kinematics only in NGC5128 and NGC4395, the highest Eddington ratio sources.

The formation and evolution of Wolf-Rayet stars with temperature-dependent winds

Guy O. Flint, Avishai Gilkis, Tomer Shenar, Christopher A. Tout

11 pages, 9 figures, 2 tables. Submitted to the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS)

PDFThu 5 Nov
Massive stars strongly influence their environments through their radiation, stellar winds and core-collapse supernovae, while their own evolution is strongly shaped by wind mass loss. Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars are characterised by strong, optically thick winds, and the implementation of these winds remains an important uncertainty in stellar evolution models. Recent hydrodynamically consistent atmosphere calculations predict WR mass-loss rates that depend strongly on the temperature at the wind-launching region. We investigate the effects of incorporating these latest theoretical WR wind prescriptions into single and binary stellar evolution models at Milky Way, Large Magellanic Cloud and Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) metallicities. We find that the temperature dependence of the WR wind can significantly alter the evolution of stars near the threshold for WR formation. In particular, an implementation that transitions from a hydrogen rich WNL wind prescription to a classical WR wind driven by the hot iron opacity bump causes some SMC-metallicity models to spend an extended period at temperatures of approximately 70 to 90 kK. This produces a concentration of models in the temperature range occupied by most observed SMC WR stars, a feature that has previously been difficult to explain with stellar evolution models. Our results demonstrate that incorporating physically motivated, temperature dependent WR winds can improve the agreement between stellar evolution models and observed WR populations.

The total mass distribution in galaxies strongly influences star formation (SF), both directly via gas availability and feedback, and indirectly via the dark matter (DM) potential well. Searching for Signatures of i-process Nucleosynthesis in Metal-Rich, Lead-rich Barium Stars

Catherine Manea, Madeleine McKenzie, Gail Zasowski, Giulia C. Cinquegrana, Amaya Sinha +4

18 pages, 7 figures, 7 tables. Submitted to MNRAS. Comments welcome

PDFThu 5 Nov
The intermediate neutron-capture process ($i$-process) has been invoked to explain the peculiar compositions of carbon-enhanced metal-poor (CEMP) stars enriched in both slow- ($s$) and rapid- ($r$) neutron-capture elements. Recent literature has questioned its operation at higher metallicities, but observational evidence remains limited. We analyse Gemini/GHOST and McDonald/Tull spectra of six $\rm -0.7 < [Fe/H] < -0.1$ stars identified in GALAH by their extreme Ba and Eu (or Sm) compositions ($\gtrsim$10$\times$ solar). We also reanalyse archival spectra of two literature $i$-process candidates. Our findings reinforce that metal-rich AGB stars contribute to the production of elements classically attributed to the $r$-process. These results identify promising candidates for $i$-process enrichment near solar metallicity and motivate additional $i$-process models to understand the contribution of this nucleosynthetic process to the chemical evolution of the Milky Way disk.

Living near filaments: How environment shapes age profiles in low-mass galaxies

Elisa A. Tau, Diego Pallero, Rory Smith, Raúl Baier-Soto, Antonela Monachesi +1

6 pages including appendices. Submitted to A&A

PDFThu 5 Nov
U-shaped radial age profiles have recently been reported in low-mass galaxies. This type of profile differs from the usually observed positive radial age gradients of the low-mass regime. We aim to characterise the environment in which low-mass galaxies with U-shaped age profiles are more commonly found. We use the IllustrisTNG-50 hydrodynamical simulations to select a sample of galaxies with stellar masses ranging from $10^9 \, M_\odot$ to $10^{10} \, M_\odot$. Based on their radial age profiles, we classify the sample into galaxies with and without a U-shaped feature. We define a U-shaped profile as one where stellar ages decrease towards a local minimum and subsequently increase, requiring an age contrast of at least 0.4 Gyr on either side of the minimum relative to its value.
